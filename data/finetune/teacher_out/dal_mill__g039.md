## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents a farmer-owned dal milling enterprise for producing split pulses from chickpea. The unit will be promoted by Gondal Tur Dal Producer Company Ltd, based in Gondal, Rajkot, Gujarat. The undertaking leverages a strong pulse-growing hinterland, direct farm linkages, and standardized processes for de-husking, splitting, and polishing to deliver quality dal for diverse consumer and institutional markets. Pulses are central to the Indian dietary basket, providing plant-based protein along with fibre, iron, and essential vitamins. By organizing primary processing at source, the FPO enhances value realization for members while supplying consistent-quality dal to the market.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a staple in Indian cuisines, used daily in households, community kitchens, and food service. It is versatile across preparations, pairs well with cereals, and contributes to balanced nutrition. Benefits to consumers include reliable quality, safe processing, and convenient pack formats. Benefits to producers include improved price discovery, reduced post-harvest losses, and participation in value addition. The FPO model further promotes traceability, fair trade practices, and localized employment.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal is stable and resilient, supported by population growth, urbanization, and the predominance of vegetarian diets in many regions. Gujarat, and the broader western belt, has established market channels for chickpea-based dal through mandis, wholesale markets, retail chains, e-commerce platforms, and institutional buyers. The FPO’s location within a pulse-growing cluster reduces logistics costs, ensures steady raw material inflow, and supports competitive pricing. Policy thrusts on micro food enterprises and farmer collectivization, along with consumer preference for hygienically processed and branded dal, further strengthen the opportunity.

### Value Chain
The value chain begins with aggregation of whole chickpea from member farmers and allied growers. After inward quality checks, lots are cleaned and stored in aerated, pest-safe conditions. Conditioning involves soaking to loosen husk, followed by drying to achieve optimal moisture balance. Cleaned material passes through reel and aspiration systems to remove grit and fines, then through an emery roll dehusker. A subsequent reel operation removes residual husk, and the kernels are split in a lentil splitting machine. Polishing enhances appearance and consumer acceptability. Final steps include grading, weighing, packing in consumer and bulk formats, palletized storage, and dispatch through wholesale, retail, and institutional channels. By-products such as husk and brokens are valorized for feed or other uses, improving overall economics.

### Competition & Marketing
The enterprise will operate in a competitive landscape with local mills and established national brands. The FPO’s differentiators include farm-level traceability, consistent quality, local freshness, and a community-first proposition. Marketing will focus on:
- A clear brand identity rooted in origin, farmer ownership, and quality assurance.
- Strong distributor and retailer relationships across nearby districts and urban consumption centres.
- Institutional sales to canteens, caterers, and community kitchens that value reliability and price stability.
- Digital channels for reach, with an emphasis on authenticity and hygienic processing.
- Transparent quality practices to build trust, including lot-wise traceability and standardized packaging.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: Mitigated through diversified sourcing among members and allied farmers, seasonal procurement planning, and storage protocols that preserve quality.
- Quality variability: Addressed via grading at procurement, moisture management, and in-process controls with documented SOPs.
- Machinery downtime: Handled through preventive maintenance, critical spares inventory, and service contracts with OEMs.
- Power and utility interruptions: Managed by scheduling flexibility, backup arrangements, and energy-efficient machinery.
- Market price swings: Balanced with dynamic pricing, focus on by-product recovery, and channel diversification.
- Compliance and food safety: Ensured through good manufacturing and hygiene practices, staff training, and periodic audits.
- Working capital strains: Managed with disciplined inventory rotation, prudent credit policies, and banking lines aligned to seasonal needs.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit will create local employment for machine operators, quality and warehouse staff, packers, and supervisors, with dedicated roles for women and youth. It enhances farmer incomes by shortening the chain, reducing wastage, and sharing margins through the FPO. Capacity building on post-harvest handling and quality standards strengthens the farm base. The enterprise multiplies benefits within the community by anchoring allied services such as transport, maintenance, and packaging.

### Sustainability
Sustainability is woven into operations through efficient energy use, potential adoption of solar power, and water stewardship via controlled soaking and reuse wherever feasible. Husk and brokens are channelled to feed or fuel applications, minimizing waste. Recyclable packaging and palletized handling reduce material losses. Local sourcing and proximity to markets lower transport emissions. Occupational health, safety, and hygiene measures protect the workforce and ensure compliant, responsible processing.
