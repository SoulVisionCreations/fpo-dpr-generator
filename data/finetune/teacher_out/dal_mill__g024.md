## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is the staple protein of Indian households and an anchor of regional cuisines. It is produced by milling pulses to remove the husk and split the cotyledons, followed by grading, polishing, and packing. Gram-based dal (chana dal) is prized for its taste, texture, and culinary versatility, while delivering fibre, plant protein, iron, and B‑vitamins that are integral to vegetarian nutrition.

Nilanga Dalhan Producer Company Ltd, based in Nilanga, Latur, Maharashtra, is well placed in a major pulse-growing belt. Locating the dal mill within this catchment enables direct farm linkages, fresher raw material, lower logistics costs, and transparent value sharing with member farmers. The project aligns with the PM‑FME vision to strengthen FPO-led processing, branding, and market access.

### Uses & Benefits
- Daily cooking in households for curries, stews, fritters, snacks, and flours; wide acceptance across regions and cuisines.
- Nutritional benefits include high plant protein and dietary fibre, contributing to satiety and balanced diets.
- Milling enhances convenience, cooking consistency, and shelf stability when handled with good storage practices.
- For the FPO and its members, dal milling converts a commodity into a branded, traceable product, improving price realization, reducing post-harvest losses, and stabilizing farm incomes.
- By‑products such as husk and brokens can be channelled to feed and allied uses, adding incremental revenue and reducing waste.

### Market Potential
The Indian dal market displays resilient, year‑round demand driven by dietary habits, health orientation, and expanding retail and food service footprints. Consumers increasingly seek clean, uniform, low‑defect, and attractively packed dal, creating space for quality‑assured, FPO‑branded products. Urban retail, institutional purchasers, and direct‑to‑consumer platforms offer multiple routes to market. With gram a core pulse in Maharashtra and neighbouring states, a steady base market exists for both bulk and consumer packs. Premiums accrue to lots with consistent size, color, cooking performance, and lower admixture.

### Value Chain
- Farm‑gate procurement from member farmers supported by aggregation, pre‑harvest advisory, and transparent grading.
- Scientific storage with stack management and integrated pest management for safe holding.
- Conditioning through soaking and gentle drying to loosen husk while preserving kernel integrity.
- Cleaning, de‑husking, splitting, and polishing through an integrated line with aspiration and grading to uniform sizes.
- By‑product segregation for feed and allied markets, ensuring cleaner main output and value capture.
- Packing into consumer packs and bulk sacks, supported by barcoding and traceability.
- Logistics and distribution to wholesale mandis, traditional retail, modern trade, institutions, and direct channels.
- Scope for allied value addition such as gram flour at a later stage, creating a diversified product basket and better capacity utilization.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition stems from established regional mills, trader‑packers, and private labels. The FPO can differentiate on farm‑traceable origin, uniform quality, food safety systems, and community impact. A focused brand narrative around Nilanga and Latur provenance, farmer ownership, and honest quality will resonate with trade and consumers.

Marketing strategy will combine:
- Channel partnerships with wholesalers, kirana stores, and modern retail.
- Institutional sales to HORECA, caterers, hostels, and public procurement where eligible.
- Direct‑to‑consumer through local delivery partners and e‑commerce.
- Consistent packaging, attractive design, and easy‑to‑understand cooking guidance.
- Certifications and compliance such as FSSAI license, AGMARK grading where applicable, and adoption of food safety management systems.
- Trade promotions, sampling, and festivals‑led visibility to build recall.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price volatility: mitigate through staggered procurement, member supply agreements, and prudent inventory buffers.
- Quality variability: deploy strict incoming quality checks for moisture, purity, and size; maintain standard operating procedures during conditioning and milling.
- Operational disruptions: preventive maintenance, trained operators, and contingency for power and spares.
- Working capital tightness: structured bank limits, warehouse receipt finance, and disciplined receivables management.
- Compliance and food safety: robust SOPs, periodic audits, pest management, and documentation for regulatory adherence.
- Market competition: brand building, customer service, and a balanced channel mix to limit dependence on any single segment.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit will generate skilled and semi‑skilled employment across operations, quality, packing, logistics, and administration, with strong opportunities for women in sorting, packaging, and lab roles. Farmer members gain from assured offtake, transparent grading, and a share of value addition. Local procurement and processing retain value within the community, spurring allied services such as transport, maintenance, and packaging supply. Training and extension will uplift post‑harvest practices and encourage youth engagement in agripreneurship.

### Sustainability
Resource‑efficient operations will emphasize responsible water use in soaking with options for reuse and safe discharge, energy‑efficient motors and airflow systems, and dust control and worker safety. Husk and other by‑products will be valorized to minimize waste. Preference for recyclable packaging and engagement with extended producer responsibility norms will reduce environmental footprint. Local sourcing shortens supply chains, lowering transport emissions, while traceability and food safety systems promote long‑term consumer trust.
