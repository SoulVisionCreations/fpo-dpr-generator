## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Ghee, or clarified butter, is a celebrated traditional dairy fat in India with deep cultural, culinary, and nutritional significance. It is prepared by converting milk into cream and butter, then gently clarifying to yield a pure, aromatic fat with a naturally nutty flavour. It is naturally free from lactose and gluten, and enjoys universal acceptance across households, confectioners, and food service.

This Detailed Project Report is prepared for Malnad Milk & Paddy Farmers Producer Co. Ltd, Yelwal, Mysuru, Karnataka. The FPO will aggregate milk from member producers and local suppliers, process it under hygienic and compliant conditions, and market premium-quality ghee under a farmer-owned brand. The core process involves pasteurization, cooling, homogenization, cream separation, chilling, butter churning, ghee separation, and packaging.

### Uses & Benefits
- Culinary versatility across frying, sautéing, baking, confectionery, and traditional sweets, thanks to its stability and characteristic aroma.
- Widely used in home cooking, restaurants, sweet shops, temples, and for auspicious occasions.
- Naturally free from lactose and gluten, making it suitable for consumers with certain dietary restrictions.
- Long ambient shelf life when packed with adequate oxygen and light barriers, supporting efficient logistics and retailing.
- Perceived health and wellness value in traditional systems of nutrition and holistic living.

### Market Potential
India is a large and growing market for dairy fats, with ghee enjoying year-round, pan-regional demand and seasonal surges around festivals and wedding periods. Consumers increasingly prefer hygienically produced, traceable, and branded ghee with consistent taste, colour, and aroma. Urban retail, e-commerce, and quick-commerce channels are expanding access, while institutional buyers such as sweet makers, bakeries, and hotels continue as anchor customers. Export prospects exist in diaspora markets and specialty ethnic retail, especially when supported by strong quality credentials, origin stories, and compliant labelling.

### Value Chain
- Milk procurement: The FPO aggregates fresh milk from members and vetted local suppliers through village-level collection points with basic quality checks for adulteration, acidity, and freshness.
- Primary processing: Pasteurization and cooling enable hygienic handling. Homogenization supports uniform fat distribution prior to cream separation.
- Cream and butter: Cream separation produces cream and skim milk. Chilled cream is churned into butter, while the buttermilk and skim milk can be valorized through sales or integration in cattle feed and allied dairy products.
- Ghee clarification: Butter is slowly heated to drive off moisture and separate milk solids, yielding clear golden ghee with a pleasant aroma. Controlled temperature and residence time safeguard flavour and oxidative stability.
- Packaging and storage: Ghee is filled into food-grade jars, tins, or pouches, sealed to protect from light, oxygen, and moisture, and stored at ambient conditions. Labelling adheres to applicable standards and declarations.
- Distribution: Multi-channel sales encompass general trade, modern retail, institutional sales, e-commerce, and FPO-direct subscription models. Local origin and farmer ownership support trust and brand equity.

### Competition & Marketing
The category features large national brands, regional dairy cooperatives, and local producers. A farmer-owned brand can differentiate on purity, provenance, freshness, and fair value to producers. Key strategies include:
- Assurance of authenticity through robust quality systems, batch-level traceability, and recognizable compliance marks.
- Distinctive branding rooted in Mysuru and Malnad identity, highlighting traditional process and community ownership.
- Multi-pack options for households and institutional buyers, gift packs during festive seasons, and curated bundles for sweet shops and bakeries.
- In-store visibility, chef and influencer associations, sampling at community events, and partnerships with temples and cultural institutions.
- Direct-to-consumer channels with subscription refills and transparent stories from farm to pack.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw milk volatility: Secure long-term procurement relationships with member suppliers, fair and transparent pricing, and seasonal smoothing through inventory planning and product mix.
- Quality and food safety: Deploy strict SOPs, HACCP-based controls, rapid test kits, periodic third-party validation, and strong sanitation and pest control.
- Adulteration and contamination: Enforce stringent collection norms, supplier grading, penalties for non-compliance, and closed-loop traceability.
- Process and utility disruptions: Maintain preventive maintenance, critical spares, backup power, and trained shift operators.
- Market competition and pricing: Focus on differentiated positioning, channel mix optimization, and promotional intensity during key seasons.
- Shelf-life and rancidity: Use high-barrier packaging, nitrogen flushing where relevant, and careful control of temperature, moisture, and light exposure across the chain.
- Working capital strain: Align credit terms with channel partners, maintain inventory discipline, and use bill discounting or cash-credit facilities.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit creates stable jobs in procurement, processing, quality control, warehousing, sales, and administration, with a strong preference for local youth and women. It improves members’ income through assured offtake, prompt payments, and patronage-linked benefits. Capacity building in clean milk production, fodder management, and animal health enhances productivity. The brand elevates the region’s identity, stimulates ancillary services in logistics and packaging, and strengthens cooperative spirit and governance within the FPO.

### Sustainability
- Energy efficiency through optimized steam systems, heat recovery, and progressively cleaner fuels; rooftop solar where feasible.
- Water stewardship with reuse of non-contact water, structured cleaning-in-place cycles, and compliant effluent treatment.
- By-product utilization of skim milk and buttermilk, reducing waste and improving overall dairy economics.
- Responsible packaging with recyclable materials, light and oxygen barriers, and extended producer responsibility alignment.
- Local sourcing to cut transport emissions and strengthen rural ecosystems; tree-based fodder promotion and veterinary outreach for herd health.
