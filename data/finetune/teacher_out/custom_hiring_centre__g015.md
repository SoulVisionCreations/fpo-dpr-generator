## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Kheda Krishi Sewa Producer Company Ltd, Matar, proposes to establish a Custom Hiring Centre to bring reliable, timely, and affordable mechanisation services to member and neighbouring farmers. The centre will own and operate a fleet of tractors, implements, and seasonal equipment, deployed on a rental basis to support key operations such as land preparation, sowing, inter-cultivation, spraying, harvesting, threshing, and residue management. The service removes the barrier of high upfront ownership costs and ensures access to well-maintained machinery and trained operators within a short response radius.

The operating cycle begins with farmer booking and scheduling, followed by dispatch of machines and operators, on-field execution, return with cleaning and routine maintenance, and transparent usage metering for billing. The model leverages the FPO’s governance to ensure fair tariffs, priority to members, and reinvestment of surpluses into fleet expansion and service quality.

### Uses & Benefits
- Timely field operations that improve crop establishment and harvest efficiency.
- Lower per-unit cost of mechanisation for small and marginal holdings through shared access.
- Reduced drudgery and enhanced labour productivity, especially during peak seasons.
- Improved quality of operations such as uniform seed placement, precise spraying, and better threshing outcomes.
- Access to trained operators and assured machine uptime through preventive maintenance.
- Transparent tariffs and standard operating procedures anchored by the FPO’s member-first approach.

### Market Potential
The service directly addresses chronic gaps in last-mile mechanisation in peri-urban and rural clusters around Matar in Kheda district. The local mix of cereals, pulses, oilseeds, fodder, and horticulture presents demand across seasons for primary tillage, seedbed preparation, sowing, plant protection, and harvesting. Rising rural wages and shrinking labour availability during peaks further support the shift from manual to mechanised operations. Government thrust on mechanisation and FPO-led service models, coupled with growing awareness of quality-of-work advantages, underpin a strong and sustained demand trajectory.

### Value Chain
- Upstream linkages: OEMs and dealers for tractors and implements, fuel suppliers, lubricants and spares vendors, and mechanics. Preventive maintenance schedules and service contracts help keep downtime low.
- Core service: Booking, dispatch, field execution, metering, and billing delivered under standard service agreements. Member farmers receive priority access and transparent pricing.
- Downstream outcomes: Improved timeliness, enhanced yields and quality, reduced post-harvest losses, and stronger market readiness. Data from job cards and meters feed into seasonal planning and fleet optimisation.
- Enablers: Digital booking, operator training, on-field safety protocols, and partnerships for demonstrations and agronomic advisory.

### Competition & Marketing
The primary competition is from informal tractor contractors, small private hire centres, and dealer-run fleets. The FPO’s differentiators include transparent tariffs, reliable service windows, assured quality through operator training, and value-added offerings like package-of-practice alignment, field demonstrations, and bundled input advisory. Marketing will rely on village-level mobilisers, member referrals, social messaging groups, seasonal campaigns, and on-field demos aligned to sowing and harvest calendars. A simple loyalty framework and advance-booking incentives will help smoothen demand and enhance utilisation.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Equipment downtime: Addressed through preventive maintenance, spares stocking, and service tie-ups with OEMs; telematics and meters support proactive care.
- Fuel price volatility: Managed via periodic tariff calibration and efficiency measures such as correct implement matching and operator coaching.
- Seasonality and weather: Balanced through diversified service offerings, advance booking, and dynamic scheduling to compress peak operations.
- Operator capacity and retention: Mitigated through structured training, performance-linked incentives, safety gear, and clear career pathways.
- Credit and collections: Reduced through partial advance payments, member guarantees, and digital collection options.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The CHC creates dignified local employment for operators, mechanics, helpers, and a booking coordinator. It reduces drudgery, particularly benefiting women and elderly farmers, and enables timely operations that support higher net returns. Savings from shared services free cash for better inputs and risk management. The FPO structure ensures that value accrues to members, building community assets, resilience, and stronger bargaining power in input and output markets.

### Sustainability
Mechanisation, when planned well, lowers total field passes, conserves fuel, and reduces soil disturbance through appropriate implement choices. Precision spraying cuts chemical overuse. Residue management services discourage burning. The centre will promote safe waste-oil disposal, water-efficient cleaning, and explore renewable energy options for yard power. Fleet usage data will guide continuous improvement in fuel economy and carbon footprint over time.
