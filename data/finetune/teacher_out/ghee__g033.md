## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Belwandi Dairy Farmers Producer Company Ltd, based in Belwandi, Shrigonda, Ahmednagar, Maharashtra, proposes a modern, hygienic ghee making unit grounded in farmer ownership and local value addition. Ghee, or clarified butter, is a celebrated traditional dairy product with a rich, nutty flavour, naturally free from lactose and gluten. The unit will leverage assured local milk availability, robust quality systems, and efficient processing to deliver consistent, authentic ghee to retail, institutional, and bulk buyers.

### Uses & Benefits
Ghee is prized for culinary excellence in everyday cooking, premium sweets, bakery, and food service applications. Its high smoke point supports versatile cooking methods, while its flavour elevates traditional cuisines and contemporary recipes alike. It is widely preferred for religious offerings and festive consumption, and is appreciated by consumers seeking lactose-free and gluten-free options. Ghee is also valued for shelf stability, portability, and convenience in diverse pack formats.

### Market Potential
Demand for pure, traceable ghee continues to expand across urban, semi-urban, and rural markets, supported by rising incomes, evolving food habits, and sustained preference for traditional dairy fats. Maharashtra’s strong dairy ecosystem, coupled with Ahmednagar’s active milk belt, provides a natural catchment for raw material and a ready market through sweet makers, eateries, retailers, wholesalers, and emerging e-commerce channels. Premium positioning around purity, origin, and farmer ownership enables strong consumer trust and brand affinity, while institutional buyers seek assured quality and reliable supply.

### Value Chain
The integrated value chain begins with responsible milk procurement from member farmers through transparent quality checks and quick chilling. The processing sequence follows a controlled path through pasteurization, cooling, milk homogenizing, cream separation, chilling, butter churning, ghee separating, clarification, and packaging. The unit emphasizes sanitary design, stainless contact parts, food-grade consumables, and batch traceability. Co-products such as skim milk or buttermilk can be channelled to allied dairy lines, community nutrition, or sale to nearby users, improving overall value realization. Packaging spans consumer and institutional formats with tamper-evident, food-safe materials, backed by batch coding and compliance documentation.

### Competition & Marketing
The market features established national and regional brands as well as local dairies. The FPO’s competitive advantages include direct farm linkage, freshness, provenance storytelling, and community-rooted governance. The marketing plan integrates retail distribution, institutional sales to sweet shops and food service, partnerships with local traders, and digital commerce. Brand identity will focus on purity, taste, and farmer empowerment, supported by relatable packaging, point-of-sale education, social media narratives, and festival-led promotions. Strong emphasis on compliance, lab testing, and consistent sensory profile underpins repeat purchases and word-of-mouth growth.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw milk variability: Mitigated through standardized procurement protocols, chilling at source, and routine quality analytics.
- Supply disruption: Reduced via diversified member sourcing, seasonality planning, and route optimization.
- Food safety and compliance: Ensured through robust SOPs, staff training, sanitation and hygiene practices, and third-party lab support.
- Price volatility of inputs and packaging: Addressed through forward tie-ups, vendor development, and inventory buffers.
- Equipment downtime: Minimized via preventive maintenance, spares planning, and qualified technical support.
- Market competition: Countered through authenticity, local branding, superior quality assurance, and responsive customer service.
- Financial liquidity: Supported by structured working capital lines, disciplined receivables, and conservative cash management.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit creates direct employment in production, quality assurance, logistics, and sales, with inclusive opportunities for women and local youth. Member dairy farmers benefit from assured offtake, fair and transparent milk pricing, and dividends in line with cooperative principles. Capacity building in clean milk production, feed and animal health practices, and financial literacy strengthens rural livelihoods. Local transporters, packers, and service providers gain ancillary income, promoting balanced regional development.

### Sustainability
Sustainability measures include energy-efficient equipment, optimized steam and thermal insulation, and exploration of renewable energy options. Water stewardship covers reuse and recycling in non-contact applications, effluent treatment, and responsible discharge. By-product utilization, controlled waste segregation, and biodegradable or recyclable packaging help reduce environmental footprint. The overarching approach integrates food safety, animal welfare awareness, and community engagement to advance resilient, circular value chains.
