## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is a staple food ingredient produced by de-husking and splitting pulses, particularly chickpea, into clean, uniform halves. As a primary protein source in the Indian vegetarian diet, dal is valued for its balanced nutrition, including fibre, iron, and B‑vitamins. This project proposes a farmer‑owned dal mill focused on gram, anchored by Ri‑Bhoi Agro Pulses Producer Company Ltd. at Nongpoh, Umling, Ri‑Bhoi, Meghalaya. Locating the unit in a pulse‑growing belt enables efficient aggregation, lower logistics costs, and traceable sourcing directly from member farmers.

### Uses & Benefits
- Daily household cooking across diverse cuisines, in soups, curries, snacks, flours, and ready mixes.
- Strong nutrition profile with plant protein and micronutrients suitable for all age groups.
- Consistent cooking quality when graded and milled with care, aiding institutional kitchens and branded retail.
- Enhanced farmer realisations through aggregation, quality grading, and direct market linkage via the FPO.
- By‑products such as husk and bran create additional value streams and reduce waste.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal remains resilient due to its central role in Indian diets, steady household consumption, and wide culinary use. Urban retail, e‑commerce, and institutional buyers prefer reliable, graded product with consistent cooking time and minimal impurities. Rising health consciousness, regional cuisine trends, and convenience formats support premiumisation and branded packs. Northeastern markets exhibit growing demand alongside opportunities to supply nearby states, while origin‑linked sourcing and farmer traceability strengthen brand positioning.

### Value Chain
The FPO aggregates whole chickpea from member farmers and partner growers, implements primary grading and storage, and schedules milling based on market orders. The process flow includes procurement and safe storage, soaking for husk loosening, gentle drying, pre‑cleaning through reel machines to remove coarse impurities, de‑husking by emery roll, secondary cleaning to separate husk and fine grit, splitting into clean halves, optional polishing, grading, and packing for dispatch. Quality control is applied at each stage for moisture, purity, uniform size, broken ratio, polish level, and cooking characteristics. Packaging ranges from bulk sacks for wholesale to consumer packs for retail. By‑products are channelled to feed or fuel markets, reinforcing resource efficiency. Traceability, digital inventory, and hygienic handling underpin compliance with food safety regulations.

### Competition & Marketing
Dal markets feature established mills, regional brands, and unbranded bulk channels. The FPO can differentiate through farmer‑first sourcing, origin assurance, transparent quality grades, hygienic milling, and reliable supply. A dual‑channel strategy is proposed:
- Wholesale and institutional sales to distributors, canteens, hostels, processors, and government‑linked buyers.
- Branded consumer packs through retail outlets, modern trade, and e‑commerce, supported by clear labelling, AGMARK and FSSAI compliance, and simple cooking claims.
Pricing balances competitiveness with quality differentiation, while consistent procurement and inventory planning help stabilise supply. Periodic on‑ground activations, tasting demos, and digital storytelling around farmer provenance amplify brand trust.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material availability and price swings: mitigate via diversified sourcing, staggered procurement, simple forward agreements with members, and strategic buffer stocks.
- Quality variability: address through pre‑harvest advisories, moisture management, grading protocols, and batch‑wise testing.
- Storage losses: reduce via scientifically ventilated stores, pallets, fumigation as per norms, and regular inspections.
- Operational downtime: minimise with preventive maintenance, trained operators, critical spares, and service contracts.
- Power interruptions: plan for backup and energy‑efficient equipment to stabilise throughput.
- Market and regulatory shifts: diversify channels, maintain compliance, build flexible pack sizes, and monitor policy updates.
- Working capital strain: use disciplined credit controls, inventory turns, and tie‑ups with lenders for inventory and receivable finance.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project creates year‑round jobs in operations, maintenance, quality assurance, logistics, administration, and sales, with targeted roles for local youth and women. Farmer incomes improve through transparent grading, lower intermediation, and patronage dividends. Reduced post‑harvest losses, skills development, and ancillary opportunities in transport, packaging, and by‑product utilisation extend benefits across the community, reinforcing the FPO’s role in rural prosperity.

### Sustainability
Resource‑efficient operations are embedded through careful water use and reuse in soaking, options for solar‑assisted drying, energy‑efficient motors, dust capture, and safe waste handling. Husk and bran are valorised as biomass or feed inputs, and the project encourages recyclable or biodegradable packaging alternatives. Local procurement reduces transport emissions, while good storage practices and loss prevention enhance overall sustainability and food system resilience.
