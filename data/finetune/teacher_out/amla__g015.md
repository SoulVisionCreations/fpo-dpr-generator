## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Akhnoor Grape Growers FPC Ltd proposes to establish a grape juice processing unit in Akhnoor, Jammu, Jammu and Kashmir. The unit will transform fresh table grapes sourced from member farmers into clarified, pasteurised grape juice that retains natural aroma, colour, and flavour. Grape juice is a gently processed, non-alcoholic beverage appreciated for its refreshing taste and perceived wellness attributes. The project formalises a farm-to-bottle model, stabilising returns for growers while delivering a consistent, safe, and traceable product to consumers.

### Uses & Benefits
Grape juice is consumed as a stand-alone beverage, as a base for mocktails, in confectionery and bakery applications, and within foodservice and hospitality segments. It suits households seeking minimally processed fruit drinks, institutional buyers looking for standardised quality, and retailers expanding their health and wellness offerings. Benefits include:
- Natural fruit sugars and polyphenol-rich profile, aligning with wellness-oriented consumption.
- No carbonation and no caffeine, making it family-friendly and suitable for a wide demographic.
- Year-round availability when processed and packed under proper preservation and hygiene.
- Versatility across ambient and chilled service formats depending on packaging and storage.

### Raw Material
The primary raw material is fresh, ripe, food-grade grapes procured from member orchards in and around Akhnoor. Preferred attributes include clean, bloom-fresh bunches with desirable colour, uniform berries, balanced sweetness and acidity, and intact stems at harvest. Varietal selection influences flavour, colour intensity, and clarity after processing; seeded and seedless types can be handled with appropriate de-stemming and seed-removal steps. Ancillary inputs include potable process water, approved preservatives such as potassium metabisulphite, food-grade packaging materials, cleaning and sanitation agents, and labels and cartons.

### Market Potential
Demand for fruit-based beverages is expanding across retail, e-commerce, institutional catering, travel, and hospitality. Consumers increasingly favour origin-linked, additive-conscious products with transparent sourcing. Grape juice is well placed in this landscape due to its distinctive flavour, culinary familiarity, and perceived functional properties. The project can tap multiple market layers: neighbourhood general trade, modern retail chains, online marketplaces, and bulk buyers such as restaurants, cafeterias, and canteens. Origin branding from Jammu’s grape belt enables differentiation and storytelling, while the FPO model builds trust on traceability and fair-farmer pricing. Value growth is further supported by festival gifting, wellness hampers, and hotel welcome beverages.

### Value Chain
The value chain integrates member vineyards to a hygienic processing line and on to diverse channels:
- Farm-level: Good Agricultural Practices, maturity indexing, careful harvest in cool hours, crate-based handling, and rapid aggregation at collection points.
- Pre-processing: Reception, sorting and grading, de-stemming, washing and sanitisation to remove soil, residues, and field contaminants.
- Processing: Seed removal and segmentation as needed, dilution to balance flavour, pulping, clarification and filtration for clarity and stability, followed by controlled pasteurisation. Post-heat treatment cooling is undertaken, with dosing of permitted preservatives where applicable, and a final stabilisation step before hygienic filling.
- Packaging and storage: Bottling or other suitable formats, coding and labelling, secondary packing, and storage under recommended conditions. Inventory is rotated using disciplined stock management.
- Distribution: Primary movements to distributors and key accounts, secondary reach to retailers and institutions, and direct-to-consumer fulfilment through online and farm-gate channels.
- By-product utilisation: Skins and pomace can be channelled to feed, compost, or value-added extracts to improve waste-to-value recovery.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition comes from national fruit drink brands, regional juice processors, imported grape beverages, and fresh juice counters. Differentiation levers for the FPO include:
- Orchard-to-bottle traceability with farmer stories and QR-enabled provenance.
- Clean-label positioning, highlighting minimal processing and responsible preservation.
- Consistent taste profile through varietal curation and standardised processing.
- Packaging formats catering to on-the-go, family packs, and institutional usage.
- Strategic presence in general trade, modern trade, and e-commerce, complemented by sampling at community events and tie-ups with cafés and hotels.
- Seasonal campaigns that align with local harvest narratives and regional pride.
Trade promotions, influencer advocacy on wellness, and partnerships with nutrition-led retailers can steadily build salience and velocity.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material variability: Mitigated by staggered harvesting, multi-varietal sourcing, pre-harvest extension, and buffer procurement from nearby clusters.
- Quality and safety: Addressed through HACCP-based controls, sanitation SOPs, potable water quality management, and regular lab testing against applicable standards.
- Demand fluctuations: Balanced with a mix of retail and institutional sales, prudent inventory, and flexible packaging plans.
- Price volatility: Managed via forward procurement agreements with members and value addition of by-products.
- Operational downtimes: Reduced by preventive maintenance, backup utilities, and trained multi-skilled staff.
- Regulatory compliance: Ensured through licences, label conformity, and documentation aligned with prevailing food safety regulations.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project formalises market linkages for grape growers, stabilising farm incomes and reducing distress sales. It creates local employment across reception, processing, quality control, packing, warehousing, sales, and logistics, with dedicated roles suitable for women and youth. Skill development in food safety, equipment handling, and sales operations uplifts workforce capabilities. The FPO structure circulates value within the community, enabling investments in better agronomy, packhouse improvements, and post-harvest infrastructure.

### Sustainability
Sustainability is integrated through efficient water use with recirculation and sanitation controls, energy-conscious heat treatment and cooling, and exploration of renewable power sources. Organic residues are diverted towards compost, feed, or extractive applications, limiting waste to landfill. Preference for recyclable or reusable packaging, eco-friendly inks and adhesives, and returnable secondary logistics further shrink the footprint. Local sourcing minimises food miles, while good agricultural practices promote soil health and biodiversity.
