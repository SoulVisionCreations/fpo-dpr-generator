## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Barwala Dairy Farmers FPC Ltd proposes a dedicated ghee making unit at Barwala, Hisar, Haryana. Ghee, or clarified butter, is a revered traditional dairy product with deep culinary, cultural, and therapeutic significance. Crafted from milk through cream separation and butter churning before gentle clarification, ghee offers a rich nutty aroma, a clean golden body, and excellent keeping quality. The FPO model ensures reliable milk sourcing, transparent farmer engagement, and strong backward linkages for quality and consistency.

### Uses & Benefits
Ghee is prized in home kitchens, sweet manufacturing, bakeries, and the food service segment for its distinctive flavour and high smoke stability. It is valued in traditional wellness practices, is inherently lactose-free and gluten-free, and is used in festive offerings and rituals. The product enhances the taste of staples, supports premium confectionery formulations, and provides versatility across regional cuisines. Properly clarified, it is shelf-stable and travel-friendly, enabling wider distribution without a cold chain for finished packs.

### Market Potential
Demand for ghee spans rural and urban households, local halwais, mithai makers, restaurants, and institutional kitchens. Consumer preferences are steadily shifting from loose to branded, sealed packs with verifiable purity and traceability. Growing awareness of authentic dairy fats, the premiumisation of traditional foods, and e-commerce penetration reinforce category growth. The Hisar milk belt provides a strong raw milk base, while proximity to key consumption centres supports both retail and bulk channels. Seasonal peaks during festive periods and wedding seasons further strengthen the business case.

### Value Chain
The value chain commences with member-farmer milk aggregation, primary chilling, and stringent quality checks at village collection points. Milk moves to the processing unit for pasteurization, controlled cooling, homogenization, cream separation, cream chilling, butter churning, and careful clarification into ghee, followed by filtration and hygienic packaging. By-product streams such as buttermilk from the churning stage can be channelled into beverages, fermented products, or cattle feed to improve overall economics. Downstream, the FPO deploys a blend of wholesale distributors, retail outlets, modern trade, institutional buyers, and direct-to-consumer platforms. Integrated procurement planning, cold chain for raw milk, disciplined batch controls, and food safety certifications anchor product integrity.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition includes national dairy brands, strong regional cooperatives, and local unbranded sellers. Barwala Dairy Farmers FPC can differentiate through farmer-linked traceability, freshness, consistent granulation and aroma, and a clear purity promise. Branding should convey tradition, quality assurance, and fair returns to farmers. A multi-channel strategy is recommended: local retail penetration with visible shelf presence, institutional supply to mithai and bakery segments, and online storefronts for wider reach. In-store sampling, festival-focused campaigns, chef collaborations, and transparent storytelling about the FPO’s farm-to-pack journey will build trust and accelerate adoption.

### Risk & Mitigation
Key risks include variability in raw milk quality, price and supply fluctuations, microbial contamination, energy interruptions, packaging supply gaps, and intense price-based competition. Mitigation rests on diversified milk routes, strict quality testing at intake, robust standard operating procedures, preventive maintenance and equipment AMC, power backup, supplier framework agreements, and adequate insurance. Regular third-party audits, batch traceability, and a responsive grievance redressal system protect brand reputation. Proactive working capital planning and prudent credit controls safeguard liquidity.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit will generate skilled and semi-skilled jobs in milk collection, processing, quality assurance, packaging, warehousing, logistics, and sales. Women’s participation can be promoted in collection centre operations, packaging, and quality labs. Stable procurement and transparent pricing raise farm incomes, foster better animal husbandry, and encourage adoption of clean milk practices. Local entrepreneurship in distribution and retail benefits from a dependable, fast-moving product line, circulating value within the community.

### Sustainability
Sustainability is rooted in efficient energy use, heat recovery in the clarification stage, and responsible water management with cleaning-in-place systems. By-products can be valorised to reduce waste. Preference for recyclable packaging and eco-conscious logistics further lowers footprint. On-farm advisory for animal health, feed optimization, and manure management complements environmental goals. Over time, distributed solar, efficient boilers, and continuous improvement in resource intensity can deepen the unit’s green credentials.
