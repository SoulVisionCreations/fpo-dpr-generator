## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Udgir Moong Dal Farmer Producer Company Ltd, based in Udgir, Latur, Maharashtra, proposes a gram-based dal milling unit under the PM-FME framework. The enterprise will procure chickpea directly from member farmers and nearby producer clusters, and process it into cleaned, de-husked, split, and polished dal. Pulses are central to Indian diets and are valued for plant-based protein, dietary fibre, and micronutrients such as iron and B-vitamins. Locating the unit in a pulse-growing belt strengthens raw material assurance, reduces logistics costs, and enables tighter quality control and traceability from farm to packet.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a staple in households, community kitchens, hostels, canteens, restaurants, and food assistance programmes. It serves as a versatile ingredient for daily meals, instant mixes, snacks, and flour blends. Benefits to consumers include consistent cooking quality, better taste, and assured safety through hygienic processing. Benefits to farmers arise from direct procurement, aggregation efficiencies, timely payment, and value capture within the FPO ecosystem. The FPO adds further value through brand building, grading, packaging, and by-product utilisation.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal is broad-based and resilient, driven by dietary preferences, rising urbanisation, retail penetration, and institutional consumption. Consumers increasingly prefer unadulterated, minimally polished, traceable pulses with reliable cooking performance. Wholesale and retail channels in Maharashtra and adjoining markets remain strong, while e-commerce and modern trade offer incremental growth. With assured raw material from member producers and quality-focused processing, the FPO is positioned to supply consistent-grade dal to regional distributors, retailers, institutional kitchens, and direct-to-consumer platforms.

### Value Chain
- Input supply: Farm production of chickpea, aggregation by the FPO, and direct procurement from member and associate farmers with quality-based selection at collection centres.
- Primary handling: Reception, sampling, grading, moisture management, and safe storage to preserve quality.
- Processing: Soaking, drying, cleaning, de-husking, separation of husk, splitting, polishing as per market preference, and final grading.
- Packaging and dispatch: Packing into sacks or consumer packs, palletisation, and dispatch to wholesale, retail, and institutional buyers.
- By-products: Husk and brokens are channelled to feed, flour, and snack segments, improving overall recovery and revenue balance.
- Enablers: Digital traceability, on-site quality testing, food safety systems, and logistics coordination to ensure timely delivery.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition includes local unbranded mills offering price-driven products and branded players offering packaged pulses with strong distribution. The FPO’s advantage lies in farmer ownership, raw material traceability, and community trust. The marketing strategy focuses on:
- Quality differentiation: Strict raw material selection, hygienic processing, minimal adulteration, and batch-wise testing.
- Brand positioning: Regionally rooted brand that communicates farm-to-fork traceability, honesty of grain, and consistent cooking quality.
- Channel strategy: Balanced presence in wholesale mandis, kirana stores, modern trade, institutional kitchens, and digital marketplaces.
- Packaging and labelling: Clear origin stories, cooking guidance, and compliance with food safety and labelling norms.
- Outreach: Farmer-member advocacy, sampling in local markets, visibility at retail points, and partnerships with caterers and canteens.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: Mitigated through seasonal procurement plans, member tie-ups, staggered buying, and hedging through diversified sourcing zones.
- Quality risks: Addressed via moisture and impurity control, storage hygiene, and in-process checks.
- Price spread compression: Managed through by-product monetisation, flexible polishing options, and focus on value-added packs.
- Operational downtime: Reduced via preventive maintenance, operator training, and critical spares inventory.
- Power and utilities: Managed through reliable connections, load optimisation, and backup arrangements where feasible.
- Regulatory and food safety: Ensured by adherence to applicable standards, periodic audits, and documented SOPs.
- Working capital constraints: Eased by disciplined inventory norms, timely receivables follow-up, and appropriate banking limits.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit creates local employment across procurement, grading, processing, quality control, packaging, logistics, and administration. It enhances farmer incomes through direct market linkage, quality premiums, lower intermediation, and potential patronage benefits. Skill development for operators and youth strengthens the local talent base. Community-level gains include stable supplies of a key staple, circulation of value within the district, and demonstration of an FPO-led rural enterprise model.

### Sustainability
The project promotes short value chains by processing near the farm gate, reducing transport emissions and post-harvest losses. Good storage practices limit wastage, while husk and brokens are channelled to feed or ancillary uses. The unit will prioritise efficient water use in soaking and cleaning, responsible effluent handling, dust control, and energy-efficient equipment. Continuous improvement in packaging materials and logistics will further lower environmental footprint while maintaining product safety.
