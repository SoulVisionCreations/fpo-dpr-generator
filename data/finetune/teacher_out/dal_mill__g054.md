## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents a dal milling enterprise anchored by Deori Urad Farmer Producer Company Limited, Deori, Gondia, Maharashtra. The unit will process chickpea (chana/gram) into dal through a sequence of soaking, drying, cleaning, de-husking, splitting, polishing, and packing. Dal is a staple of Indian cuisine and a critical source of plant-based protein, dietary fibre, iron, and B vitamins. Locating the mill within a pulse-growing rural or semi-urban belt ensures reliable raw material access, reduces transport losses, and enhances farmer realisation through direct procurement.

### Uses & Benefits
- Nutritional mainstay for households, hospitality, institutions, and food processors, contributing to balanced vegetarian diets.
- Versatile ingredient for everyday cooking, ready-to-cook mixes, and packaged foods.
- Long shelf-life when properly milled and packed, supporting year-round supply.
- By-products such as husk and brokens can be channelled to feed, flour blending, or biomass energy, improving overall resource use.
- FPO-led processing strengthens local value capture, stabilises farm incomes, and enables quality-led market entry.

### Market Potential
Dal enjoys sustained, nationwide demand across urban and rural markets, driven by habitual consumption, rising health awareness, and steady institutional off-take. Branded retail packs and quality-certified bulk supply both register consistent traction. Pulses are increasingly favoured in nutrition programs, modern retail, e-commerce, and Horeca segments. Seasonal arrivals and crop diversity create opportunities for strategic raw material planning and year-round operations.

### Value Chain
- Farm-level aggregation: Direct procurement of whole chickpea from member and allied farmers using transparent grading and fair pricing.
- Primary handling: Safe unloading, sampling, storage, and stack management to preserve moisture and quality.
- Conditioning: Soaking in clean water for an adequate period, followed by air or sun drying to achieve uniform tempering for efficient de-husking.
- Cleaning: Reel machines and aspirators remove dust, stones, and coarse and fine impurities.
- De-husking and splitting: Emery roll de-huskers remove husk; lentil splitters separate the cotyledons; secondary cleaning eliminates residual husk.
- Polishing: Gentle polishing to meet market-preferred finish while minimising losses.
- Packing and dispatch: Food-grade sacks for bulk and consumer-grade packs for retail, labelled as per FSSAI and other applicable norms, then routed to wholesalers, retailers, and institutional buyers.
- By-product utilisation: Husk and brokens channelled to feed, flour, or biofuel value chains.

### Competition & Marketing
The dal market features numerous regional mills and several national brands. An FPO-owned unit can differentiate through:
- Consistent quality, traceable sourcing, and hygienic processing aligned with food safety standards.
- Grade-wise offerings for retail and bulk buyers, with clarity on polish levels and moisture norms.
- Branding that highlights farmer ownership, local origin, and nutrition commitments.
- Multi-channel sales: wholesale mandis, regional distributors, modern retail, Horeca and institutional contracts, and digital commerce.
- Relationship-based procurement and supply programs that ensure reliability across seasons.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price volatility: Mitigate via staggered procurement, warehouse receipt systems, and forward tie-ups with member farmers.
- Quality variability and moisture issues: Standardised grading, calibrated soaking/drying protocols, and robust storage practices.
- Operational downtime and energy disruptions: Preventive maintenance, equipment spares planning, and suitable backup power arrangements.
- Food safety and compliance: Implementation of GMP, SOPs, sanitation plans, periodic lab testing, and FSSAI compliance.
- Working capital strain: Structured inventory cycles, disciplined receivables, and bank limits aligned with seasonal stocking.
- Market competition: Differentiation through quality, service levels, and farmer-backed branding with assured traceability.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit creates local employment in operations, quality control, maintenance, logistics, and sales. It promotes women’s participation in sorting, packing, and labelling functions, and builds technical capacity through training. By integrating farmers into procurement and profit-sharing mechanisms, the FPO enhances income stability, encourages adoption of better agronomic practices, and strengthens the rural economy.

### Sustainability
- Resource efficiency through optimised soaking and drying to conserve water and energy.
- Product and by-product valorisation to minimise waste and support circular outcomes.
- Potential integration of renewable energy for auxiliary loads, and improved insulation/ventilation for drying.
- Food-grade reusable or recyclable packaging options to reduce environmental footprint.
- Farmer training on pre- and post-harvest handling to reduce losses at the source.
