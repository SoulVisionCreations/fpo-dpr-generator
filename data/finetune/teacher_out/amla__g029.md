## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Litchi juice is a premium fruit beverage prized for its floral aroma, delicate sweetness, and refreshing character. The fruit’s translucent arils yield a distinctive juice that captures the essence of the orchard when processed with care. This DPR proposes a value-added processing unit for Wyra Litchi Growers Producer Company Ltd, Wyra, Khammam, Telangana, leveraging member-grown litchi to produce pasteurised, preservative-stabilised juice under a controlled and hygienic process.

High-quality raw material is central to this product. Freshly harvested litchi with uniform maturity, clean peels, firm arils, and characteristic aroma is sorted, washed, peeled, and de-seeded promptly to protect flavor and color. Swift movement from farm to processing limits enzymatic browning and microbial load. The recipe uses potable water, food-grade additives as permitted by regulations, and a defined thermal process to ensure safety and shelf stability while retaining natural sensory attributes.

### Uses & Benefits
- As a ready-to-drink beverage, litchi juice offers instant refreshment with the fruit’s natural aroma and sweetness.
- Litchi is known for vitamin C and polyphenols; careful processing helps retain these qualities to the extent possible.
- The juice serves as a versatile base for mocktails, cocktails, smoothies, iced teas, and dessert applications in homes, cafes, and hotels.
- Pasteurisation and permitted preservatives extend shelf life, enabling availability beyond the short harvest window and reducing post-harvest losses at farm level.
- Convenient, hygienic packaging widens access to urban and semi-urban consumers seeking authentic, origin-linked fruit beverages.

### Market Potential
Indian consumers increasingly prefer fruit-based beverages with traceable origin, shorter ingredient lists, and authentic flavor. Litchi stands out as a seasonal delicacy with strong recall, commanding premium positioning relative to common tropical flavors. Modern retail, e-commerce, and food service channels are expanding shelf space for differentiated, region-based products, while gifting and festive demand further support off-take. Institutional buyers value consistent quality, assured supply, and compliance with food safety norms—factors that an FPO-backed brand can credibly deliver.

### Value Chain
The proposed value chain begins with orchard-level aggregation by the FPO, emphasizing timely harvest, field sorting, shaded handling, and rapid transport to the unit. At the plant, a hygienic workflow includes washing, grading, peeling and de-seeding, measured addition of potable water, pulping, filtration for clarity and mouthfeel, and pasteurisation to inactivate spoilage organisms and enzymes. Potassium metabisulphite (KMS), where permitted and correctly declared, protects color and flavor. A final thermal treatment, controlled cooling, and sanitary filling under good manufacturing practices ensure product safety and consistency.

Packaging options include food-grade PET bottles, glass bottles, and pouches, selected to balance shelf life, transport efficiency, and brand presentation. Batch coding and robust documentation enable traceability. Finished goods move through distributors and modern trade partners, while direct-to-consumer sales and institutional supply help smooth seasonality. By-products such as peels and seeds can be channelled into composting, animal feed pre-mixes, or value-extracts where feasible, and process water is managed through cleaning-in-place systems and effluent treatment to meet environmental norms.

### Competition & Marketing
Litchi juice competes with branded fruit drinks, nectars, syrups, carbonated beverages, and local sherbets. Differentiation rests on authentic origin from Wyra, farmer ownership, and consistent sensory quality. A clear positioning—natural litchi taste, traceable source, and hygienic processing—enables premium pricing and loyalty.

Marketing will blend in-store visibility, tasting in modern trade, seasonal campaigns around the litchi harvest, collaborations with cafes and restaurants for signature beverages, and digital storytelling that highlights the growers, the orchards, and responsible processing. Attractive packaging, QR-based provenance, and participation in state and national food fairs can amplify reach.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material seasonality and weather variability: Mitigate through staggered harvest planning with members, short-haul logistics, and a buffer of frozen or aseptic pulp to balance off-season supply.
- Quality and food safety risks: Address via supplier protocols, sanitation standard operating procedures, validated pasteurisation, allergen and preservative declarations, and third-party audits.
- Color and flavor degradation: Control with rapid processing, controlled pH and oxygen exposure, KMS within regulatory limits, and light-protective packaging where needed.
- Power and utility interruptions: Provide backup power and preventive maintenance to protect in-process product.
- Market and price volatility: Diversify channels, secure institutional offtake, and sustain brand building to stabilise realisations.
- Regulatory non-compliance: Adhere to licensing, labeling, and preservative limits, with regular compliance reviews.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project generates inclusive jobs in sorting, peeling and de-seeding, pulping, quality control, packing, warehousing, sales, and administration, with strong potential for women’s participation in hygienic processing roles. It lifts farm-gate realisations by converting perishable fruit into a higher-value, branded product, reduces wastage, builds technical and entrepreneurial skills within the FPO, and retains more value locally through procurement, transport, and ancillary services. Farmer ownership ensures profits and learning circulate within the community.

### Sustainability
Sustainability measures include efficient water management with recirculated rinse streams and proper sanitation, energy-efficient pasteurisation and chilling with insulated lines, and exploration of renewable energy for utilities. Peels and seeds can be valorised or composted to reduce waste. Packaging choices can prioritise recyclability and light-weighting. An effective effluent treatment process safeguards the environment, while transparent sourcing and traceability reinforce responsible practices across the value chain.
