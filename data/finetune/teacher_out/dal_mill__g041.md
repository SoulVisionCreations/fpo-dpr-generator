## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is a staple food product made by milling pulses to produce clean, de-husked, and split grains. This project focuses on gram-based dal (chana dal), produced by the Farmer Producer Organization Pachora Chana Dal Producer Company Ltd in Pachora, Jalgaon, Maharashtra. The enterprise will aggregate whole chickpeas from member farmers, process them into premium-quality dal, and market under an FPO brand while also catering to bulk buyers. The unit aligns with local agro-ecology, leveraging proximity to pulse-growing belts and established trading linkages.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is central to the Indian diet, valued for plant-based protein, fibre, and micronutrients such as iron and B-vitamins. It offers culinary versatility across cuisines, is shelf-stable, and suits both household and institutional kitchens. For producers, primary processing at source reduces wastage and logistics costs, adds value to farm output, and stabilizes incomes. Consumers benefit from improved quality, hygiene, and traceability when dal is processed and packed by a farmer-led enterprise following standardized protocols.

### Market Potential
Pulses hold a strategic place in India’s food and nutrition landscape, with consistent consumption across regions, income bands, and meal occasions. Rising health consciousness, urbanization, branded retail penetration, and growth in modern trade and e-commerce are expanding the addressable market for packaged dal. The food service sector, government and quasi-government institutions, and bulk users provide additional demand anchors. Gram-based dal enjoys robust preference in western and central India, with Jalgaon well connected to nearby mandis and logistics corridors, enabling efficient procurement and distribution.

### Value Chain
- Farm-level aggregation: Member farmers supply whole chickpeas to collection centres with transparent grading and digital weighment.
- Primary handling: Cleaning and safe storage preserve grain quality and minimize post-harvest losses.
- Processing: Soaking and drying condition the grain; cleaning and de-husking remove the husk; splitting, polishing, and grading yield uniform dal with desirable appearance and cooking quality.
- By-product utilization: Husk and brokens can be channelled to feed and value-added applications, creating secondary revenue streams.
- Packaging and dispatch: Retail packs and bulk sacks are sealed, batch-coded, and palletized for distribution through wholesale, retail, institutional, and e-commerce channels.
- Quality assurance: In-process checks for moisture, impurities, and breakage ensure consistency; finished goods comply with FSSAI standards and relevant agmark practices where applicable.

### Competition & Marketing
The market is served by local unbranded mills and established regional and national brands. The FPO’s strategic edge comes from farm-gate sourcing, shorter supply chains, transparent quality, and community-backed branding. The marketing plan combines:
- B2B sales to wholesalers, modern trade, and institutional buyers with consistent specifications and service reliability.
- B2C retail through general trade, modern trade, and online marketplaces under a regionally resonant brand identity.
- Trade promotions, in-store sampling, and digital storytelling around farmer ownership, traceability, and quality.
- Co-branding and white-label opportunities where appropriate to optimize plant utilization.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price and availability risk: Mitigated through seasonal aggregation, diversified geographies, and supplier relationships with member farmers.
- Quality variability: Managed via strict grading, storage hygiene, and standard operating procedures with operator training.
- Processing efficiency: Addressed through preventative maintenance, calibrated equipment, and yield-focused monitoring.
- Power and utility interruptions: Contingency plans include alternate power, water reuse, and scheduled operations.
- Working capital constraints: Structured bank facilities, disciplined inventory planning, and robust receivables management.
- Regulatory compliance: Adherence to FSSAI licensing, packaging and labelling norms, food safety audits, and traceability.
- Market price volatility: Portfolio of channels, forward agreements with institutional buyers, and active engagement with trade networks.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The enterprise will generate skilled and semi-skilled jobs in operations, quality control, packaging, logistics, and administration, with meaningful opportunities for women in grading and packing. Member farmers gain from assured offtake, better realization through aggregation, and patronage-linked benefits. Localized value addition reduces wastage and transportation overheads, stimulates allied services, and strengthens community resilience.

### Sustainability
The project emphasizes efficient use of energy and water, food loss reduction, and circular use of by-products. Clean-in-place protocols, hygienic material flows, and improved packaging extend product life and reduce spoilage. Options such as solar augmentation, water recycling from soaking, responsible husk utilization, and recyclable packaging support environmental stewardship. Inclusive governance and transparent pricing reinforce social sustainability and farmer welfare.
