## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Mango juice is a popular, naturally sweet, and aromatic fruit beverage processed from ripe, sound mangoes. It captures the characteristic color, flavor, and mouthfeel of the fruit, offering a refreshing experience while preserving much of the fruit’s natural goodness. For Keshkal Mango & Spices FPC Ltd in Kondagaon, Chhattisgarh, mango juice presents a strategic value-addition pathway to stabilize farm incomes, reduce post-harvest losses, and extend the availability of mango’s sensory appeal beyond the short harvest window.

Processing begins with careful selection and cleaning of fruit, followed by seed removal, pulping, and clarification or fine filtration to achieve the desired consistency. The juice is pasteurized to ensure safety and shelf life, with the option to use permitted preservatives such as KMS (potassium metabisulfite) in compliance with relevant standards. Filled and sealed under hygienic conditions, the product is offered in convenient retail packs suitable for households, food service, and institutional buyers.

Raw materials include locally sourced processing-grade mangoes from member farmers, potable water treated to beverage standards, and approved food additives wherever specified under the applicable food safety norms. Packaging materials such as food-grade bottles, closures, and labels support product integrity and market presentation.

### Uses & Benefits
Mango juice serves as a ready-to-drink fruit beverage for households, hospitality, and institutional channels. It offers convenience, consistent quality, and familiar taste year-round. Consumers value its fruit-forward profile, perceived nutritional attributes, and versatility for direct consumption, mocktails, culinary applications, and festive occasions.

For the FPO and its farmer-members, processing converts a perishable crop into a storable, marketable product, mitigating the risk of distress sales and harvesting losses. By channeling surplus and processing-grade fruit into juice, the FPO unlocks better price realization, improves inventory management, and enhances brand presence linked to the Keshkal origin. The enterprise also catalyzes local employment, skill development, and entrepreneurship in packaging, logistics, and retail distribution.

### Market Potential
Mango-based beverages enjoy strong acceptance across urban, semi-urban, and rural markets. Demand is driven by familiarity with mango flavor, rising preference for fruit-based drinks, and expanding retail footprints in both traditional and modern trade. HoReCa and institutional procurement are additional pull factors, particularly during warmer months and festive seasons. Increasing consumer interest in origin-linked, minimally processed, and hygienically packed beverages favors FPO-led brands that can communicate authentic farm stories and transparent sourcing.

E-commerce and quick-commerce channels enable broader reach without heavy upfront investments in physical retail. The FPO can also tap regional schools, canteens, and local events, positioning mango juice as a safe, standardized, and community-backed beverage. With appropriate certifications and quality assurance, regional brands can steadily capture mindshare amid a market historically dominated by large national players.

### Value Chain
- Farm: Aggregation of ripe, sound mangoes from member orchards; emphasis on maturity indices, varietal segregation, and careful handling to limit bruising.
- Procurement & Grading: Sorting to remove damaged fruit; cleaning and sanitization; storage in ventilated crates to maintain quality before processing.
- Processing: Washing, seed removal, pulping, filtration, standardization with potable water as per product specifications, pasteurization, optional use of KMS within legal limits, and hygienic filling and packing.
- Packaging & Storage: Use of food-grade primary packaging, appropriate closures, labeling in line with FSSAI requirements, and storage under recommended conditions.
- Distribution: Movement through wholesalers, retailers, institutional buyers, e-commerce platforms, and direct-to-consumer channels; potential cold-chain linkages for quality assurance in warmer months.
- By-products & Waste: Peel and seed streams can be valorized into cattle feed, compost, pectin-rich extracts, or kernel oil, contributing to circularity and incremental revenue.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition includes established beverage brands, regional processors, and fresh juice vendors. To differentiate, the FPO can:
- Position on farm-origin authenticity, community ownership, and transparent sourcing.
- Emphasize hygienic processing, compliance with food safety standards, and consistent sensory quality.
- Offer attractive packaging, varied pack sizes, and clear ingredient labeling.
- Promote via in-store sampling, community events, and local influencer collaborations.
- Build institutional relationships with schools, canteens, and tourism operators.
- Use digital channels for storytelling, traceability features, and seasonal promotions.

Branding that celebrates Keshkal’s orchards and farmer ownership can enhance trust and repeat purchase. Quality consistency and reliable availability are critical to winning shelf space and building long-term customer loyalty.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw Material Seasonality and Price Swings: Mitigate via member contracts, planned procurement calendars, on-farm handling protocols, and buffer stocks of pulp or semi-processed intermediates.
- Quality Variability: Standardize varietal blends, maturity criteria, and sanitation; deploy trained quality staff and documented SOPs; implement regular product testing.
- Food Safety & Compliance: Maintain GMP, SSOPs, and HACCP-based controls; ensure traceability, batch coding, and a documented recall plan; comply with FSSAI labeling and additive norms.
- Processing Downtime: Engage preventive maintenance and AMCs; maintain critical spares; provide power backup to safeguard heat-treatment and filling operations.
- Market Acceptance & Competition: Differentiate through origin-led branding, targeted channel strategies, and competitive packaging; manage trade schemes prudently.
- Logistics & Climate Risks: Use robust secondary packaging, temperature-aware transport, and insurance; diversify channels to reduce single-market exposure.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The enterprise creates local jobs in processing, quality control, packaging, warehousing, sales, and transport, with strong scope for women’s participation in sorting, packing, and labeling. Member farmers gain improved market access, technical advisory on harvest timing and handling, and steadier incomes through pooled procurement. Skill-building in food safety, equipment operation, and sales expands livelihood options for youth. By anchoring value addition within Keshkal, the FPO strengthens the rural economy, encourages orchard care, and reduces post-harvest waste.

### Sustainability
Sustainability priorities include efficient water use with in-plant reuse where permissible, responsible effluent treatment, and energy-saving through optimized heat recovery and efficient motors. Organic waste can be converted into compost, cattle feed, or inputs for small-scale extraction, reducing landfill burden. Adoption of renewable energy, responsible packaging choices, and local sourcing curtail the product’s environmental footprint. Transparent governance, fair procurement from members, and community investments further embed social sustainability into the business model.
