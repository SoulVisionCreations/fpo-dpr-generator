## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents an integrated dal milling enterprise for Kaliabor Pulses Producer Company Ltd, Silghat, Kaliabor, Nagaon, Assam. The project focuses on gram-based dal, produced by de-husking and splitting whole chickpea into clean, uniform halves. The activity is well-aligned with rural and semi-urban pulse belts where raw material aggregation, primary processing, and market linkages can be efficiently organized by an FPO. The product is a daily staple in Indian households and institutional kitchens, and serves as a dependable, shelf-stable source of plant protein.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a versatile ingredient used across cuisines for everyday meals, ready-to-cook blends, snack manufacturing, and value-added food products. Nutritionally, it provides high-quality plant protein along with dietary fibre, iron, and B-vitamins, making it central to balanced vegetarian diets. For consumers, key benefits include consistent cooking quality, clean grains free of stones and husk, and convenient packaging. For farmers and the FPO, the enterprise enables local value addition, better price realization, and reduced post-harvest losses through scientific handling and storage.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal is resilient and broad-based, spanning retail households, hotels and caterers, food processors, and public nutrition channels. Increasing health consciousness, preference for protein-rich vegetarian foods, and the steady shift to branded, hygienically processed staples underpin structural growth. Assam and neighbouring states provide strong demand corridors, while improved logistics and e-commerce open access to distant markets. Policy thrust on local processing, FPO-led aggregation, and MSME strengthening further support the business case.

### Value Chain
- Farm production: Member farmers cultivate chickpea; the FPO anchors aggregation, primary grading, and traceability.
- Procurement and storage: Clean, dry whole gram is procured directly from members and stored in ventilated, pest-managed facilities with moisture-safe handling.
- Pre-processing: Soaking and drying condition the grain for optimal de-husking performance, with attention to uniformity and product integrity.
- Milling: Cleaning removes foreign matter; emery roll dehusking separates husk; splitting machines produce uniform dal; polishing is applied as per market preference for polished or unpolished variants.
- By-products: Husk and brokens are channelled to feed, flour blending, or bio-fuel uses, contributing to resource efficiency.
- Packaging and dispatch: Product is packed in food-grade sacks or consumer packs with proper labelling, then supplied to wholesalers, retailers, institutions, and online partners.

### Competition & Marketing
The market comprises local mills supplying bulk channels and larger brands serving organized retail. The FPO can differentiate on farmer-traceable sourcing, consistent quality, and fair, transparent pricing. Marketing strategy centers on:
- B2B supply to wholesalers, institutional kitchens, processors, and government procurement where eligible.
- Regional retail through distributors and modern trade, with clear labelling that highlights quality, origin, and FPO ownership.
- Direct-to-consumer pilots via e-commerce and social media, offering both polished and unpolished options and recipe-oriented packs.
- Quality cues like low foreign matter, stable cooking time, uniform grain size, and food-safe packaging.
- Relationship-building with community institutions, self-help groups, and kirana networks for sustained offtake.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material variability and price volatility: Mitigated through staggered procurement, member forward agreements, prudent stocking, and hedging instruments where accessible.
- Quality risks: Addressed via moisture control, calibrated soaking and drying, calibrated machine settings, and batch-wise quality testing.
- Storage and pest risks: Managed by good warehouse practices, clean pallets, hermetic/food-safe liners, and approved fumigation protocols.
- Operational downtime: Minimized with preventive maintenance, critical spares, trained operators, and reliable power backup.
- Working capital strain: Eased through disciplined inventory cycles, supplier and buyer credit terms, bank limits, and insurance-backed receivables where feasible.
- Compliance and food safety: Ensured through adherence to FSSAI, standard operating procedures, and audit-ready documentation.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit creates skilled and semi-skilled employment in operations, quality control, maintenance, warehousing, and sales. Women’s participation can be strong in grading, packing, and quality roles. Member farmers benefit from assured procurement, transparent weighing and payments, and dividends when surpluses accrue. Localized processing keeps value within the community, stimulates allied services like transport and packaging, and strengthens the resilience of farm incomes.

### Sustainability
The dal mill adopts resource-efficient practices: careful water use in conditioning, reuse and safe disposal protocols, energy-efficient dryers and motors, and dust control for a clean environment. By-product utilization lowers waste intensity and can substitute fossil fuels. Good housekeeping, LED lighting, and optional solar integration reduce operating footprint. Food safety and traceability systems support responsible sourcing and consumer confidence.
