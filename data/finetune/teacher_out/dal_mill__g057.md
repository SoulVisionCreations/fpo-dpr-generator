## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents a dal milling enterprise for Ghansor Seoni Dalhan Producer Company Limited, located in Ghansor, Seoni, Madhya Pradesh. The unit will process whole chickpea (chana/gram) into split dal through de-husking and splitting, with cleaning, polishing, and hygienic packaging. The model strengthens farmer ownership across the value chain, converting locally grown pulses into a branded, traceable product that meets contemporary food safety, quality, and shelf-life expectations. Locating the mill in a pulse-growing belt shortens procurement distances, improves raw material control, and anchors value addition within the producing community.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a daily staple across Indian households and institutional kitchens, prized for its protein density and balanced nutrition, including fibre, iron, and B‑vitamins. It is versatile in culinary applications, supports vegetarian and vegan diets, and aligns with regional taste preferences. A farmer-owned dal mill enhances price realization at the farm gate, reduces post-harvest losses, and promotes rural enterprise. By-products such as husk and brokens can be gainfully utilized for cattle feed, fuel, or further processing, improving overall resource efficiency.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal is broad-based and resilient, cutting across rural and urban markets and diverse income segments. Retail trends show a steady shift toward clean, graded, and packaged pulses with assured provenance and hygiene. Institutional consumption through hostels, catering, and food service adds depth to demand. A traceable FPO label enables transparent sourcing, farmer connect, and trust. With steady year-round consumption and strong cultural anchoring, dal offers a stable product platform for a community-owned processing venture.

### Value Chain
- Farm-level production by member growers, followed by aggregation and primary grading at collection centres.
- Procurement and scientific storage of whole chickpea in aerated, pest-managed godowns with quality checks for moisture, purity, and varietal uniformity.
- Conditioning through soaking and controlled drying to prepare kernels for efficient de-husking.
- Cleaning to remove foreign matter, staged de-husking with emery rolls, screening to separate husk, splitting into dal, and optional polishing to market preference.
- Quality assurance covering physical parameters, hygiene, and packaging integrity, with batch-wise traceability.
- Packing into customer-friendly retail and bulk formats, dispatch to wholesale, retail, institutional, and e-commerce channels.
- Utilization of husk and brokens to maximize value recovery and minimize waste.

### Competition & Marketing
The competitive set includes regional mills, national brands, and private labels. Differentiation for the FPO will rest on consistent quality, traceability to member farmers, honest weights, and reliable supply. A balanced channel mix covering local kirana stores, modern retail, institutional buyers, and online platforms will diversify offtake. Branding will emphasize farmer ownership, purity, and traditional taste, supported by standardized packaging, clear labelling, food safety compliance, and periodic consumer engagement. Partnerships with self-help groups, canteens, and local government institutions can deepen community reach.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material availability and price volatility: mitigate through staggered procurement, storage planning, and strong member engagement with transparent pricing policies.
- Quality variability and storage losses: manage with strict inward QC, moisture control, fumigation protocols, and first-in-first-out practices.
- Operational downtime: reduce via preventive maintenance, spares inventory, and operator training.
- Working capital stress: plan with appropriate limits, timely receivable collection, and alignment of procurement cycles with cash flows.
- Regulatory compliance: ensure valid FSSAI license, GST registration, factory and pollution consents, and food safety systems with documented SOPs.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project creates skilled and semi-skilled jobs in operations, stores, quality, maintenance, logistics, and sales, with meaningful roles for women and youth. Members benefit from better market linkages, lower transaction costs, and improved realization through value addition and patronage-based returns. Local sourcing and processing retain margins in the community, stimulate ancillary enterprises, and enhance resilience in the rural economy.

### Sustainability
Sustainability measures include efficient motors and dust collection, reuse of husk as fuel or feed, responsible water use and recycling, and safe storage practices that reduce losses. Clean production, worker safety, and food-grade materials contribute to environmental and social responsibility. The FPO can progressively adopt renewable energy, green packaging options, and extended producer responsibility where applicable, reinforcing long-term viability.
