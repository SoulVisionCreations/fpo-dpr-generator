## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Teesta Valley Citrus Growers FPC Ltd, based in Melli, Namchi, Sikkim, proposes to process and market freshly extracted orange juice from locally grown citrus. The product is a hygienically prepared, filtered, and pasteurised juice beverage that retains the bright aroma, natural color, and refreshing tart-sweet profile characteristic of Himalayan mandarins and sweet oranges. Processing stabilises the juice for safe ambient storage, enabling round-the-year availability while preserving sensory quality.

The FPO sources citrus directly from member orchards, ensuring traceability from farm to bottle. Standardised operating procedures cover selection of mature fruit, gentle handling to avoid peel oil bitterness, effective seed and rag removal, controlled dilution with potable water as per beverage standards, and pasteurisation for safety. A permitted preservative, potassium metabisulphite (KMS), is added as per regulations to support shelf stability. The finished product is packed via hot-fill into food-grade containers, cooled, and stored under good warehousing practices.

### Uses & Benefits
- A convenient, ready-to-serve beverage rich in natural citrus nutrients and refreshing flavour, suitable for homes, hotels, cafes, travel, and institutional settings.
- Versatile for mocktails, smoothies, desserts, bakery applications, marinades, and culinary glazes.
- Offers consistent quality and hygiene compared to street-vended juices, with reliable labelling and compliance to food safety norms.
- Enables farmers to realise better value by converting perishable fruit into a longer-life product, reducing distress sales and post-harvest losses.
- Supports regional branding of Sikkim citrus, creating a differentiated, origin-linked product story.

### Market Potential
Packaged fruit beverages enjoy growing preference among health-conscious consumers seeking clean-label, fruit-forward options. Within the broader non-alcoholic ready-to-drink category, orange remains a classic, high-acceptance flavour across age groups and geographies. The North-Eastern Himalayas see strong tourism and hospitality demand, which creates steady offtake for premium and natural-positioned beverages. Institutional buyers, modern retail, and e-commerce channels further expand reach for an authentic, farmer-owned citrus brand from Sikkim. Government thrust on micro food enterprises and local value addition strengthens the ecosystem for such ventures.

### Value Chain
The value chain begins with orchard-level harvesting at optimal maturity, field sorting, and farm-gate aggregation through FPO collection points. Produce is transported in ventilated crates to the processing unit. The processing sequence includes washing, seed removal and segmentation, controlled dilution with potable water, mechanical pulping, fine filtration, pasteurisation at an appropriate temperature, cooling, addition of KMS within regulatory limits, a final heat treatment, hot-fill packaging, cooling, and storage.

By-products such as peels and seeds can be valorised as cattle feed, compost, or processed for essential oils and pectin, enhancing overall resource efficiency. Finished goods move through distributors, modern trade, local retailers, institutional buyers, and direct-to-consumer channels, supported by cold-chain only where needed for premium lines. Routine quality control checks monitor physico-chemical and microbiological parameters at inbound, in-process, and finished stages.

### Competition & Marketing
The product will compete with national fruit beverage brands and regional players, alongside fresh juice kiosks. The FPO’s differentiation anchors on orchard-origin sourcing, farmer ownership, a clean, trustworthy ingredient list with permitted preservatives only, and a distinctive Sikkim citrus taste profile. The branding strategy highlights terroir, traceability, and responsible processing. Marketing will combine in-store tastings, digital storytelling, seasonal promotions, and co-branding with tourism and hospitality stakeholders. A balanced SKU mix for retail and institutional packs will address diverse use occasions, while trade schemes and reliable service levels will build repeat offtake.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material seasonality and weather variability: mitigated through staggered harvesting, multi-orchard procurement, primary grading at collection centres, and short-term bulk storage of semi-processed intermediates where feasible.
- Quality variability: addressed through orchard advisories, maturity indexing, and strict incoming QC.
- Food safety and shelf-life risks: mitigated via validated pasteurisation, controlled KMS dosing, hygienic design of equipment, sanitation SOPs, and routine lab testing under a food safety management system.
- Market and pricing pressures: managed by product differentiation, disciplined cost control, and channel diversification.
- Utility and equipment downtime: contained through preventive maintenance, critical spares, service contracts, and backup power arrangements.
- Packaging supply disruptions: covered by dual sourcing and safety stock policies.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project creates steady employment in processing, packaging, quality control, warehousing, and logistics, with strong opportunities for local youth and women in both shopfloor and sales roles. Farmer members gain improved price realisation and assured offtake, while auxiliary livelihoods develop in crate supply, transport, maintenance, and retail. The initiative strengthens the regional citrus cluster, reduces post-harvest waste, and channels a larger share of consumer spend back to producer households.

### Sustainability
Sikkim’s ecological ethos and emphasis on low-chemical agriculture align well with a citrus-based processing enterprise. The unit will prioritise water-efficient washing systems, recovery and reuse where permissible, and compliant effluent treatment. Organic waste from peels and seeds will be diverted to feed or compost, supporting circularity. Energy efficiency measures and progressive adoption of renewables will curb operating emissions. Responsible packaging choices and reverse logistics pilots will be explored in collaboration with partners.
