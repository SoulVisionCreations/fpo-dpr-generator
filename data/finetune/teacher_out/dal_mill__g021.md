## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Vidarbha Tur Producer Company Ltd, Patur, Patur, Akola, Maharashtra proposes to establish a dal milling unit focusing on gram-based dal. Dal is produced by de-husking and splitting whole pulses such as chickpea into clean, uniform cotyledons suitable for household and institutional consumption. As a staple in the Indian diet, pulses are valued for plant-based protein, dietary fibre, iron and B‑complex vitamins. Setting up the unit within a pulse-growing belt enables cost-effective raw material aggregation, quality control at source, and timely processing aligned with harvest cycles. The proposed facility aligns with cluster-based value addition, standardized processing, and market linkage objectives under the PM-FME approach.

### Uses & Benefits
- Core ingredient in daily meals across regions, cuisines and income segments.
- Versatile for home cooking, community kitchens, restaurants and ready-to-cook applications.
- Nutritionally dense, shelf-stable and easy to transport and store.
- Enables consistent quality, cleanliness and palatability through controlled soaking, de-husking, splitting and polishing.
- Generates marketable by-products such as husk and brokens that can be channelled into feed and other uses.
- Offers farmers a reliable outlet and price realization through direct procurement by the FPO.
- Enhances resilience of local food systems by shortening the supply chain and reducing post-harvest losses.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal remains resilient due to its central role in Indian dietary habits, growing urbanization, health awareness, and preference for minimally processed, traceable food staples. Retail modern trade, neighborhood kirana, e-commerce grocery platforms and institutional buyers together provide a broad channel mix. The project leverages regional raw material availability, logistics connectivity from Akola to consumption markets, and the trust advantages of farmer-owned branding. Value addition through consistent grading, moisture management and food safety assurance supports a steady premium over undifferentiated commodity supplies.

### Value Chain
- Aggregation: Direct procurement of whole gram from member and associate farmers using transparent quality and moisture parameters; price discovery through seasonal pooling and forward alignment with demand.
- Primary handling: Scientific storage with pallets and aeration; pre-cleaning to remove stones, dust and foreign matter; water soaking for loosening husk; drying through ambient or mechanical systems to stabilize for milling.
- Milling: Cleaning in reel machines, de-husking in emery roll dehuskers, secondary cleaning to separate husk, splitting in lentil splitters, and controlled polishing for desired appearance while maintaining nutrition.
- Packing & dispatch: Grading, weight-checking and hygienic packing in sacks or retail packs; labeling compliant with food standards; dispatch through transporters to wholesale mandis, distributors and institutions.
- Quality management: Standard operating procedures for each stage, in-process checks for cleanliness and damage, packaging integrity tests, and periodic third-party validation.
- By-product utilization: Husk and brokens channelized to local feed and allied markets to optimize recovery and reduce waste.
- Enablers: Input partnerships for packaging material, lab tie-ups for quality verification, and logistics contracts for reliable, cost-effective distribution.

### Competition & Marketing
The competitive landscape includes local mills serving nearby mandis and national brands supplying packaged dal. Differentiation for the FPO will rest on farm-to-fork traceability, consistent particle size and cooking performance, minimized admixture, lower breakage, controlled moisture, and credible food safety practices. A dual-channel strategy will address:
- Institutional and bulk buyers seeking consistency and reliable deliveries.
- Retail consumers via distributors and modern trade through branded packs emphasizing origin, farmer ownership and quality assurance.
Promotions will focus on provenance, purity, nutrition and community impact, supported by sampling, influencer outreach in regional markets, and co-branding with allied FPOs where relevant.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: Mitigated through staggered procurement, member supply commitments, and inventory planning.
- Quality variability: Addressed with pre-procurement testing, calibrated soaking and drying protocols, and in-line quality checks.
- Processing downtime: Prevented via preventive maintenance, on-call OEM support, and critical spares stocking.
- Market and price risk: Reduced by diversified channels, forward sales to anchor buyers, and dynamic packaging formats for different segments.
- Working capital strain: Managed through structured receivables, credit discipline, and season-aligned banking limits.
- Compliance and food safety: Ensured through licensing, robust hygiene practices, pest management, and documented traceability with recall readiness.
- Climate and storage risk: Mitigated by weather-aware procurement scheduling, ventilation, moisture control and insurance coverage.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit generates skilled and semi-skilled employment in operations, quality, warehousing, sales and administration, with targeted roles for women in grading and packing. It strengthens farmer incomes through direct market access, transparent quality benchmarking, and reduced intermediation. Local service ecosystems in transport, packaging, maintenance and testing also benefit. The FPO model retains value within the community, builds managerial capacity, and promotes inclusive, resilient rural enterprise.

### Sustainability
Environmentally responsible operations will include efficient motors, daylighting, and dust extraction; water stewardship through controlled soaking and reuse; and beneficial use of by-products to move towards low-waste processing. Responsible packaging choices, safe chemical management, and clean housekeeping contribute to steady compliance. Over time, renewable energy options and green logistics partnerships can further reduce the footprint while enhancing brand value.
