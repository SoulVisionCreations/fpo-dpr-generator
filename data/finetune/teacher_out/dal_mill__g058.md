## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is the split form of pulses produced by de-husking and splitting whole grains such as chickpea, locally known as chana or gram. Pulses are a cornerstone of the Indian vegetarian diet, valued for protein, dietary fibre, and essential micronutrients like iron and B‑vitamins. This project proposes a gram-based dal mill to be owned and operated by Dongargarh Black Gram Producer Company Limited in Dongargarh, Rajnandgaon, Chhattisgarh. Locating the mill in a pulse-growing belt enables direct farmer linkages, lower logistics costs, and fresher, traceable raw material. The plant will undertake procurement, scientific storage, pre-conditioning, de-husking, splitting, polishing, grading, and packaging for both bulk and retail markets.

### Uses & Benefits
- Household staple consumed daily across cooking traditions in India, with strong repeat demand.
- Widely used in institutional kitchens, food service, and food processing, including ready-to-cook and blended products.
- Nutritional attributes support health-conscious and protein-focused diets.
- For the FPO, dal milling captures value within the community, stabilizes farm-gate prices, reduces distress sales, and creates local employment.
- By-products such as husk and brokens have viable markets in feed and allied uses, improving overall recovery value.

### Market Potential
India is among the largest producers and consumers of pulses, and dal enjoys year-round, inelastic demand across urban and rural markets. Rising incomes, organized retail penetration, and growing preference for hygienically processed and consistently graded dal support premiumization and brand building. The FPO’s proximity to raw material clusters provides an edge on procurement freshness, aggregation efficiency, and traceability. There is meaningful room for import substitution and for serving regional taste profiles through tailored polishing and grading standards. Retail packs, institutional bulk sales, and e-commerce channels collectively provide a diversified demand base.

### Value Chain
The value chain starts with aggregation from member farmers and local producers, followed by cleaning and pre-conditioning of the raw grain. Soaked and dried grain is cleaned, de-husked in emery roll dehuskers, passed through aspiration and grading systems to remove husk and grit, and then split in lentil splitters. Polishing is adjusted to market preference, after which graded dal is packed and dispatched. Quality control at critical points ensures moisture, purity, and uniform grain size. Storage practices maintain grain integrity and minimize losses. Husk, fines, and brokens are safely collected for by-product monetization. Efficient logistics and inventory planning align procurement with seasonal harvests and steady-year sales. End-to-end documentation strengthens traceability and compliance.

### Competition & Marketing
The market features a mix of traditional mills supplying loose dal and organized players offering branded, packaged products. The FPO can differentiate through direct-from-farm sourcing, consistent grading, strict moisture and impurity control, hygienic processing, and credible branding around farmer ownership. A dual-channel strategy can serve wholesale mandis and institutional buyers with bulk packs while building a regional brand in consumer packs through kirana stores, modern trade, and online platforms. Transparent quality claims, tamper-proof packaging, and clear labeling enhance trust. Promotional focus on local origin, freshness, and fair trade to farmers reinforces the brand story.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw-material price and availability risk mitigated through forward aggregation plans with members, diversified sourcing, and timely procurement aligned to harvests.
- Quality and storage risks addressed via scientific warehousing, moisture and impurity testing, and first-in-first-out inventory control.
- Operational risks from power and machinery downtime mitigated through preventive maintenance, service agreements, trained operators, spares inventory, and backup power.
- Market and price volatility countered by diversified customer segments, agile pack-size strategy, and continuous market intelligence.
- Compliance and food safety risks managed through robust SOPs, worker hygiene, sanitation, pest control, and certification-led systems.
- Financial risks reduced through prudent working-capital planning, credit control on debtors, and suitable insurance coverage.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project creates steady local jobs in procurement, operations, quality control, packaging, warehousing, and sales. Women’s participation is encouraged in sorting, packing, and quality roles. Member farmers benefit from assured offtake, transparent quality-based pricing, patronage returns, and capacity building on post-harvest handling. Local value addition spurs allied services such as transport and packaging supply, while better price realization and reduced wastage strengthen rural incomes and resilience.

### Sustainability
Energy-efficient machinery, dust collection, and good housekeeping minimize losses and improve air quality. Renewable options, improved lighting, and optimized process flow reduce energy intensity. Water used for soaking is managed responsibly with reuse and safe disposal practices. By-products are gainfully utilized as feed or fuel, reducing waste. Preference for recyclable or biodegradable packaging and responsible sourcing supports a lower environmental footprint. Farmer-owned governance embeds inclusive growth and long-term stewardship of resources.
