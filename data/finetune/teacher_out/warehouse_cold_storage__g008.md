## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Upper Assam Riverbank Warehousing Producer Company Ltd proposes a scientific grain storage warehouse at Titabor, Jorhat, Assam. The facility will rent out safe and compliant storage space to farmers, FPOs, processors, and traders for cereals, pulses, oilseeds, and allied produce. The warehouse consumes no raw material; it sells storage capacity as a service. Deposited commodities remain the property of depositors, while the FPO earns rental and service income for receipt, scientific storage, fumigation, inventory management, and dispatch.

Operations will follow structured standard operating procedures: receipt, weighing, and quality checks; stacking and scientific storage under controlled conditions; scheduled fumigation and periodic quality monitoring; robust digital inventory with warehouse-receipt issuance; and orderly dispatch with exit weighing and documentation. The project aligns with PM-FME objectives by strengthening post-harvest infrastructure, reducing losses, and improving farmer incomes through better timing of sales.

### Uses & Benefits
- Enables farmers and FPO members to store produce safely, avoid distress sales during glut periods, and liquidate stock when market conditions improve.
- Reduces post-harvest losses through moisture control, pest management, and standardized handling practices.
- Facilitates access to finance via warehouse receipts, improving liquidity and bargaining power.
- Offers value-added services such as weighing, grading, re-bagging, fumigation, and quality certification to support trade and procurement.
- Improves supply chain reliability for processors and traders through assured quality, traceability, and predictable dispatches.

### Market Potential
Assam’s agricultural landscape features significant production of paddy, pulses, maize, and oilseeds, with marked seasonality and weather-linked volatility. Regional aggregation hubs around Jorhat witness heavy arrivals during harvest and limited private storage, leading to price pressure and post-harvest loss risks. Public agencies, millers, poultry and feed units, and edible oil processors prefer reliable storage close to sourcing zones to smoothen procurement and reduce logistics costs.

Growing adoption of electronic trading platforms, interest in warehouse receipt finance, and policy thrust on scientific warehousing together create strong demand for compliant storage. The location at Titabor offers proximity to farm clusters and arterial roads, providing a service hub for multiple blocks in Upper Assam and neighboring districts.

### Value Chain
- Upstream: Farmers, FPOs, primary collectors, and procurement agencies deliver produce to the warehouse where it is weighed, sampled, and quality-graded.
- Core operations: Stacking on pallets or dunnage, controlled ventilation, scheduled fumigation, infestation monitoring, and digital stock accounting with traceability. Storage protocols follow commodity-specific standards for moisture, aeration, and stacking stability.
- Financial linkages: Warehouse receipts enable pledge finance from banks and NBFCs. The FPO coordinates with lenders, insurers, and rating/certification agencies to streamline credit and risk cover.
- Downstream: Traders, processors, and institutional buyers lift stocks based on sales contracts, tenders, or spot trade. Timely dispatches, accurate documentation, and transport coordination ensure efficient turnaround and repeat business.

### Competition & Marketing
The competitive set includes central and state warehousing corporations, private godowns, and miller-owned stores. The FPO’s edge lies in farmer-centric services, transparent pricing, digital inventory visibility, and doorstep facilitation for smallholders. Marketing will combine direct outreach to member villages, tie-ups with nearby FPOs and PACS, onboarding of lenders for pledge finance, and collaboration with processors seeking just-in-time supply.

A relationship-based approach will be adopted: seasonal booking drives before harvest, assured fumigation schedules, bundled services for high-volume clients, and loyalty incentives for repeat users. Participation in agri-trade networks and coordination with procurement programmes will help maintain healthy occupancy across seasons.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Occupancy risk from crop variability: Mitigated through multi-commodity storage, flexible tenancy terms, and procurement tie-ups.
- Quality and pest risks: Addressed via strict SOPs, trained staff, scheduled fumigation, integrated pest management, and routine audits.
- Counterparty and receivable risks: Managed with clear service agreements, invoice discipline, pledge finance linkages, and appropriate security.
- Regulatory and compliance risk: Managed by aligning with warehousing standards, fire and safety norms, and, where applicable, accreditation requirements.
- Disaster and climate risks: Reduced by a raised plinth, robust drainage, structural resilience, insurance coverage, and emergency response plans.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The facility generates local employment for operations, quality control, security, and administration, with targeted skilling for youth and inclusion of women in record-keeping and quality roles. Members benefit from improved price realization, access to formal finance, and reduced loss. Service linkages with transporters, weighbridge vendors, and input suppliers stimulate the local economy. The FPO model strengthens producer bargaining power and fosters cooperative growth.

### Sustainability
The warehouse will prioritize energy-efficient lighting and ventilation, adopt renewable energy where feasible, and practice responsible chemical stewardship with emphasis on integrated pest management. Rainwater and stormwater management will protect stacks and reduce runoff. By preventing food loss and enabling efficient logistics, the project contributes to lower emissions intensity across the value chain. Waste minimization, safe disposal protocols, and community health and safety practices will be embedded in operations.
