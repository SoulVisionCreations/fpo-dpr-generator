## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is a staple split pulse, produced by de‑husking and splitting whole pulses such as chickpea. It is central to Indian diets, especially among vegetarian households, and is prized for balanced nutrition, culinary versatility, and affordability. The proposed dal mill of Akot Moong Dal Producer Company Ltd in Akot, Akola district, Maharashtra, leverages proximity to pulse-growing clusters, direct farm linkages, and an efficient FPO-led aggregation model. The plant will undertake cleaning, conditioning, de‑husking, splitting, polishing, and packing, ensuring consistent quality and traceability from farm to fork.

### Uses & Benefits
- Core protein source in daily meals across regional cuisines, suitable for home cooking, institutional kitchens, and food service.
- Rich in fibre and micronutrients, aiding satiety and digestive wellness while complementing cereals nutritionally.
- Offers diverse value-added formats such as polished and unpolished styles, various grades, and assorted pack formats for retail, wholesale, and institutional buyers.
- By‑products like husk and brokens provide additional feed and fuel value, improving overall resource efficiency.
- Shelf‑stable product with low cold-chain dependence, enabling distribution across rural and urban markets.

### Market Potential
- Broad, year‑round consumption driven by household staples, ready‑to‑cook demand, and rising preferences for hygienically packed products.
- Expanding modern retail, e‑commerce, and institutional catering opens new channels, while traditional kirana and mandi buyers remain strong.
- Increasing consumer attention to quality parameters such as cleanliness, sorting accuracy, and consistency enhances demand for standardized, branded FPO offerings.
- The project benefits from farm‑proximate procurement, potentially lowering logistics costs and quality risks while improving farmer realization through collective marketing.

### Value Chain
- Farm aggregation: Direct procurement of whole chickpea from member farmers and allied growers, supported by transparent grading and fair payments.
- Primary handling: Safe unloading, sampling, and scientific storage to preserve quality and reduce moisture and pest risks.
- Pre‑processing: Prolonged soaking and controlled drying to condition kernels for efficient de‑husking and splitting, with careful handling to minimize breakage.
- Milling: Sequential cleaning, de‑husking with emery rolls, fine aspiration to remove husk, precision splitting, and optional polishing tailored to market preferences.
- Quality assurance: In‑house checks for moisture, purity, uniform grain size, and food safety compliance before packing.
- Packing and dispatch: Durable, food‑grade sacks and consumer packs, proper palletization, and dispatch to wholesale, retail, institutional, and online channels.
- By‑product utilization: Husk and brokens diverted to feed and biofuel users, improving recovery values and sustainability metrics.

### Competition & Marketing
- Competitive landscape spans traditional unbranded mills, regional brands, and large national labels with strong distribution. Private labels in modern retail are expanding.
- Differentiation levers for the FPO include farmer‑linked traceability, consistent recovery and sorting, food‑safe processing, and steady supply through harvest and lean seasons.
- Marketing strategy emphasizes:
  - Quality assurance with food safety licensing and adherence to recognized standards; optional agri‑mark quality endorsement for select grades.
  - Pack formats for households, hotels, caterers, and institutions, with clear labelling on origin, quality grade, and cooking characteristics.
  - Route‑to‑market blending mandi sales, distributor networks, direct institutional tie‑ups, and digital commerce.
  - Brand storytelling around farmer ownership, local sourcing, and freshness to win consumer trust.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: Addressed through staggered procurement, diversified sourcing within the pulse belt, and prudent inventory cover aligned to working capital.
- Quality variability: Mitigated by farm‑gate grading, moisture and impurity checks, and conditioning protocols tuned to each lot.
- Operational risks: Reduced via preventive maintenance, skilled operators, calibrated machines, and energy‑efficient utilities with backup arrangements.
- Market and price risks: Managed through multi‑channel sales, flexible pack offerings, and hedging exposure with forward alignments where feasible.
- Compliance and food safety: Ensured via robust hygiene practices, pest control, traceability records, and periodic third‑party audits.
- Climate and storage risks: Addressed through improved warehouses, aeration, pallets, fumigation protocols, and insurance coverage.

### Employment & Socio‑Economic Impact
- Direct and indirect jobs in procurement, processing, packing, logistics, and sales, with opportunities for women’s participation in grading, sorting, and packing.
- Higher and more stable farm incomes through collective bargaining, quality‑linked premiums, and reduced intermediation.
- Local value addition curbs distress sales, stimulates allied services, and retains economic value within the producing region.
- Capacity building in quality standards, post‑harvest handling, and enterprise management enhances long‑term competitiveness of member farmers.

### Sustainability
- Resource‑efficient processing through optimized soaking, controlled drying, and well‑tuned milling to reduce breakage and wastage.
- Energy management with efficient motors, lighting, and consideration of renewable options; monitoring of specific consumption and power factor.
- Water stewardship via measured use in conditioning and responsible reuse where feasible.
- By‑product valorization of husk and brokens into feed and biomass supports circularity and reduces disposal burdens.
- Environment, health, and safety systems including dust control, ergonomic workflows, and safe chemical handling for pest management.
