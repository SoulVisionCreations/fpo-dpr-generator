## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Indri Milk Producers FPC Ltd, based in Indri, Karnal, Haryana, proposes a farmer-owned ghee making unit under the PM-FME framework. Ghee, or clarified butter, is the most cherished traditional dairy product in India, celebrated for its pure aroma, rich nutty flavour and culinary versatility. It is made from milk, cream or butter and, when properly prepared, is naturally lactose-free and gluten-free. The proposed unit will leverage local milk availability and organized farmer membership to produce high-quality, branded ghee that meets stringent food safety and purity benchmarks.

### Uses & Benefits
Ghee is an everyday cooking medium, a premium finishing fat, and a vital ingredient in Indian sweets, bakery and festive foods. It is prized for high-heat stability, long shelf life, rich mouthfeel and characteristic flavour release. Households use it for tadka, frying and baking, while sweet makers prefer it for consistency and taste. It is widely used in traditional wellness, religious offerings and celebratory cuisine. Its lactose-free profile makes it suitable for many consumers with milk sensitivities, and convenient sealed packs make storage and transport easy.

### Market Potential
Demand for packaged, branded ghee remains resilient across urban and rural markets, with steady traction in modern retail, kirana, institutional kitchens and e-commerce. Rising preference for safe and traceable foods favours FPO-branded products with farmer provenance and quality assurance. Haryana is a strong dairy belt, offering reliable access to fresh milk and a ready base of institutional buyers such as sweet shops and restaurants. There is ongoing premiumization in the category, with consumers seeking purity, traditional processing cues, and lab-tested claims. Seasonal peaks around festivals further support stable throughput for a well-organized unit.

### Value Chain
The value chain begins with transparent procurement of milk from member farmers, supported by collection points, rapid chilling, and basic quality checks for fat, adulteration and freshness. Within the plant, milk is pasteurized, cooled, homogenized and routed for cream separation and chilling. Butter churning produces butter, which is clarified to ghee, filtered and packed in consumer and bulk formats. Co-products such as skim milk and buttermilk can be channelled to liquid milk sales or value-added lines like curd or paneer, improving overall recovery and farmer returns. Packaging, labelling, warehousing and dispatch complete the chain, supported by batch traceability and documented food safety protocols. The system emphasizes real-time testing, hygienic handling and clear farmer payment mechanisms.

### Competition & Marketing
The unit will compete with national brands, cooperative dairies, regional players and unorganized loose ghee. Differentiation will come from purity, consistent flavour, reliable lab validation, AGMARK and FSSAI compliance, and farm-to-pack traceability. A multi-channel approach is planned:
- General trade through distributors, milk booths and kirana stores
- Food service supply to sweet shops, bakeries, restaurants and caterers
- Modern retail and e-commerce, including subscription-led repeat purchases
- Festival-focused gift packs and on-ground sampling
- Story-led branding that emphasizes farmer ownership, local sourcing and quality control
Distinctive packaging in pouches, jars and tins will target both household and institutional users, supported by recipe content, chef partnerships and community outreach.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw milk variability, price swings and seasonal fluctuations: address through membership engagement, transparent pricing, extension services on animal nutrition, and pre-agreed supply schedules.
- Quality and food safety risks: mitigate with validated SOPs, HACCP-based systems, calibrated testing equipment, AGMARK grading and periodic third-party audits.
- Operational downtime and utilities interruptions: reduce via preventive maintenance, spares planning, trained operators, and backup power and steam arrangements.
- Market competition and price pressure: respond with clear positioning on purity and provenance, pack-size strategy, channel mix, and disciplined trade schemes.
- Compliance, licensing and claims risk: ensure timely regulatory renewals, accurate label declarations, and robust documentation.
- Working capital strain: maintain prudent credit terms, inventory norms, and efficient receivables management supported by banking limits.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project strengthens member incomes through assured offtake and value addition close to the farm gate. It enables local employment in procurement, processing, quality control, packaging, logistics and sales. Women’s participation can be enhanced in collection, testing, packaging and micro-distribution. Structured training on clean milk production, animal health and financial literacy deepens capabilities within the community. Surpluses generated at the FPO level can be reinvested in chilling, extension services and shared infrastructure, creating a virtuous cycle of growth.

### Sustainability
Sustainability measures include energy-efficient boilers and ghee kettles, heat recovery from hot streams, and progressive adoption of solar power. Water stewardship through CIP optimization and rinse-water reuse helps reduce consumption. Food-grade, recyclable packaging is prioritized with clear disposal guidance. Co-product valorization and responsible waste management limit environmental footprint. On-farm interventions such as balanced ration, clean milking and manure management further support a lower-impact dairy value chain.
