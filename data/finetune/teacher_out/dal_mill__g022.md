## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report proposes a gram-based dal mill under the Farmer Producer Organization model for Dahod Gram Growers Producer Company Ltd., Limkheda, Dahod, Gujarat. The enterprise focuses on producing dal from chickpea (chana/gram) through de-husking and splitting of whole pulses. Dal is a staple in the Indian vegetarian diet, valued as a primary protein source and recognized for its fibre, iron, and B‑vitamin content. Locating the unit in a pulse-growing rural or semi-urban belt strengthens raw material access, lowers logistics costs, and anchors the FPO’s farm-to-market linkage.

The core process involves procurement and storage of whole gram from member farmers, followed by soaking, drying, staged cleaning, de-husking in emery-roll machines, separation of husk and grits, splitting, polishing, and packing for dispatch. The FPO-led structure enables traceable quality, farmer-centric pricing, and collective marketing.

### Uses & Benefits
- Dietary staple across cuisines: used for daily cooking, flours, snacks, ready mixes, and institutional kitchens.
- Nutritional advantages: high plant protein with dietary fibre and micronutrients; supports balanced vegetarian diets.
- Culinary functionality: quick-cooking split pulses with desirable texture, colour, and aroma; versatile across recipes.
- FPO advantages: assured procurement channel for farmers, stable offtake for buyers, and scope for value-added grades and polishes.
- Supply reliability: near-farm processing ensures freshness, quality consistency, and reduced post-harvest losses.

### Market Potential
Demand is driven by steady household consumption, growing urban markets, health-conscious preferences, and a shift toward packaged, hygiene-certified foods. Institutional buyers such as hostels, mid-day meal programs, food processors, and retailers prefer reliable quality and traceability—key strengths of an FPO. Integration with e-commerce and modern retail widens reach beyond local mandis. Seasonality in arrivals can be balanced through planned procurement and storage, enabling continuous supplies and stable quality.

### Value Chain
- Farm production: member farmers cultivate gram using recommended practices, supported by the FPO’s input and advisory services.
- Aggregation and procurement: produce is pooled at village collection points with preliminary grading and moisture checks.
- Primary processing: soaking, drying, cleaning, and de-husking using standardized parameters for optimal yield and minimal breakage.
- Secondary processing: splitting and polishing to achieve uniform size, colour, and shine as per market preferences.
- Quality control: sampling at each stage for moisture, impurities, hardness, and finish; adherence to FSSAI norms and food safety systems.
- Packing and storage: robust sack or retail pouches with labelling, batch codes, and traceability; safe, ventilated warehousing.
- Marketing and distribution: direct B2B sales, institutional contracts, retailer tie-ups, online marketplaces, and local wholesale networks.
- By-product utilization: husk and brokens monetized as cattle feed or fuel, improving overall economics and reducing waste.

### Competition & Marketing
The market features local mills, regional brands, and national players. Differentiation for the FPO will come from:
- Consistent quality standards and calibrated polishing aligned with regional taste.
- Farm-traceable sourcing and transparent procurement practices.
- Graded SKUs for retail and bulk, with sturdy packaging that preserves freshness.
- Competitive logistics from a near-farm location and responsive order fulfillment.
- Multi-channel sales: wholesale mandis, kirana networks, institutional tenders, and digital platforms.
Brand building will emphasize purity, traceability, farmer welfare, and food safety certifications. Sampling, trade influencer outreach, and steady service levels can strengthen repeat demand.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: mitigate through staggered procurement, hedging via forward agreements with members, and buffer stocks within safe storage norms.
- Quality variability: introduce moisture targets, pre-cleaning at collection points, and process controls to limit breakage and maintain yield.
- Operational downtime: ensure preventive maintenance, operator training, and spare-parts management with vendor support.
- Market price pressure: focus on efficiency, by-product recovery, and graded product mix to sustain margins.
- Credit risk: diversify buyers, enforce prudent credit terms, and use trade credit insurance or secured advance mechanisms where feasible.
- Compliance and food safety: adopt SOPs, periodic audits, and certifications; maintain batch-wise records for full traceability.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The dal mill creates local jobs in operations, quality, logistics, and administration, with potential for women’s participation in grading, packing, and documentation. Members benefit from assured offtake, more predictable prices, and access to input services and advisory. Local value addition increases farm-gate realization, keeps margins within the community, and stimulates ancillary services such as transport, warehousing, and maintenance.

### Sustainability
Resource-efficient processing is integral: energy-efficient motors, feasible renewable energy options, and optimized drying methods. Water use in soaking can be managed with reuse protocols and safe discharge practices. Husk and brokens are valorized as feed or fuel, minimizing waste. Cleanliness, pest-proof storage, and compliance with food safety standards enhance product longevity and reduce losses, aligning commercial success with environmental stewardship.
