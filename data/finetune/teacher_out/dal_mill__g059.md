## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents a gram-based dal milling enterprise for Kongu Dalhan Producer Company Limited, Kurumbapalayam, Annur, Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu. The unit will mill whole chickpea into dal through a sequence of soaking, drying, cleaning, de-husking, splitting, polishing, and packing operations. Locating the mill within a pulse-growing belt enables efficient raw material aggregation from member farmers, seamless logistics, and lower in-transit losses. The product aligns with regional dietary preferences and the FPO’s mission to add value at the farm gate.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a staple protein source in the Indian vegetarian diet. Gram dal offers balanced nutrition with protein, dietary fibre, essential minerals, and B‑vitamins. It is versatile in home cooking, institutional kitchens, and food service, and serves as an ingredient for snacks and flours. For farmer members, local processing ensures transparent grading, reduced distress sales, and better realization. For consumers, FPO-branded dal provides freshness, traceability, and consistent cooking quality.

### Market Potential
Pulses enjoy sustained demand across regions and income segments, supported by stable household consumption and growing health consciousness. Urban retail expansion, institutional catering, and e-commerce are reinforcing demand for standardized, hygienically packed dal. Premium niches are emerging for unpolished variants, origin-linked branding, and traceable farmer-sourced products. The Coimbatore region is well connected to major urban markets, enabling the FPO to serve wholesale, retail, and institutional channels with reliable supply.

### Value Chain
The value chain spans farm-level aggregation of chickpea, scientific storage, and staged processing up to consumer-ready packs. Member farmers supply graded whole grains that are soaked and dried to condition the seed coat, then cleaned, de-husked in emery roll machines, split, polished as per market preference, and packed for dispatch. Husk and brokens are captured as by-products for sale to feed and allied sectors, improving overall recovery value. Quality control at each stage, coupled with proper warehousing and moisture management, ensures shelf-stable, uniform dal.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition includes local traders, unorganized mills, and national brands. The FPO can differentiate through farmer-first sourcing, transparent quality norms, minimal handling losses, and fresh milling close to harvest. Branding will emphasize origin, purity, and cooking performance, supported by clean packaging and batch traceability. A multi-channel approach covers wholesalers in regional mandis, kirana and modern retail, institutions, and digital marketplaces. Seasonal outreach, recipe education, and community sampling will reinforce brand recall.

### Risk & Mitigation
Key risks include raw material price and availability volatility, moisture-related storage losses, process variability, equipment downtime, power interruptions, working capital constraints, and food safety non-compliance. Mitigation strategies include advance procurement plans with member farmer tie-ups, scientific warehousing with aeration and pest management, documented standard operating procedures for soaking, drying, and de-husking, annual maintenance contracts and critical spares, reliable power backup and energy management, diversified sales contracts, credit insurance where feasible, and rigorous adoption of FSSAI-compliant GMP and HACCP principles.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit will create steady employment for operators, technicians, supervisors, storekeepers, quality staff, and sales personnel, with additional opportunities in logistics, maintenance, and packaging supply. Women’s participation in grading, packing, and quality functions will be prioritized. The model strengthens local incomes by reducing intermediaries, enabling better price discovery, and sharing value addition through patronage benefits. Skills training and youth engagement help retain talent in rural and semi-urban areas, fostering an inclusive local growth cycle.

### Sustainability
Sustainability anchors the project through efficient water use in soaking, clean handling to minimize losses, and utilization of husk and brokens as valuable by-products. Dust extraction and housekeeping protect worker health and product safety. Energy-efficient drives and consideration of rooftop solar help manage operating costs and emissions. Fire safety, rainwater harvesting where feasible, and compliance with environmental norms ensure responsible operations. Continuous quality improvement reduces waste and enhances consumer confidence.
