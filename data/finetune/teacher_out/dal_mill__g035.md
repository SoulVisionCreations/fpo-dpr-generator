## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents the establishment of a gram-based dal milling unit under Karanja Arhar Dal Producer Company Ltd at Karanja (Ghadge), Wardha, Maharashtra. The unit will process whole chickpea into dal through a structured de-husking and splitting operation, delivering clean, polished, and packaged split pulses suitable for retail and institutional markets. Pulses are central to Indian diets, providing affordable plant protein along with fibre, iron, and B vitamins. Locating the mill within a pulse-growing belt ensures close access to raw material, farmer participation, and reduced logistics costs.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a daily staple for households and community kitchens, and a dependable ingredient for food service establishments. Benefits include:
- Nutritional adequacy for vegetarian and flexitarian diets.
- Wide culinary adaptability across cuisines and formats.
- Longer shelf life and ease of storage when properly milled and packed.
- Convenience for consumers and caterers due to consistent cooking quality.
For the FPO and its members, dal milling converts farm produce into value-added, branded products, increasing farmgate realization, improving price discovery, and creating local employment.

### Market Potential
The market for packaged dal is robust across rural, semi-urban, and urban segments, supported by rising health awareness, preference for hygienic and traceable foods, and expanding modern retail and e-commerce channels. Gram-based dal enjoys steady demand throughout the year, with seasonality on the supply side rather than consumption. Vidarbha’s pulse basket and proximity to consuming centers offer a strategic edge in procurement and distribution. There is scope to serve both retail consumers and institutional buyers, alongside opportunities for private-label partnerships.

### Value Chain
- Farm-level production by member and allied farmers, with aggregation at the FPO level.
- Transparent procurement, moisture and quality assessment, and warehousing with standard operating procedures for storage hygiene.
- Primary processing including soaking, drying, cleaning, de-husking, splitting, grading, polishing, and packaging.
- Distribution to wholesalers, kirana stores, modern trade, institutional kitchens, and regional e-commerce partners.
- Utilization of by-products such as husk, brokens, and bran for feed, snacks, and biomass, enhancing overall value realization.
- Digital traceability and batch-wise quality control to maintain consistent standards.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition includes local unorganized mills, regional brands, and national packaged food companies. The FPO’s differentiated advantage lies in farm-level backward linkage, traceability, fresher product cycles, and community trust. The marketing approach will emphasize:
- A clean, credible brand identity highlighting farmer ownership and origin.
- Attractive, food-grade, tamper-evident packaging with clear cooking instructions.
- Multi-channel distribution across traditional trade, modern retail, institutions, and online marketplaces.
- Quality certifications, food safety compliance, and periodic lab testing to reinforce trust.
- Consumer outreach via in-store sampling, social media storytelling, and nutrition-focused communication.
- Seasonal procurement campaigns to lock in quality and stabilize costs.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility and climate risks: forward purchase planning, staggered procurement, and diversified sourcing within the pulse belt.
- Quality variation and storage losses: strict grading protocols, improved aeration, palletization, fumigation, and moisture control.
- Operational downtime: annual maintenance contracts, spares inventory, and operator training for critical equipment.
- Energy and utility interruptions: backup power, energy-efficient drives, and load management during peak operations.
- Regulatory and food safety compliance: documented SOPs, periodic audits, and staff training in hygiene and sanitation.
- Market and credit risk: diversified customer base, credit checks for institutional buyers, and measured exposure.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project catalyzes local non-farm employment across operations, maintenance, quality assurance, packaging, logistics, and sales. It enables inclusive participation of women and youth, upskills member-farmers on quality and post-harvest practices, and strengthens local service ecosystems. By aggregating produce and value-adding within the community, the initiative improves price realization, reduces distress sales, and contributes to rural resilience.

### Sustainability
Sustainability is embedded through:
- Efficient water use and reuse for soaking and cleaning, with appropriate treatment and safe disposal protocols.
- Dust suppression and air filtration to protect workers and product quality.
- Energy-efficient motors and drives, with a pathway to integrate renewable power.
- Conversion of husk and other by-products into useful inputs, minimizing waste.
- Environmentally considerate packaging options and responsible waste management.
- Continuous training on safety, hygiene, and resource efficiency.
