## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Chikhaldara Girivan Dairy FPC Ltd proposes a ghee making unit at Chikhaldara, Amravati, Maharashtra. Ghee, or clarified butter, is a revered traditional dairy product with a rich nutty aroma and granular texture. It is derived from milk, cream, or butter, and is naturally free from lactose and gluten when processed under hygienic conditions. The proposed unit leverages a farmer-owned model to aggregate quality milk, process it into premium ghee, and market the product under a traceable FPO brand.

The production process is a well-established dairy sequence: procurement of raw milk, pasteurization, cooling, homogenization as required, cream separation, chilling, butter churning, clarification to ghee, and packaging. The only raw material is milk, readily available from member farmers and nearby dairying communities, complemented by utilities, packaging, and approved food-grade inputs.

### Uses & Benefits
- Culinary use in Indian cooking, sweets, baking, frying, and tempering due to its stable flavor and high thermal tolerance.
- Traditional and wellness-oriented use in Ayurveda-inspired diets, rituals, and functional foods.
- Suitable for consumers seeking dairy fat without lactose, aligning with specific dietary needs.
- Longer ambient stability compared to table butter when processed and packaged as per good manufacturing practices.
- Adds significant value to member milk, enabling better price realization and stable offtake for farmers.

### Market Potential
Ghee enjoys pan-India acceptance across households, sweet makers, restaurants, institutional kitchens, and emerging online gourmet channels. Demand is propelled by culinary traditions, premiumization trends, festive consumption, and rising preference for authentic, traceable dairy fats. Urban retail, semi-urban groceries, modern trade, HORECA, sweet and snack manufacturers, and e-commerce offer multiple routes to market. A farmer-owned, origin-linked brand from Chikhaldara can position on freshness, authenticity, and quality assurance.

### Value Chain
- Milk sourcing: Daily pooling from member farmers with lactometer checks, antibiotic screening, and fat/SNF testing at collection points. The FPO’s direct relationships support consistent quality and ethical sourcing.
- Cold chain: Village-level chilling or rapid transit to the processing unit safeguards raw milk integrity.
- Processing: Pasteurization and controlled cream separation, followed by butter churning and careful clarification to achieve desired flavor notes and granulation.
- By-products: Skim milk, buttermilk, and ghee residue can be channelled to cattle feed, beverages, or allied dairy lines, enhancing overall value realization.
- Packaging and storage: Food-grade, aroma-protective packs such as pouches, jars, or tins, with batch coding and traceability.
- Sales and distribution: Hybrid model covering wholesale, retail, institutional, and direct-to-consumer channels, supported by local distributors and digital outreach.
- Quality and compliance: FSSAI licensing, AGMARK for ghee, documented SOPs, GMP and HACCP-aligned practices, and periodic lab testing build trust and repeat demand.

### Competition & Marketing
The market hosts national dairy brands, regional cooperatives, and local artisanal producers. Differentiation levers for the FPO include:
- Provenance and traceability to member farms.
- Consistent flavor profile and purity verified through independent labs.
- Transparent labelling, AGMARK certification for ghee, and strong food safety systems.
- Focused segmentation for households, premium sweet shops, temple trusts, HORECA, and e-commerce audiences.
- Story-led marketing that highlights farmer ownership, fair pricing, and community impact, reinforced by attractive packaging and customer education on storage and usage.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw milk variability: Tackle through member training, clean milk production practices, chilling, and strict intake QC protocols.
- Input price fluctuations: Use seasonal contracting, diversified catchment sourcing, and dynamic pricing policies.
- Food safety and compliance: Implement GMP and HACCP-aligned SOPs, maintain a capable in-house lab, undertake third-party audits, and ensure recall readiness.
- Energy and utility reliability: Provide adequate backup, preventive maintenance, and efficiency upgrades to manage costs and uptime.
- Market competition and channel risk: Pursue a balanced channel mix, sustained brand building, and periodic product-line refresh anchored in consumer insights.
- Packaging supply continuity: Qualify multiple suppliers and maintain safety stocks where feasible.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit catalyzes local employment across milk collection, processing, maintenance, logistics, sales, and administration, with strong opportunities for women’s participation in quality control and packing. Stable offtake and fair procurement practices support farmer incomes, while dividends and patronage-based benefits strengthen the FPO model. Allied services such as transport, input supplies, equipment servicing, and retail distribution further stimulate the local economy.

### Sustainability
- Resource efficiency through heat recovery, judicious water use, and CIP optimization.
- Renewable and cleaner energy options such as solar thermal support lower emissions.
- By-product utilization of skim milk, buttermilk, and ghee residue reduces waste.
- Responsible packaging with recyclability considerations and improved material efficiency.
- Wastewater treatment and reuse for non-process applications, and safe handling of boiler emissions and solid waste in line with regulations.
- Continuous farmer training on climate-smart dairying and animal nutrition for resilient supply.
