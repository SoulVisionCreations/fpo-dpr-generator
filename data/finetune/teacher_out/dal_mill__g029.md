## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Kaliganj Pulse Growers Producer Company Ltd, Debagram, Kaliganj, Nadia, West Bengal proposes a modern dal milling unit to process gram into high-quality dal. Dal is produced by de-husking and splitting whole pulses and then polishing and packing for retail and institutional markets. Pulses are central to Indian diets, especially for vegetarian households, and are valued for protein, dietary fibre, iron and B‑vitamins. Establishing the facility within a pulse-growing belt enables efficient aggregation from member farmers, lower logistic costs, better quality control at intake, and improved traceability.

### Uses & Benefits
- Staple food ingredient across households and food service, suited for daily cooking, traditional recipes, ready-to-cook blends, and fortified products.
- Nutritional advantages from plant-based protein, fibre and micronutrients support balanced diets and food security.
- Benefits to farmers through assured procurement, better price realization, reduced post-harvest losses and value addition within the community.
- By-products such as husk and brokens find ready use in feed and allied sectors, adding revenue streams and minimizing waste.
- Locally milled dal shortens the supply chain, enabling freshness, consistent cooking quality, and price stability for consumers.

### Market Potential
- Demand for pulses is resilient due to dietary habits, rising health awareness, and preference for minimally processed, traceable foods.
- Urban retail, traditional wholesale mandis, emerging e‑commerce channels, and institutional segments such as hostels, canteens, and community kitchens provide diversified market avenues.
- Government nutrition and social protection programs, alongside state procurement and development initiatives, create additional institutional demand.
- West Bengal and adjoining states present a strong consumption base, while sourcing from member farmers stabilizes supply and ensures year-round availability with suitable storage.

### Value Chain
- Upstream: Aggregation of whole gram from member farmers, supported by quality grading, moisture testing, and fair procurement practices.
- Primary processing: Intake and storage, soaking, drying, cleaning using reel and air classification, de-husking in emery roll systems, husk removal through aspiration and fine cleaning, splitting in lentil splitters, polishing, grading and final packing.
- By-product management: Husk and brokens are collected separately and channelled to feed and allied users, generating additional value.
- Downstream: Distribution through wholesale partners, retailers, institutional buyers, and direct-to-consumer channels. Branding, standardized quality, and reliable supply underpin long-term relationships.
- Enablers: Testing and quality assurance systems, standard operating procedures, food safety and hygiene practices, and digital farmer traceability enhance confidence across the chain.

### Competition & Marketing
- Competitive landscape includes local mills, regional brands, and national packaged food companies. Differentiation is achieved through consistent recovery, low foreign matter, uniform grains, and dependable cooking performance.
- The FPO will position its dal as farmer-sourced, traceable, and quality-assured, with clear labelling, hygienic packaging, and transparent moisture and grade specifications.
- Marketing channels include wholesale distributors, kirana and modern retail, food service buyers, and digital platforms. Participation in local haats and fairs, tie-ups with self-help groups, and institutional tenders will broaden reach.
- Certifications for food safety and quality, adherence to regulatory norms, and batch-wise testing will reinforce brand trust. Seasonal promotions and community nutrition campaigns will build local brand loyalty.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price volatility: Mitigated through staggered procurement, warehouse receipt-backed storage, and supply agreements with member producers.
- Quality variability and moisture risk: Managed by standardized intake testing, conditioned soaking and drying protocols, and covered storage with pest control.
- Process and equipment downtime: Reduced through preventive maintenance, adequate spares, and annual maintenance contracts for critical machines.
- Working capital strain: Addressed via disciplined inventory and receivables cycles, credit control, and appropriate banking limits.
- Market competition and price pressure: Countered by strong branding, quality differentiation, and diversified channel mix.
- Climate and logistics disruptions: Buffer stocks, local sourcing, and flexible dispatch planning sustain continuity.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
- The unit will create direct jobs in operations, quality control, packing, stores, and sales, and indirect livelihoods in transport, packaging, and allied services.
- Inclusive employment opportunities for women in sorting, grading, and packing will be prioritized.
- Member farmers benefit from stable offtake, capacity building on post-harvest handling, and potential patronage-based returns, strengthening the FPO ecosystem.
- Localized value addition keeps margins within the community, stimulating the rural economy.

### Sustainability
- Efficient water use in soaking, with reuse and controlled discharge; dust and particulates managed through aspiration and filters.
- Energy-efficient motors and drives, consideration of renewable power options, and optimized dryer operations to reduce energy intensity.
- Husk and other by-products are valorized for feed or fuel, minimizing waste to landfill.
- Preference for recyclable or biodegradable packaging where market-acceptable, and supplier engagement on extended producer responsibility.
- Strong food safety culture, personal protective equipment, and safe material handling protect workers and product integrity.
