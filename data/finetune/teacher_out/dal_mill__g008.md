## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is the split form of pulses produced by de-husking and splitting whole grains such as chickpea (chana). It is the staple protein for vegetarian households across India and is valued for its fibre, iron, and B‑vitamins. This DPR proposes a gram-based dal milling unit for Surajpur Gram Dal Producer Company Ltd at Pratappur, Surajpur, Chhattisgarh, leveraging proximity to pulse-growing belts and the collective strength of an FPO to secure raw material directly from member farmers. The processing sequence includes procurement and storage, soaking, drying, cleaning, de‑husking, splitting, polishing, and packing, aligned to good manufacturing practices and food safety norms.

### Uses & Benefits
- Staple protein source in everyday cooking across households, community kitchens, and the HoReCa channel.
- Versatile ingredient for dals, khichdi, snacks, and value-added foods, with by-products suitable for feed or flour applications.
- Nutritional benefits include plant-based protein, dietary fibre, and essential micronutrients, supporting balanced diets and health-conscious consumption.
- Localized processing enhances freshness, reduces post-harvest losses, and improves price realisation for farmers through aggregation and transparent grading.
- The FPO model improves bargaining power, lowers transaction costs, and creates stable market linkages for members.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal remains resilient due to entrenched consumption habits, rising health awareness, and urbanisation-led growth in packaged staples. Gram dal enjoys strong acceptance in central and northern markets, with steady institutional demand from boarding facilities, restaurants, food processors, and public programs. Policy emphasis on pulse self-sufficiency, better storage infrastructure, and branding support under PM-FME strengthen prospects for a professionally managed FPO-led milling unit. The proposed location in Chhattisgarh enables access to raw material clusters and nearby demand centres, balancing farm-gate procurement with efficient distribution.

### Value Chain
- Farm aggregation: The FPO pools chickpea from member farmers using transparent weighment and moisture-based quality checks, encouraging primary cleaning and good storage practices at village level.
- Primary handling and storage: Scientific storage with pallets, aeration, and pest management safeguards quality. Grading and lot formation ensure consistent input to the mill.
- Processing: Soaking softens husk; drying is achieved through mechanical air dryers or sun-drying; cleaning removes grit and foreign matter; emery roll de‑huskers loosen and remove husk; reel machines separate fine impurities; splitting converts kernels into uniform dal; polishing is applied as per market preference for polished or unpolished variants.
- Quality assurance: In‑process checks for moisture, foreign matter, defectives, and uniformity; finished product testing for appearance, cooking quality, and sensory attributes; compliance with food safety regulations.
- Packaging and dispatch: Retail and bulk packs with batch coding, tamper-evident sealing, and clear labelling; dispatch through distributors, institutional buyers, modern trade, and e‑commerce.
- By‑products: Husk and brokens channelled to feed, flour, or energy applications, improving overall recovery and revenue realisation.
- Traceability: Member-wise procurement records and lot tracking strengthen brand assurance and facilitate certifications and buyer audits.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition spans branded national players, established regional mills, and unorganised local players. The FPO can differentiate through farmer-traceable sourcing, consistent grading, hygienic processing, and the option of unpolished dal for health-oriented consumers. A balanced channel mix across wholesalers, retailers, institutional buyers, and digital platforms reduces risk. Brand identity can highlight origin, quality assurance, and fair value to farmers. Structured trade terms, dependable order fulfilment, and after-sales engagement with key accounts help build loyalty. Participation in government procurement and community programs provides volume stability alongside consumer-pack growth.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: Staggered procurement, storage readiness, and supplier diversification mitigate price and quality swings.
- Quality risk: Robust SOPs for cleaning, moisture control, and grading; frequent in‑process checks; trained staff.
- Equipment downtime: Preventive maintenance, critical spares, and annual maintenance contracts preserve uptime.
- Power and utilities: Back‑up arrangements and energy-efficient equipment safeguard continuity and cost control.
- Working capital strain: Prudent inventory and receivables management, insurance, and well-structured banking limits ensure liquidity.
- Market risk: Diversified channels, flexible product mix (polished and unpolished), and customer service reduce dependency on any single segment.
- Compliance and safety: Food safety systems, documented hygiene practices, and periodic audits minimise regulatory and reputation risks.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit will generate steady jobs in operations, quality, warehousing, logistics, and sales, with inclusive opportunities for local youth and women. Farmers benefit from assured offtake, transparent pricing, and patronage-based returns. Capacity building in post-harvest handling and quality upgrades leads to higher incomes. Local value addition keeps margins within the community, catalysing allied micro-enterprises in transport, packaging, and services.

### Sustainability
Resource-efficient processing with controlled water use during soaking, energy-conscious drying, and dust collection improves environmental outcomes. Husk and brokens are channelled into productive uses, reducing waste. Adoption of renewable energy options, good housekeeping, and responsible packaging choices lower the footprint. Occupational safety practices and community engagement promote long-term resilience for the enterprise and its members.
