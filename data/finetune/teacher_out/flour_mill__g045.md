## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Shajapur Sharbati Wheat Producer Company Ltd, based at Sundersi, Shujalpur, Shajapur, Madhya Pradesh, proposes an integrated wheat flour (atta) milling and packaging unit under the PM-FME framework. Wheat flour milled from cleaned, tempered wheat grain is a staple across Indian households. Organised, hygienically packed atta has been steadily displacing traditional home or local chakki grinding due to convenience, consistency, and better food safety. The FPO will leverage proximity to premium Sharbati wheat belts, farmer linkages, and collective scale to deliver a reliable, branded staple with traceable origin.

The process flow comprises thorough cleaning via vibro separation, destoning, gravity separation, conditioning or tempering, calibrated milling, sieving or grading, and form-fill-seal packaging into consumer and bulk formats. By-products such as bran are collected and sold to feed markets, enhancing overall conversion value.

### Uses & Benefits
- Daily staple for chapati, phulka, paratha, poori, and allied culinary uses across households, food service, institutions, and small bakeries.
- Consistent granulation and moisture control support better dough handling, puffing, and softness.
- Hygienic, sealed packs reduce contamination risk and extend shelf life compared to loose flour.
- Traceable farmer-origin branding and quality assurance create trust and repeat purchase.
- Value to members through assured procurement, logistics efficiency, and market access, translating into improved realisation at the farm gate.

### Market Potential
Organised atta has transitioned from a convenience niche to mainstream pantry essential in both urban and rural markets. Demand is underpinned by changing lifestyles, growing preference for branded staples, and retail penetration through general trade, modern formats, and e-commerce. Sharbati-origin positioning commands strong acceptance due to perceived taste and softness advantages. Institutional consumption through tiffin services, canteens, caterers, and quick-service outlets provides additional volume stability. With reliable inputs and efficient conversion, the category sustains steady offtake across seasons.

### Value Chain
- Farm sourcing: Aggregation from member farmers and nearby mandis ensures reliable supply, competitive procurement, and traceability.
- Primary handling: Scientific storage, aeration, and fumigation maintain grain integrity and reduce infestation or moisture swings.
- Cleaning and conditioning: Multi-stage removal of impurities, stones, and light matter, followed by tempering to optimise bran separation and flour yield.
- Milling and sifting: Calibrated roller milling and sieving achieve the desired granulation and extraction, with bran captured as a saleable by-product.
- Quality control: In-process checks for moisture, ash, granulation, and microbiological safety maintain consistent product standards.
- Packing and dispatch: Form-fill-seal in food-grade laminate for retail and bulk formats for B2B channels, with barcoding and batch traceability.
- Sales and distribution: Hybrid route-to-market via local wholesalers, retail networks, institutional contracts, and digital commerce, supported by brand communication.
- Feedback loop: Market insights and lab analytics inform continuous improvement in conditioning, granulation, and pack design.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition spans national brands, regional mills, and local chakki operators. Differentiation for the FPO brand will rest on origin-led storytelling, consistent softness and taste, hygienic packs, and transparent quality parameters. Key strategies include:
- A clear brand identity highlighting Sharbati origin, farmer ownership, and food safety credentials.
- Focused distribution in surrounding districts and adjacent urban clusters, expanding through channel partners with stable service levels.
- Consumer sampling at retail and community events, co-branded promotional tie-ups with kirana stores, and digital outreach.
- Distinct SKUs for consumer pantry, food service, and institutional kitchens, supported by on-time supply and technical service.
- Co-products marketing of bran to feed manufacturers and dairies to maximise overall plant contribution.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price swings: Seasonal procurement planning, staggered stocking, and forward supply arrangements with members help stabilise input costs.
- Quality variability: Standardised grading at intake, tempering controls, and continuous lab testing preserve consistency in granulation and dough performance.
- Operational downtime: Preventive maintenance programs, spares planning, and trained operators sustain plant uptime.
- Food safety incidents: HACCP-based controls, pest management, hygienic design, and robust sanitation reduce contamination risks; full traceability aids rapid response.
- Market competition: Origin-based branding, reliable service, and distinct pack propositions mitigate price-led rivalry.
- Working capital strain: Structured credit lines aligned to procurement cycles, prudent receivables terms, and inventory turns protect liquidity.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project generates skilled and semi-skilled employment in operations, maintenance, quality, packing, logistics, and sales. It opens avenues for women’s participation in quality and packaging. As a farmer-led enterprise, the FPO strengthens producer bargaining power, reduces post-harvest losses through scientific handling, and channels more value back to the community. Regular agronomy and post-harvest training improve grain quality and farm incomes. Localised procurement and distribution shorten supply chains and stimulate allied services.

### Sustainability
Wheat milling is inherently resource-efficient and, with good housekeeping, exhibits low specific water use limited to tempering and sanitation. Energy conservation through efficient motors, dust control, and heat recovery enhances performance. Dust collection and bag filters safeguard worker health and the environment. Bran valorisation minimises waste, while recyclable packaging and adherence to EPR norms support circularity. Responsible storage fumigation and integrated pest management reduce chemical loads, and rooftop solar or green power options can progressively decarbonise operations.
