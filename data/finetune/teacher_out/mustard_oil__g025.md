## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Groundnut oil, also known as peanut oil, is an edible vegetable oil extracted from mature groundnut kernels. It is valued for its pleasant aroma, clean taste, and a naturally high smoke tolerance that makes it suitable for a wide range of Indian cooking styles. When produced through mechanical expeller pressing followed by filtration, the oil retains characteristic nutty notes and desirable micronutrients, aligning with consumer preferences for minimally processed, chemical-free edible oils. For Keshkal Groundnut Growers Producer Company Ltd in Kondagaon, Chhattisgarh, the venture converts member-grown groundnuts into a branded, traceable, value-added product with assured quality and local identity.

### Uses & Benefits
- Culinary applications across household kitchens, eateries, and snack manufacturers due to its stability in frying, sautéing, and tempering.
- Favorable fatty acid profile and natural antioxidants that support heart-friendly positioning and shelf stability.
- Distinct sensory attributes that enhance traditional recipes and regionally popular snacks.
- Compatibility with spice blends and condiments, making it a preferred medium for pickling and seasoning.
- Non-food applications such as artisanal soaps and traditional remedies in limited, niche markets.

### Raw Material
The primary raw material is clean, mature groundnut kernels sourced from member farmers. Consistent varietal selection, proper post-harvest handling, and careful drying are vital to achieve desired oil flavor, oxidative stability, and safe storage. Procurement protocols emphasize:
- Field-level training on harvesting at physiological maturity and safe, hygienic drying.
- Cleaning and grading to remove stones, shells, splits, and discolored kernels.
- Moisture management and aflatoxin risk control through tarpaulin drying, covered storage, and rapid turnaround.
- Farmer aggregation to create uniform lots for efficient expeller pressing and predictable yield.
Auxiliary inputs include food-grade packaging materials, labels, cartons, and consumables for filtration and hygiene.

### Market Potential
Demand for groundnut oil is sustained by cultural preference, taste, and its suitability for Indian cuisines. Consumers are increasingly seeking traceable, minimally processed oils, creating an opening for FPO-branded offerings. In Chhattisgarh and adjoining markets, groundnut oil competes favorably with refined blended oils when positioned on purity, taste, and local provenance. Institutions, snack makers, sweet shops, and restaurants constitute attractive bulk buyers, while retail consumers prefer sealed bottles and pouches. The by-product oil cake has strong traction with dairy farmers and feed manufacturers, ensuring near-complete utilization of processed seed and diversified revenue streams.

### Value Chain
The farm-to-fork chain integrates primary production, collective procurement, processing, and market linkage:
- Aggregation: Member farmers bring dried, cleaned groundnuts to the FPO collection centers with lot-wise traceability.
- Pre-processing: Sun drying to safe moisture, cleaning to remove foreign matter, and grading for uniformity.
- Seed heating: Gentle conditioning to enhance oil release, flavor, and press efficiency while preserving cake quality.
- Oil extraction: Mechanical expeller pressing to obtain crude groundnut oil and de-oiled cake.
- Filtration: Multi-stage filter press to deliver clear, bright oil without chemical refining.
- Packaging: Filling into food-grade bottles, pouches, and tins with tamper-evident closures and compliant labeling.
- By-product utilization: Press cake marketed as an ingredient for cattle feed formulations.
- Distribution: Direct supply to retailers, wholesalers, institutions, and consumers through online and offline channels.

### Competition & Marketing
The category features national refined-oil brands, regional expeller-pressed labels, and premium cold-pressed entrants. The FPO can differentiate through:
- Provenance: Farm-traceable oil cultivated and processed by local growers.
- Purity and process: Expeller-pressed, non-solvent method with transparent quality controls.
- Freshness: Short, controlled supply chain with frequent batch rotation.
- Affordability with quality: Optimized logistics and producer ownership keeping margins within the ecosystem.
Marketing levers include distinctive regional branding, AGMARK and FSSAI compliance, QR-based traceability stories, chef and influencer tie-ups, in-store sampling, festivals and haats, and collaboration with SHGs, cooperatives, and institutional kitchens.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: Addressed through staggered procurement, scientific storage, and minimum support arrangements within member networks.
- Quality and food safety: Aflatoxin control with strict lot testing, moisture management, supplier training, and documented HACCP-aligned SOPs.
- Operations and machinery: Preventive maintenance, availability of spares, trained operators, and service contracts.
- Power reliability: Backup solutions and energy-efficient equipment to maintain uptime.
- Market and price risk: Multi-channel sales, product variety in pack formats, and brand building to reduce dependency on any single segment.
- Working capital gaps: Structured receivables, inventory discipline, and credit lines aligned to seasonal procurement.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit generates local jobs in aggregation, grading, processing, packaging, logistics, and sales, with strong inclusion potential for women and youth. Farmers benefit from assured offtake, better realization through value addition, transparent weighment and quality-linked premiums, and patronage dividends. The oil cake strengthens the local dairy economy, while skill-building and governance systems enhance the FPO’s institutional capacity and community resilience.

### Sustainability
The model emphasizes near-zero waste by fully utilizing oil and cake, encourages regenerative agronomy through training, and advances energy-conscious operations with options like solar-assisted drying and efficient motors. Food-safe cleaning agents, water stewardship, and responsible packaging choices such as recyclable bottles and returnable bulk tins reinforce environmental commitments. Localized procurement and marketing reduce transport emissions, while traceability fosters accountability and continuous improvement.
