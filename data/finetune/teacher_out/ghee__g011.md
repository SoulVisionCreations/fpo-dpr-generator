## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Thembang Highland Dairy FPC Ltd, based in Thembang, Dirang, West Kameng, Arunachal Pradesh, proposes a farmer-owned ghee making unit rooted in the rich Himalayan dairy tradition. Ghee, or clarified butter, is India’s most cherished traditional dairy product, valued for its aroma, taste, and culinary versatility. The unit will source fresh milk locally, a dependable and familiar raw material for member farmers, and convert it into premium ghee through a standardized sequence: raw milk procurement, pasteurization, cooling, homogenization, cream separation, chilling, butter churning, ghee clarification, and hygienic packaging. The model integrates village-level milk aggregation with modern processing, quality assurance, and brand-led marketing to deliver a consistent, traceable, and authentic product.

### Uses & Benefits
- Lactose-free and gluten-free traditional fat suitable for diverse dietary preferences.
- High-heat stability and rich nutty flavour ideal for Indian cooking, frying, baking, and tadka.
- Widely used in festive foods, mithai, temple offerings, and traditional wellness practices.
- Shelf-stable edible fat with strong consumer trust and cultural acceptance.
- Serves household, foodservice, confectionery, and Ayurvedic applications.

### Market Potential
Demand for pure, traceable ghee is growing across urban and rural markets, supported by rising interest in traditional fats and clean-label foods. Consumers value provenance-led brands and artisanal quality, which aligns with a highland origin story. The Northeast offers a differentiated opportunity through tourism, hospitality, monasteries, and institutional kitchens, alongside household demand via retail and direct-to-consumer channels. Digital commerce and regional distribution can extend reach beyond local towns, while premium positioning enables better value realization for member farmers.

### Value Chain
The value chain begins with local milk procurement from member households and partner dairy groups, followed by reception, filtration, quality testing, and rapid chilling. Pasteurization and homogenization ensure food safety and consistent texture before cream separation. The cream is churned into butter, clarified into ghee, and finely filtered to attain desirable colour, aroma, and granulation. Packaged ghee moves to ambient storage and then to retail, wholesale, institutional, and online channels. By-products such as skim milk, buttermilk, and ghee residue can be channelled into beverages, culinary uses, feed, or tie-ups with local food enterprises, enhancing overall economics.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition spans cooperative dairies, large private brands, and regional artisanal producers. The FPO can differentiate on purity, origin, and traceability, backed by transparent farm-to-jar storytelling. A brand identity anchored in “Thembang Highland” can highlight pasture-led feeding, mountain freshness, and farmer ownership. A multi-channel strategy combining local retail, kirana partnerships, weekly markets, tourism touchpoints, institutional sales, and direct-to-consumer fulfillment will support steady offtake. Product formats can address household and professional kitchens, with premium packaging, lab certifications, batch traceability, and the AGMARK quality mark reinforcing consumer trust.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Milk supply variability: structured procurement contracts, seasonal planning, and fodder support through FPO services.
- Quality and adulteration risks: strict SOPs, rapid testing at collection, calibrated instruments, and batch-wise retention samples.
- Process and food safety: validated heating profiles, filtration, hygienic design, and a documented food safety management system.
- Price volatility: prudent inventory management, flexible pricing ladders, and strategic hedging through diversified channels.
- Logistics in hilly terrain: decentralized chilling, route optimization, buffer stocks of packaging inputs, and contingency planning.
- Regulatory compliance: FSSAI licensing, labelling norms, periodic audits, and routine staff training on GMP and hygiene.
- Market acceptance: focused brand building, sampling, cook-with campaigns, and institutional engagement.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit generates inclusive jobs across milk collection, chilling, processing, quality control, packaging, and sales. Women’s self-help groups can contribute to packing and retail activations, while local youth can staff procurement and logistics. Stable, transparent milk payments raise farm incomes and encourage better animal nutrition and health services. Value addition within the district retains wealth locally, strengthens cooperative spirit, and builds entrepreneurial capacity in allied segments such as feed, transport, and retail.

### Sustainability
- Energy: high-efficiency boilers and motors, heat recovery from ghee kettles, solar preheating, and scope for dung-based biogas.
- Water: clean-in-place optimization, rainwater harvesting where feasible, and treatment and reuse of dairy effluent for non-process purposes.
- Waste and by-products: culinary use of ghee residue, safe disposal of fats, and recyclable or reusable packaging choices.
- Climate resilience: diversified milk catchments, temperature-controlled logistics where needed, and preventive maintenance to minimize downtime.
