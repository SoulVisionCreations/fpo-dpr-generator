## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is the staple split pulse of India, obtained by de-husking and splitting whole pulses. This DPR focuses on gram-based dal derived from chickpea, popularly known as chana dal. Pulses are a primary source of plant protein in vegetarian diets and are valued for dietary fibre, iron, and B‑vitamins. Hinganghat Tur Dal Farmer Producer Company Ltd, based in Hinganghat, Wardha, Maharashtra, proposes to establish a modern dal mill in the pulse-growing belt to aggregate members’ produce, process it scientifically, and market consistent-quality dal under an FPO brand.

### Uses & Benefits
Gram dal is used daily in households and commercial kitchens across regions for curries, snacks, flours, and ready blends. It offers balanced plant protein, complex carbohydrates, and micronutrients, supporting satiety and wellness. For the FPO, dal milling enables reduction of post-harvest losses, value addition close to farm-gate, and better price realization for members. Consumers benefit from origin-assured, clean, uniform, safely processed dal with reliable cooking quality.

### Market Potential
India’s steady demand for pulses, rising health consciousness, and preference for minimally processed, protein-rich foods underpin sustained consumption of dal across seasons. Gram is widely cultivated, and the proposed location within a pulse-producing cluster ensures strong raw material access and logistics advantages. There is ample headroom to serve wholesale mandis, kirana stores, modern retail, institutions, and online channels with differentiated, traceable FPO-branded dal. Government nutrition and food security programs, as well as hotel and catering demand, add resilient offtake avenues.

### Value Chain
Member farmers supply cleaned or farm-graded whole gram to the FPO for aggregation. The milling value chain comprises procurement and storage, soaking for husk loosening, drying through controlled air or sun methods, staged cleaning and sieving to remove grit, de-husking with emery-roll technology, further aspiration and grading to separate husk, splitting on lentil splitters, optional polishing to meet market preferences, and packing into bulk and retail sacks for dispatch. Quality checks at intake, in-process, and final packing ensure food safety and uniformity. By‑products such as husk and brokens are monetized for feed and allied uses, improving overall value capture.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition spans unorganized local mills offering price-led supplies and regional or national brands emphasizing consistent quality. The FPO can differentiate through origin traceability, farmer-owned governance, transparent procurement, and clean-label processing. A hybrid channel strategy covering wholesalers, neighborhood retailers, institutional buyers, and e-commerce can diversify risk. Standardized SKUs for retail and bulk, attractive yet cost-effective packaging, assured quality certifications, and service reliability strengthen market access. Relationship selling, community connect, and tie-ups with government or quasi-government buyers can further stabilize demand.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price and availability risk: mitigate via seasonal aggregation, staggered procurement, and diversified sourcing within the cluster.
- Quality variability and storage losses: address with moisture-linked intake norms, fumigation and pest management, aerated godowns, and first‑in‑first‑out inventory controls.
- Processing bottlenecks and downtime: manage through preventive maintenance, critical spares, and annual maintenance contracts.
- Power interruptions: ensure reliable supply through sanctioned load adequacy and backup.
- Market and margin volatility: hedge through multi-channel sales, by-product revenue, and disciplined cost control.
- Compliance and food safety: adopt robust SOPs, hygiene practices, and routine lab checks.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The mill will generate skilled and semi‑skilled employment in operations, quality control, packing, logistics, and sales, with meaningful roles for local youth and women. Farmer members benefit from direct market linkage, grading-based payouts, and patronage dividends. Local sourcing and processing stimulate the rural economy, encourage good agricultural practices, and reduce wastage through scientific handling and storage.

### Sustainability
Dal milling is inherently low on effluents and supports circularity by utilizing husk and brokens. Energy‑efficient motors, dust control systems, and potential rooftop solar reduce the environmental footprint. Water use in soaking can be optimized through measured replenishment and hygienic handling. Recyclable bags and reusable pallets minimize packaging waste. Continuous improvement in housekeeping and occupational health enhances long-term sustainability.
