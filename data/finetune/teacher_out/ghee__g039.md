## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Kshipra Valley Dairy Producer Co Ltd, Runija, Tarana, Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh proposes to establish a ghee making unit to capture growing demand for authentic, hygienically processed clarified butter. Ghee is a time-honoured dairy product in Indian cuisine and Ayurveda, valued for its rich, nutty flavour and culinary versatility. It is naturally lactose-free and gluten-free, aligning well with modern dietary preferences while preserving traditional taste and functionality. The proposed unit leverages the FPO’s milk aggregation strength, member network, and local market familiarity to deliver consistent, traceable quality under a farmer-owned brand.

### Uses & Benefits
Ghee is a premium cooking and finishing fat suitable for sautéing, tempering, baking, and confectionery. It elevates taste in everyday meals, festive sweets, and foodservice preparations. Households prefer it for its aroma and shelf stability, sweet makers rely on it for authentic flavour, and institutional kitchens value its performance under heat. In wellness circles, ghee is embraced for traditional applications and culinary balance. For the FPO, ghee offers superior value realisation from milk fat, opportunities for brand building, and better margins compared to selling raw milk or cream.

### Market Potential
Demand for packaged, trustworthy ghee spans urban and rural markets, traditional retailers, modern trade, e-commerce, and institutional buyers. Consumers increasingly favour origin-assured products with farm-level traceability and transparent sourcing. Festive seasons and weddings amplify offtake, while year-round consumption in households and eateries maintains a stable base. Local cooperatives and private dairies have nurtured category familiarity, allowing a farmer-led brand to compete on purity, provenance, and freshness. Premium and niche variants also open avenues for differentiated offerings over time.

### Value Chain
The proposed value chain builds from farm-gate aggregation to branded retail packs:
- Milk procurement from FPO members with standardised quality checks and fat verification at collection points.
- Chilling and hygienic transport to the processing site to preserve freshness and fat quality.
- Pasteurization, cooling, and homogenization to stabilise milk parameters.
- Cream separation followed by chilling to set optimal churning conditions.
- Butter churning and controlled clarification to obtain granular, aromatic ghee.
- Filtration and hygienic packaging into consumer and institutional packs such as pouches, jars, and tins.
- Co-product monetisation through skim milk and buttermilk channels to improve overall value capture.
- Backward linkages for input services to members and forward linkages with distributors, retailers, and online platforms for wider market reach.

### Competition & Marketing
The category is served by local dairies, state milk unions, national brands, and private labels. A farmer-owned enterprise can differentiate on authenticity, short and transparent supply chains, and strict adherence to good manufacturing and hygiene practices. Marketing will highlight purity, origin traceability, and consistent flavour, supported by food safety compliance and batch-wise quality assurance. Route-to-market will combine general trade, institutional sales to sweet makers and restaurants, modern retail tie-ups, and e-commerce onboarding. Sampling in neighbourhood stores, festive promotions, and partnerships with regional sweet brands can accelerate acceptance.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw milk quality and fat variability: Deploy robust testing at collection, supplier training, and incentive-linked quality premiums to stabilise inputs.
- Input price volatility: Use procurement planning and flexible pricing with key B2B customers to partially hedge exposure.
- Process and food safety risks: Enforce documented SOPs, HACCP-based controls, skilled supervision, and regular audits.
- Power and utility disruptions: Provide adequate backup and preventive maintenance of utilities to ensure process continuity.
- Packaging integrity and shelf life: Adopt validated packaging materials and sealing systems, with routine stability checks.
- Market and receivable risks: Diversify channels and buyers, apply prudent credit policies, and leverage channel financing where available.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit generates local jobs across milk collection, processing, quality assurance, logistics, packaging, and sales. It strengthens member incomes through assured procurement, better price realisation for fat, and profit accruals at the producer collective. The model fosters skill development, women’s participation in processing and retailing, and allied enterprises in transport, packaging, and input supply. Stable dairy offtake improves household cash flows and resilience in the FPO’s command area.

### Sustainability
Sustainability will be embedded through energy-efficient chilling and heating systems, insulation, and heat recovery in clarification. Water conservation, effluent management, and fat-trap compliance will protect local ecosystems. Co-products such as skim milk and buttermilk will be channelled productively to minimise waste. Packaging choices will prioritise recyclable materials where feasible, and logistics planning will optimise routes to lower emissions. Over time, renewable energy integration can further reduce the unit’s footprint.
