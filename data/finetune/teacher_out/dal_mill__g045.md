## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is the split and de-husked form of pulses, produced through a sequence of cleaning, de-husking, splitting, and polishing. Gram-based dal from chickpea is a staple across Indian cuisines and an anchor of the vegetarian diet. For Doimukh Farmers Pulses Producer Company Limited, located in Doimukh, Papum Pare, Arunachal Pradesh, establishing a dal mill aligns with the region’s agrarian base and the proximity to pulse-growing belts in adjoining areas. Setting up processing near the source improves raw-material quality control, reduces logistics costs, and ensures timely delivery to local and regional markets.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a daily protein source used in home cooking, food service, and institutional kitchens. It supports a wide range of dishes, batters, and flours, and forms the base for snacks and ready mixes. Nutritionally, dal offers plant proteins, dietary fibre, and vital micronutrients. Processed dal allows for consistent cooking qualities and predictable taste. Properly milled and graded dal shortens cooking time, enhances palatability, and provides a clean, uniform product for retail and bulk buyers. Polished and unpolished variants can be offered to address diverse consumer preferences.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal remains resilient across income segments due to its central role in Indian diets. Urbanization, rising health consciousness, and preference for packaged, safe, and traceable food drive a steady shift to branded and hygienically processed dal. The North East offers an attractive market, with local consumption growing and trade linkages to neighboring states. Institutional demand through public procurement, hostels, defense establishments, and hospitals adds further depth. Policy support for pulses, improved market linkages through FPOs, and expanding modern retail and e-commerce channels enhance the opportunity for a community-owned dal brand from Doimukh.

### Value Chain
- Farmer sourcing: Aggregation of whole chickpea from member and associate farmers, guided by transparent quality standards, moisture checks, and fair grading.
- Primary handling: Cleaning, bagging, and storage at the FPO collection points and central warehouse with palletization, aeration, and regular fumigation as per good storage practice.
- Processing: Soaking, drying, pre-cleaning in reel machines, de-husking in emery roll dehuskers, post-dehusking cleaning, splitting in a lentil splitter, polishing, grading, and packing.
- By-products: Husk and brokens channelled into feed and allied markets, supporting circularity and incremental revenue.
- Packaging & dispatch: Consumer packs and bulk sacks with tamper-evident seals and batch coding for traceability, dispatched to wholesalers, retailers, institutions, and online partners.
- Quality assurance: Standard operating procedures, in-process checks, finished-goods inspection, and periodic lab testing for food safety, supported by FSSAI compliance and HACCP-based practices.
- Market feedback: Continuous feedback loop from traders, retailers, and consumers to finetune grades, polish levels, and pack sizes.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition arises from established regional mills, national brands, and informal trade. The FPO can differentiate on freshness, traceability, community impact, and consistent grading. A multi-tier branding strategy can offer everyday value packs, institution-grade bulk, and premium variants. Marketing will emphasize:
- Farm-to-fork transparency and quality assurances.
- Hygienic processing, standardized moisture, and low admixture.
- Local origin stories in packaging and promotions to build trust and recall.
- Multi-channel routes: traditional wholesale, kirana stores, modern retail, institutional supply, e-commerce, and social commerce.
- Demand activation through community demos, influencer tie-ups in local languages, and nutrition messaging.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material seasonality and price volatility: Mitigated through staggered procurement, forward contracts where feasible, buffer stocks, and diversified sourcing across geographies.
- Quality and storage risks: Addressed with moisture control, scientific storage, pest management, and regular quality audits.
- Processing risks: Preventive maintenance, critical spares inventory, trained operators, and safety protocols reduce downtime.
- Power reliability: Backup generation and energy-efficient machinery ensure continuity.
- Market risks: A balanced customer mix, value-added packaging, and brand-building lessen dependence on any single channel.
- Compliance and reputational risk: Robust food safety systems, batch traceability, and proper documentation ensure adherence to regulations and consumer expectations.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The dal mill creates steady employment for operators, helpers, quality and accounts staff, loaders, and drivers, with scope for women’s participation in grading and packaging. Local value addition improves farm-gate realization, reduces post-harvest losses, and keeps margins within the community. Skill development, exposure to modern food safety practices, and entrepreneurship opportunities for youth strengthen the rural economy. The FPO structure ensures equitable benefits distribution among member farmers.

### Sustainability
Sustainable operations focus on efficient water use in soaking, responsible effluent handling, energy-efficient motors, and potential solar integration. Dust extraction and PPE safeguard worker health. Husk and brokens are valorized into feed or biomass, cutting waste. Preference for recyclable, lower-footprint packaging and optimized transport consolidations further reduce environmental impact.
