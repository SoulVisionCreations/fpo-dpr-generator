## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents a farm-to-fork dal milling enterprise for Jasdan Pulses Producer Company Ltd, located at Jasdan in Rajkot district of Gujarat. The enterprise focuses on gram-based dal, produced by carefully de-husking and splitting chickpea into clean, uniform halves. Pulses serve as the principal protein source in the Indian vegetarian diet and are naturally rich in dietary fibre, iron and B-vitamins. The proposed dal mill anchors itself in a pulse-growing belt, enabling direct procurement from member farmers and neighbouring communities, reducing post-harvest losses, and enhancing value realisation within the FPO ecosystem.

The production process follows a proven sequence: procurement and safe storage of whole gram, controlled soaking, gentle drying, thorough cleaning to remove inert matter, precise de-husking with emery rolls, separation of husk, splitting, optional polishing to market preference, grading, and robust packing for dispatch. The arrangement is designed to deliver consistent quality while adhering to food safety and hygiene requirements.

### Uses & Benefits
Gram dal is a daily staple in Indian households, institutional kitchens, restaurants, and food service. It is used in dals, curries, khichdi, snacks, and as a base for ready-to-cook mixes. Consistent, low-defect, well-graded dal reduces cooking time, improves sensory quality, and enhances consumer trust in packaged staples. By-products such as husk, brokens and fines create additional revenue streams through feed, flour and snack applications, improving overall resource efficiency.

For the FPO, dal milling converts locally grown grain into higher-value, market-ready product, stabilises prices across seasons, and builds a community brand around purity, traceability and fairness. Farmers benefit from assured offtake, better grading, lower handling losses, and access to agronomic and post-harvest services coordinated by the FPO.

### Market Potential
Demand for packaged, traceable, hygiene-assured dal is strong across the country and remains resilient through economic cycles. Urbanisation, rising incomes, and health-conscious consumption patterns favour clean, branded staples with reliable cooking performance. Retailers and institutional buyers prefer suppliers with assured quality, consistent grading, food safety compliance, and steady supply.

Gujarat and the wider western belt offer a strategic sourcing and logistics base for gram, ensuring competitive procurement and timely market servicing. Opportunities exist in general trade, modern retail, institutional kitchens, processors, and export to diaspora markets, supported by India’s standing in pulses trade. The FPO’s direct-from-farm positioning, coupled with transparent quality practices, can capture premium shelf space and steady contracts.

### Value Chain
- Farm production and aggregation: Member farmers receive guidance on seed selection, pre-harvest practices, timely harvesting and primary cleaning to preserve grain quality.
- Procurement and storage: The FPO aggregates whole gram at village centres and the mill, ensuring moisture-safe storage, fumigation and FIFO discipline.
- Primary processing: Soaking for husk tempering, controlled drying, and staged cleaning remove stones, dust and extraneous matter without damaging kernels.
- De-husking and splitting: Emery roll de-husking is calibrated to minimise brokens, followed by separation of husk and efficient splitting on dedicated machinery.
- Finishing: Polishing is applied as per market preference, and grading ensures uniform size and appearance.
- Packaging and dispatch: Food-grade sacks and pouches safeguard shelf life and brand integrity; pallets and strapping support safe, efficient logistics.
- By-product utilisation: Husk and brokens are channelled to feed and flour markets, improving overall recovery value.
- Quality assurance and compliance: In-line checks, sampling, and documented SOPs maintain FSSAI compliance, with batch-wise traceability back to member lots.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition arises from local mills, regional brands, and national players. The FPO’s edge lies in traceable farm-sourced grain, consistent grading, transparent practices, and community-oriented branding. A multi-channel strategy spans wholesale distributors, kirana stores, modern trade, institutional buyers, processors, and e-commerce. Distinctive packaging, origin stories, kitchen-friendly pack sizes, and customer education through demos and digital outreach reinforce brand trust. Service reliability, quick replenishment, and retailer schemes help win shelf share in competitive clusters.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price and supply volatility: Addressed through staggered procurement from member pools, diversified sourcing geographies, hedging via forward tie-ups with buyers, and prudent inventory buffers.
- Quality and contamination risks: Mitigated with moisture control, calibrated cleaning, routine testing, and supplier grading protocols.
- Operational downtime: Reduced through preventive maintenance, skilled operators, critical spares planning and annual maintenance contracts.
- Power disruptions: Managed with stabilised power systems and contingency backup solutions.
- Working capital strain: Managed by disciplined credit policies, anchor buyer relationships, and aligned bank limits.
- Market and brand risk: Addressed through differentiated quality, consumer-focused branding, and continuous retailer engagement.
- Compliance and safety: Ensured by adherence to food safety standards, worker safety practices, and environmental norms.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project creates local jobs in procurement, operations, quality assurance, warehousing, logistics, sales and administration, with dedicated roles for women in grading, packing and quality control. Member farmers benefit from assured market access, better realisation for graded grain, and year-round services. The FPO structure strengthens collective bargaining, promotes financial inclusion, and circulates value within the community through dividends and patronage incentives. Training and upskilling raise productivity and safety, fostering a stable rural enterprise hub.

### Sustainability
Sustainability is embedded through efficient motors, dust control, and judicious water use with reuse opportunities in soaking and cleaning. By-products are valorised as feed and biomass, limiting waste to landfill. Grain storage and handling practices reduce loss and maintain quality, while responsible packaging options and route-optimised logistics lower environmental footprint. The FPO’s direct sourcing shortens the supply chain, enhances transparency, and supports climate-resilient farming advisory for member producers.
