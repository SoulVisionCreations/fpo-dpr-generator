## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Champaran Chana Dal Producer Co. Ltd., Semra, Bairiya, West Champaran, Bihar proposes a member-owned dal milling enterprise focused on gram. Dal is produced by de-husking and splitting whole pulses into clean, uniform cotyledons, followed by gentle polishing and hygienic packing. Pulses are central to the Indian diet, serving as an accessible and affordable plant protein, with valuable dietary fibre, iron, and vitamin B group nutrients. Locating a dal mill in a pulse-growing belt enables direct farm-gate procurement, lower logistics cost, and better quality control from the outset.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a staple for households, community kitchens, and institutional buyers. It is used in everyday cooking, quick-cook mixes, flour blends, snacks, and ready-to-eat or ready-to-cook formats. For consumers, benefits include consistent cooking quality, taste, and nutrition. For farmers, a nearby FPO-led mill creates an assured market, reduces post-harvest losses, and can reward good agronomy and better varietal choices. For the FPO, dal adds value to members’ produce, diversifies revenue through by-products, and fosters local employment.

### Market Potential
Urban and rural consumption of pulses is structurally strong, supported by rising health consciousness, preference for traditional foods, and food-service demand. Demand is resilient across income segments and shows low substitution risk because dal is a core kitchen essential. In Bihar and adjoining markets, gram-based dal has steady offtake through wholesale mandis, kirana shops, modern retail, institutional channels, and public nutrition programs. Stable year-round demand, coupled with the FPO’s proximity to raw material, supports reliable plant utilisation and market reach.

### Value Chain
- Farm production: Member farmers cultivate gram using locally adapted varieties. The FPO aggregates produce, encourages graded deliveries, and supports primary cleaning at village level.
- Procurement & storage: Direct procurement from members on transparent quality parameters, with safe storage under controlled aeration and pest management. Moisture and impurity control at intake drives recovery and quality.
- Primary processing: Soaking to condition grains, drying through ambient or assisted methods, sequential cleaning and aspiration to remove dust and grit, followed by de-husking with emery rolls and separation of husk through grading.
- Splitting & polishing: Controlled splitting yields uniform cotyledons; gentle polishing enhances appearance without compromising cooking performance.
- Grading & packing: Size and defect grading, metal detection and quality checks, then packing into food-safe sacks with proper labelling and traceability.
- Distribution: Sales through regional wholesalers, retailers, food-service operators, institutional buyers, and online marketplaces. By-products such as husk and brokens are channelled to feed, flour, or ancillary users, improving overall value capture.

### Competition & Marketing
The competitive set spans unbranded millers, regional brands, and national packagers. Differentiation for the FPO will come from farm-direct sourcing, batch-level traceability, consistent quality, and fair, transparent pricing. Marketing will prioritise:
- Quality assurance with food safety compliance, clean packaging, and accurate labelling.
- A dual channel approach covering traditional trade and modern formats, supported by trade schemes and in-store visibility.
- Business-to-business tie-ups with mid-size retailers, caterers, and institutional kitchens, offering reliable supply and custom pack options.
- A community brand story anchored in farmer ownership, local sourcing, and social impact, amplified through digital and local community channels.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price and availability: Addressed through staggered procurement, aggregation from multiple clusters, and structured contracts with members supported by transparent quality incentives.
- Quality and process risk: Managed with standard operating procedures for soaking, drying, and de-husking; in-process checks on moisture and defects; and batch traceability.
- Equipment downtime and power interruptions: Reduced through preventive maintenance, critical spares planning, and provision for backup power or hybrid energy solutions.
- Working capital constraints: Mitigated via appropriate banking limits, procurement planning aligned to harvest cycles, and disciplined receivables management.
- Compliance and food safety: Ensured through regular audits, sanitation protocols, pest control, and staff training.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project will create local jobs across procurement, quality assurance, operations, maintenance, warehousing, logistics, and sales. It strengthens farmer incomes through direct market access, reduces distress sales, and encourages adoption of better post-harvest practices. Women’s participation in grading, packing, and quality roles can be fostered through targeted training. Local sourcing, local processing, and local branding together stimulate the rural economy and enhance community resilience.

### Sustainability
Resource stewardship is embedded in the design. Efficient water use in soaking, with opportunities for reuse and safe discharge, and rainwater harvesting in the premises reduce freshwater dependence. Energy-efficient motors, improved material handling, and optional rooftop solar cut energy intensity. Husk and brokens are channelled to productive uses, minimising waste. Preference for recyclable or reusable packaging and responsible waste management further reduce environmental footprint, while good ventilation and dust control improve worker health and safety.
