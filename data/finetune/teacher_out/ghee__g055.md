## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Shivalik Dugdh Utpadak FPC Ltd, Nagal, Saharanpur, Uttar Pradesh proposes a community-owned ghee making unit that anchors dairy value addition within the producer ecosystem. Ghee, a clarified form of butter, is a revered staple in Indian culinary and wellness traditions. It is prepared from milk, cream, or butter through controlled heating and clarification, yielding a rich, nutty-flavoured fat phase that is lactose-free and gluten-free. The proposed unit aligns with the FPO’s mandate to enhance farmer incomes through aggregation, processing, branding, and direct market linkage.

### Uses & Benefits
Ghee is used for everyday cooking, deep frying, seasoning, and in preparation of sweets, bakery items, and traditional snacks. It is central to festive cuisines, temple offerings, and Ayurvedic formulations. Households prefer ghee for flavour and high-heat stability, while the food service sector values its performance and consistency. For farmers, local value addition stabilizes demand for milk across seasons, improves price realization, and opens up diversified revenue streams through by-products and allied dairy lines over time.

### Market Potential
Demand for ghee is broad-based across rural and urban markets, spanning kirana stores, sweet shops, bakeries, restaurants, catering units, religious institutions, and wellness outlets. Rising awareness of traditional fats, premiumization, and home-cooking trends support sustained consumption. The district and adjoining regions have strong dairy cultures with ample raw milk availability and established wholesale and retail networks. The category also supports differentiated positioning, including farm-fresh, source-traced, and community-branded variants that resonate with quality-conscious buyers.

### Value Chain
- Upstream: Member dairy farmers supply fresh milk to village-level collection points. Aggregation, quality checks, and timely chilling ensure raw material integrity.
- Midstream: Pasteurization, homogenization, and cream separation prepare fat-rich intermediates. Butter is churned and clarified to produce ghee, which is filtered and packed. Stringent hygiene, temperature control, and standard operating procedures maintain consistency and shelf stability.
- Downstream: Finished goods move through distributors, retailers, direct institutional buyers, and online channels. Packaging formats suit both household and bulk users. Traceability, labelling compliance, and batch-wise quality records build market trust.

### Competition & Marketing
Ghee competes with large dairy brands, regional cooperatives, and local unorganized players. An FPO brand can differentiate on farm connect, purity assurance, and transparent sourcing. Marketing will focus on:
- Assured quality through robust testing and compliance.
- Consistent flavour and aroma through controlled clarification.
- Trusted community brand story of farmer ownership.
- Multi-channel presence across kirana, modern trade, sweets and namkeen makers, bakeries, restaurants, e-commerce, and institutional supply.
- Seasonal push during festivals, weddings, and community events, with visibility at local melas and religious centres.
Value-added formats, attractive packaging, and AGMARK and FSSAI compliance support premium positioning.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw milk price and availability volatility: Mitigate through member-centric procurement, seasonal pooling, and supplier loyalty programs.
- Quality and adulteration risks: Enforce strict testing at intake and pre-dispatch, with documented SOPs and trained QA staff.
- Process variability: Adopt calibrated equipment, preventive maintenance, and batch parameter controls to ensure uniformity in colour, grain, and flavour.
- Market competition: Differentiate via authenticity, traceability, and reliable delivery; cultivate institutional accounts and subscription models.
- Regulatory and food safety compliance: Maintain valid licenses, internal audits, staff training, and recall readiness.
- Utility interruptions: Provide backup power, adequate boiler capacity, and contingency plans for water and packaging inputs.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit creates stable local employment in milk collection, processing, quality assurance, packaging, logistics, and sales. It strengthens the income base of member farmers through assured procurement and better margins from value addition. Skill development in dairy hygiene, equipment operation, and entrepreneurship uplifts youth and women. Community ownership fosters equitable growth, with surpluses recycled into member services, input support, and productivity enhancement.

### Sustainability
Efficiency in steam and electricity use, heat recovery, and insulated piping reduces energy intensity. Ghee production is shelf-stable, lowering cold-chain needs beyond milk chilling. Water-saving protocols and clean-in-place systems minimize consumption and effluent load, with appropriate treatment and reuse where permissible. By-products like buttermilk and skim milk can be channelled into cattle feed, beverages, or allied dairy lines, reducing waste. Responsible packaging choices and recycling channels further improve the environmental footprint.
