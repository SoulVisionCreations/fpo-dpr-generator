## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Ghee, the quintessential clarified butter of the subcontinent, holds a revered place in Indian kitchens, culture, and commerce. It offers a rich nutty flavour, is naturally free from lactose and gluten, and is prized for culinary versatility and traditional wellness uses. This project proposes a modern, hygienic ghee making unit under Korchi Sahyog Dugdh FPC Ltd, Korchi, Gadchiroli, Maharashtra, aggregating milk from local producers and converting it into premium, standardized ghee through a controlled process. The enterprise aligns with the PM-FME vision of value addition at the community level while strengthening farmer incomes and local processing capacity.

### Uses & Benefits
- Culinary: Ideal for everyday cooking, deep-frying, tempering, bakery and confectionery, and as a flavour enhancer with a high tolerance to heat.
- Nutrition & Wellness: Recognized in traditional food systems for aromatic short-chain fats and fat-soluble components; suitable for consumers seeking lactose-free and gluten-free options.
- Religious & Festive: Extensively used in offerings, sweets, and festive preparations, ensuring year-round baseline demand.
- Storage & Distribution: Naturally stable when properly clarified and packed, supporting wider market reach without cold-chain dependence.
- Institutional: Preferred by sweet makers, restaurants, and caterers for consistent flavour and performance.

### Market Potential
Demand for authentic ghee remains robust across rural and urban markets, cutting across income segments and regional cuisines. Growth is supported by rising packaged food consumption, a premiumization trend towards purity and origin-based branding, and increased home cooking. Institutional consumption by sweet manufacturers and food service adds depth to the market. E-commerce and quick-commerce channels have expanded access to branded regional products. The diaspora and gifting seasons further amplify offtake. An FPO brand anchored in traceable farmer networks can credibly address purity concerns and leverage local milk availability to build a loyal consumer base.

### Value Chain
- Raw Milk Procurement: The only raw material is milk, aggregated through village-level collection points and transported under hygienic conditions to the processing unit. Transparent quality testing at reception ensures fair pay and consistent input quality.
- Processing: The process follows a disciplined sequence—pasteurization, cooling, homogenization to standardize fat distribution, cream separation, chilling, butter churning, and controlled clarification to yield ghee with desired granulation, aroma, and colour. Filtration and moisture control safeguard shelf stability.
- Packaging & Storage: Ghee is packed in food-grade containers suited to retail, HoReCa, and institutional channels, with tamper-evident sealing and compliant labelling. Ambient storage under clean conditions preserves product integrity.
- By-products & Utilization: Skim milk and buttermilk streams from separation and churning can be channelled into liquid sales, fermented products, cattle feed supplements, or community nutrition initiatives, improving overall economics and minimizing waste.
- Quality & Compliance: A basic in-house lab, standard operating procedures, sanitation regimes, and documentation systems anchor compliance with relevant food safety standards and audits.
- Distribution: A blended route-to-market covering retail, wholesale, institutional sales, cooperative outlets, modern trade, and digital channels provides diversified reach and risk dispersion.

### Competition & Marketing
The category is competitive, with national brands, cooperative dairies, regional labels, and loose unbranded sellers. The FPO can differentiate through verifiable purity, farmer traceability, and a Korchi origin story. Branding should emphasize traditional process, rich flavour, and clean-label credentials. Marketing levers include:
- Partnerships with sweet makers, tiffin services, and restaurants for steady offtake.
- Demonstrations and tasting at retail points and community events.
- Digital storytelling showcasing farmers, ethical sourcing, and batch-level transparency.
- Attractive, robust packaging formats for household and institutional buyers.
- Certification, lab test disclosures, and grievance redressal to build consumer trust.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Input Variability: Seasonal shifts in milk supply and fat content can affect yields. Mitigation includes diversified procurement, incentive-linked quality programs, and rapid chilling at source.
- Quality & Food Safety: Contamination or adulteration can harm brand equity. Robust testing, supplier onboarding protocols, sanitation, and traceability reduce this risk.
- Price Volatility: Milk and fuel prices can compress margins. Long-term procurement relationships, periodical price resets, and efficiency measures in energy and process control help stabilize costs.
- Market Pressure: Intense competition may trigger pricing pressure. Focus on brand, purity assurance, institutional contracts, and value-added packs to protect realization.
- Operational Downtime: Equipment failures disrupt supply. Preventive maintenance, critical spares inventory, and operator training ensure continuity.
- Regulatory & Compliance: Changing labelling and fortification norms require vigilance. Dedicated compliance oversight and regular audits maintain alignment.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit will generate local employment in milk collection, processing, quality assurance, packaging, warehousing, logistics, and sales. It deepens the regional dairy ecosystem by offering stable offtake and fair, transparent payments to member farmers. Training and roles in quality, operations, and finance can support youth and women’s participation. Local procurement retains value within the community, catalyzing allied activities such as transport, packaging, and input supply.

### Sustainability
Sustainability is embedded through efficient heat recovery in the ghee kettle, insulation to cut thermal losses, and rational water use with cleaning-in-place systems. By-product valorization reduces waste. Options such as biomass or solar pre-heating, LED lighting, and energy-efficient motors lower the footprint. Responsible packaging choices, take-back pilots with institutional buyers, and safe effluent handling reinforce environmental stewardship. Farmer advisory on clean milk production, feed, and animal health uplifts upstream sustainability and quality.
