## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report proposes a farmer-owned dal milling enterprise anchored by Deesa Moong & Gram Producer Company Ltd, Deesa, Banaskantha, Gujarat. The unit will mill gram into dal by methodical cleaning, de-husking, splitting, polishing, and packing. Dal is a staple protein for vegetarian households across India and a central ingredient in everyday cuisine. Gram-based dal is valued for its balanced nutrition profile with fibre, iron, and B-vitamins, and its suitability for a wide range of regional recipes.

The proposed location in a pulse-growing belt enables direct farm-gate aggregation of raw gram, reduced transit losses, better quality control, and timely market response. The model strengthens the FPO’s role from aggregation to value addition, creating steady outlets for member-producers while improving consumer access to hygienic and standardized dal.

### Uses & Benefits
- Essential dietary protein for households, community kitchens, hostels, and institutional buyers.
- Ingredient for packaged foods, blended flours, and traditional snacks.
- Improved shelf-life and cooking performance when milled and polished under good manufacturing practices.
- Value addition at source enhances farmer realization versus trading whole grain, improving income stability and market resilience.
- FPO-led processing improves traceability, food safety, and branding credibility.

### Market Potential
Dal has enduring demand across seasons due to its role in daily meals and its affordability compared to animal protein. Urban retail, rural mandis, wholesalers, modern trade, institutional caterers, and e-commerce collectively ensure a deep and diversified market. Consumer preference is shifting toward clean, uniform, and hygienically packed dal with trusted sourcing. FSSAI-compliant packaging, consistent grain size, and reliable cooking quality strengthen acceptance across geographies. With strategic sourcing and prudent working capital, the FPO can address intra-year price movements while honoring supply commitments.

### Value Chain
- Farm-level aggregation: Member farmers and local growers supply cleaned, dried gram to the FPO under transparent quality parameters.
- Primary handling: Scientific storage with grading and lot-wise traceability preserves quality and enables planned processing.
- Pre-milling preparation: Soaking followed by controlled drying conditions primes kernels for efficient de-husking and splitting.
- Milling: Stepwise cleaning, emery roll de-husking, husk separation, splitting, and polishing yield uniform dal with minimal breakage.
- By-product handling: Husk and brokens are collected for livestock feed or ancillary uses, enabling additional revenue streams.
- Packing & dispatch: Food-grade sacks with batch coding, statutory labels, and brand elements support retail and wholesale channels.
- Market linkages: Direct supply to distributors, retailers, institutional kitchens, and digital marketplaces enhances reach and margins.

### Competition & Marketing
Dal retail is served by local millers, regional brands, and national players. The FPO’s competitive edge stems from origin-linked sourcing, fresher milling cycles, transparent quality norms, and community trust. The marketing approach will emphasize:
- Consistent grade and color with assured cooking performance.
- Hygienic, tamper-evident packaging and clear labeling.
- Origin and traceability narratives highlighting farmer ownership.
- Channel-focused SKUs for kirana, modern trade, and B2B buyers.
- Relationship-led sales with wholesalers in pulse clusters and nearby urban centers.
- Participation in state procurement and institutional tenders where feasible.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: Mitigated through diversified sourcing across member blocks, phased procurement, and prudent inventory planning with quality-based contracts.
- Quality variability: Addressed via pre-defined moisture and impurity norms, batch-wise grading, and calibrated machinery with standard operating procedures.
- Price fluctuations: Balanced through hedging via structured procurement cycles, flexible sales mix across channels, and focus on efficiency to protect margins.
- Operations downtime: Prevented through preventive maintenance, trained operators, and standby critical spares.
- Compliance and food safety: Ensured by GMP, hygiene protocols, periodic audits, and robust documentation.
- Market competition: Countered with strong branding, dependable service levels, and loyalty programs for trade partners.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project expands farm-to-market value capture within the community, enabling stable procurement, local jobs, and enterprise skills. It promotes producer participation in processing and marketing decisions, improves post-harvest handling, and fosters inclusive growth through women and youth engagement in grading, packing, and sales support. Strengthened local processing reduces logistics burden and enhances cash flow within the rural economy.

### Sustainability
The mill design encourages efficient water use in soaking, energy-efficient dryers, and dust control in processing lines. Husk and other by-products are gainfully utilized for feed or energy substitution. With planned adoption of renewable energy and responsible effluent management, the unit aligns with environmental compliance and climate-smart operations. Shorter supply chains from local sourcing reduce transport emissions and food loss.
