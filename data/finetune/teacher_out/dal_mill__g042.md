## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal, or split pulses, is a staple protein for Indian households and an essential ingredient in community feeding, hospitality, and food service. This project focuses on gram-based dal produced by de-husking and splitting chickpea. The operation is proposed by Shamgarh Urad Dal Farmers Producer Company Ltd, located in Shamgarh, Mandsaur, Madhya Pradesh, a pulse-growing cluster well suited for raw material access and market linkages. The enterprise aggregates whole pulses from member farmers, processes them into dal, and sells to wholesale, retail, and institutional channels, creating value at source and improving farmer incomes.

### Uses & Benefits
- Nutritionally dense: rich in plant protein, dietary fibre, iron, and B‑vitamins, supporting balanced diets.
- Culinary versatility: suited for daily home cooking, ready-to-cook mixes, and convenience foods.
- Shelf-stable: suitable for decentralized storage and long-distance distribution without cold chain.
- Value capture for farmers: FPO-led aggregation reduces intermediation, improves price realization, and rewards quality.
- Rural industrialization: processing at origin reduces logistics cost and grain handling losses.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal is resilient across income segments and geographies, with steady household consumption complemented by institutional demand from hostels, canteens, catering, and government-supported feeding programs. Urbanization and the rise of modern retail have expanded opportunities for standardized, branded packs, while traditional trade continues to prefer bulk sacks. Consumers increasingly seek clean, uniformly graded dal with consistent cooking quality, presenting scope for FPO-branded offerings. Madhya Pradesh is a leading pulse belt, providing abundant procurement potential and proximity to key markets in central, western, and northern India.

### Value Chain
- Farm production: chickpea grown by member farmers using locally adapted varieties; adoption of good agricultural practices and post-harvest handling improves quality.
- Aggregation and storage: the FPO organizes procurement at village collection points, conducts preliminary moisture and impurity checks, and stores in ventilated godowns.
- Primary processing: extended soaking, followed by drying, cleaning through reel machines, de-husking on emery roll equipment, husk separation, splitting in a lentil splitter, polishing, and final grading.
- Packing and dispatch: packing into sacks for wholesale and optional consumer packs for retail; distribution via transporters to mandis, wholesalers, retailers, and institutions.
- By-products: husk and brokens are marketed for cattle feed or further processing, enhancing overall recovery and margin.
- Services and finance: input credit, warehouse receipts, transport, and market information provided through the FPO network, strengthening member loyalty and supply stability.

### Competition & Marketing
Dal markets feature traditional traders, regional millers, and large national brands. FPO-led mills can differentiate through origin-linked quality, transparent grading, and farmer-backed traceability. A blended go-to-market strategy is recommended:
- Wholesale focus in nearby districts with stable bulk off-take.
- Institutional sales to hostels, caterers, and community kitchens with quality assurance and timely deliveries.
- Co-branded retail packs through kirana stores and modern trade, emphasizing cleanliness, uniform cooking, and farmer ownership.
- Digital outreach and local promotions at haats and melas, combined with sampling and recipe education.
Quality certifications, food safety compliance, and clear labelling will strengthen trust and enable premium positioning.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: manage through forward linkages with member farmers, staggered procurement, price hedging where feasible, and buffer stocking.
- Quality and moisture risk: deploy moisture meters, scientific storage, pallets, tarpaulin covers, and scheduled fumigation under professional supervision.
- Operations downtime: secure annual maintenance contracts, maintain critical spares, train operators, and follow preventive maintenance schedules.
- Power disruptions: ensure reliable grid connection with backup power and energy-efficient machinery.
- Market and price risk: diversify channels, build offtake agreements with wholesalers and institutions, and develop a steady branded retail base.
- Compliance and food safety: maintain FSSAI licensing, AGMARK grade alignment, hygiene SOPs, and batch-level traceability with proper documentation.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project creates local employment in plant operations, quality control, warehousing, logistics, and sales. It builds capacity among member farmers through grading-linked incentives and post-harvest training, improving incomes and resilience. Women’s participation is encouraged in sorting, packing, and quality functions. Local transporters, packers, and service providers benefit from steady business. The FPO structure ensures that surpluses can be reinvested in community assets, agronomic services, and member dividends, reinforcing inclusive rural development.

### Sustainability
Pulses enrich soils through biological nitrogen fixation, support climate-smart agriculture, and require comparatively modest inputs. Water used for soaking can be conserved through recirculation and safe discharge practices. Adoption of solar-assisted drying, efficient motors, and dust extraction improves energy efficiency and workplace safety. Husk and dust can be channelled to feed or biomass uses, minimizing waste. Preference for recyclable or biodegradable packaging, along with responsible plastic stewardship, further strengthens environmental performance.
