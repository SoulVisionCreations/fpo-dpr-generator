## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Jatni Mahila Dairy Producer Company, based in Jatni, Khordha, Odisha, proposes a Farmer Producer Organization-owned ghee making unit to transform locally procured milk into high-quality clarified butter. Ghee is a revered traditional dairy fat in India, valued for its rich nutty flavour, culinary versatility, and cultural significance. It is naturally lactose-free and gluten-free when produced from pure dairy fat, meeting both heritage and modern wellness preferences. The proposed unit strengthens local dairy value addition, improves farmer incomes through assured offtake, and establishes a traceable, quality-led brand for regional and wider markets.

### Uses & Benefits
- Everyday culinary medium with a high smoke point suitable for frying, sautéing, and tempering.
- Integral to Indian sweets, bakery, pooja offerings, and festive foods.
- Preferred fat in many traditional health and wellness practices.
- Naturally free from lactose and gluten, appealing to sensitive consumers.
- Shelf-stable when produced and packed correctly, reducing cold chain dependency.
- Distinct aroma and flavour elevate home cooking, HoReCa dishes, and packaged food applications.

### Market Potential
Demand for branded, traceable ghee continues to deepen across urban, semi-urban, and rural segments. Households, mithai and bakery units, restaurants, caterers, and institutional kitchens are sizable and growing consumer bases. Within retail, modern trade, neighborhood stores, and e-commerce collectively support premiumization and convenient pack sizes. Rising interest in traditional fats, clean labels, and farm-to-fork provenance strengthens the case for an FPO brand anchored in transparent sourcing, consistent quality, and responsible production practices.

### Value Chain
- Milk sourcing: The FPO aggregates milk from member producers through village-level collection points to ensure freshness and traceability. The sole raw material is milk.
- Primary processing: Procurement of raw material (milk) -> Pasteurization -> Cooling -> Milk Homogenizing -> Cream Separation -> Chilling -> Butter Churning -> Ghee Separating -> Packaging.
- Quality assurance: Standard operating procedures cover raw milk testing, fat standardization, hygienic handling, filtration, and final product checks to meet regulatory and brand norms.
- Packaging and distribution: Consumer packs in jars and pouches, and bulk packs for HoReCa and institutional buyers. Distribution through local retail, regional distributors, and online channels.
- By-products and resource optimization: Skim milk, buttermilk, and other streams can be directed to allied products, cattle feed, or B2B sales, improving overall value realization and minimizing waste.

### Competition & Marketing
The competitive landscape comprises national dairy cooperatives, private brands, regional dairies, and the unorganized sector. The FPO can differentiate through:
- Purity, taste consistency, and credible batch-level traceability back to member farms.
- Compliance with dairy standards and recognized certifications to assure buyers of quality and safety.
- Strong local identity, transparent farmer ownership, and authentic storytelling.
- Smart pack formats for both household and institutional users, with attractive, informative labels.
- Omnichannel go-to-market: local retail penetration, partnerships with sweets and bakery makers, sampling with culinary influencers, and targeted digital marketing.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw milk variability: Introduce farmer training, prompt chilling, and incentive-linked quality parameters.
- Input price volatility: Enter into seasonal supply MoUs, maintain buffer stocks of packaging, and dynamic pricing with key accounts.
- Operational reliability: Invest in preventive maintenance, trained operators, standby utilities, and SOP-driven production.
- Food safety and compliance: Strengthen in-house lab testing, vendor audits, and documentation rigor.
- Market acceptance: Pilot batches, consumer panels, and gradual geographic expansion with feedback loops.
- Working capital pressures: Align credit terms with buyers, leverage invoice discounting, and maintain disciplined inventory turns.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit catalyzes local livelihoods through steady milk procurement, dignified employment for women and youth, and skill upgradation in dairy operations, quality control, and sales. Better price discovery and profit sharing promote inclusive growth within member villages. Linkages with local transporters, packaging suppliers, and retailers expand the economic multiplier in and around Jatni.

### Sustainability
- Energy stewardship via efficient boilers, heat recovery, and optimized chilling loads; renewables wherever feasible.
- Water responsibility through CIP discipline, wastewater treatment, and reuse for non-contact applications.
- Waste minimization by valorizing by-products and adopting recyclable packaging options.
- Short, transparent supply chains that reduce transport miles and strengthen local circularity.
