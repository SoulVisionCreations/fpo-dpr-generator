## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Tenali Dal Farmers Producer Company Ltd, based in Tenali, Guntur, Andhra Pradesh, proposes to establish a gram-based dal milling unit. The unit will de-husk and split chickpea into high-quality dal, adding value at the FPO level and ensuring better returns to member farmers. Dal is a staple of Indian cuisine and a vital source of plant protein, dietary fibre, iron, and B-vitamins. Locating the mill in a pulse-growing belt ensures assured raw material availability, lower logistics cost, and strong community linkage.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is consumed daily across households, institutions, and the food service sector. It is versatile in cooking, nutritionally dense, and complements cereals to deliver a balanced diet. For consumers, benefits include consistent cooking quality, cleanliness, and improved shelf life through proper packaging. For the FPO, processing whole gram into dal captures margin that would otherwise accrue to intermediaries, stabilizes incomes, and creates local employment.

### Market Potential
The domestic market for dal is broad-based, resilient, and aligned with dietary habits across regions. Demand is supported by retail consumption, ready-to-cook and convenience foods, public nutrition programmes, and institutional buyers such as hostels, canteens, and caterers. Urbanization, health awareness, and organized retail expansion further support branded dal uptake. Tenali is a recognized pulse trading hub, which enhances the FPO’s access to markets and logistics.

### Value Chain
The proposed value chain integrates farm-gate procurement with modern processing and market access:
- Aggregation of whole chickpea from member farmers at village collection points, with moisture and quality checks.
- Primary cleaning and scientific storage to preserve grain integrity and minimize losses.
- Conditioning through soaking and drying to facilitate gentle de-husking.
- Mechanical cleaning to remove grit and foreign matter, followed by emery roll de-husking and husk separation.
- Splitting into uniform cotyledons, optional polishing based on market preference, and final grading.
- Packing in bulk sacks for B2B buyers and consumer packs for retail, followed by dispatch through wholesalers, modern trade, and direct institutional supply.
Traceability and quality control at each stage strengthen buyer confidence and enable premium positioning.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition includes local mills competing on price and freshness, and national brands competing on consistency and packaging. The FPO’s marketing strategy centers on raw material traceability, unpolished and low-polish variants, stringent hygiene, and reliable supply. Branding can highlight the Tenali provenance, farmer ownership, and purity. Channel focus will include nearby mandis, kirana stores, modern trade, e-commerce, and institutional buyers. Differentiators include uniform cooking quality, low breakage, optimized moisture, and clean packaging.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price volatility: staggered procurement, farmer tie-ups, warehouse receipt finance, and prudent hedging through timing and diversified sourcing.
- Quality variability: strict intake standards, on-site testing, calibrated soaking and drying, and continuous process monitoring.
- Operational risks: preventive maintenance, trained operators, and documented SOPs with food safety certifications.
- Market and receivable risks: channel diversification, credit discipline, and insurance cover for stocks and receivables.
- Compliance and EHS: adherence to food safety, labour, fire, and pollution norms; regular audits and staff training.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit will generate steady employment in operations, quality control, procurement, warehousing, packing, and administration, with additional seasonal opportunities. Women’s participation in cleaning, grading, and packing can be prioritized. Member farmers benefit from assured offtake, fair pricing, prompt payments, input advisory, and participation in value addition, thereby improving household incomes and resilience.

### Sustainability
Sustainability measures include efficient motors, optimized drying techniques, solar augmentation where feasible, and water reuse in conditioning. Husk and fine bran can be valorized as fuel or feed, reducing waste. Dust control and noise abatement protect worker health. Local sourcing and short supply chains lower transport emissions, while robust storage reduces post-harvest losses.
