## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Litchi juice is a premium fruit beverage known for its delicate floral aroma, translucent appearance, and refreshing sweetness. Prepared from ripe, deseeded litchi arils, it captures the characteristic bouquet and taste that consumers associate with fresh litchi. The product can be offered as a ready-to-drink juice or as a nectar-style beverage, with careful formulation to preserve the fruit’s natural flavor profile while achieving shelf stability. The Lundra Litchi Growers FPC Ltd of Lundra, Surguja, Chhattisgarh, will aggregate member harvests and convert them into value-added juice, stabilizing returns for growers and extending market presence well beyond the short harvest window.

Litchi naturally contains vitamin C, phenolics, and aromatic volatiles. The juice is processed under hygienic conditions to reduce enzymatic browning and microbial load, employing controlled dilution, pulping, clarification or fine filtration, and thermal processing to ensure safety and quality. Use of permitted preservatives such as potassium metabisulphite (KMS), together with good manufacturing practices, safeguards color, aroma, and shelf life.

Raw material considerations are central to product quality. Fruit should be harvested at optimum maturity, handled gently to minimize bruising, and quickly moved to processing to limit enzymatic and oxidative changes. Desired fruit attributes include high juiciness, intact arils, clean peel, and a typical sweet aroma. Sourcing from member orchards enables consistent supply and traceability.

### Uses & Benefits
- A refreshing beverage suitable for family consumption, hospitality, and institutional catering, as well as cafés and quick-service restaurants.
- A premium option for festive occasions and gifting due to its distinctive taste and regional identity.
- A value-addition pathway for farmers, converting perishable surplus into a branded product with a longer selling season.
- Potential base for mocktails, blends, and culinary applications such as desserts and syrups, enhancing versatility for foodservice.
- Convenience for urban consumers seeking authentic fruit-based beverages with consistent quality and safety.
- Supports better price realization for growers by reducing gluts during peak harvest and distributing sales across the year.

### Market Potential
Demand for authentic fruit juices is expanding across urban and semi-urban India, driven by convenience, health orientation, and preference for recognizable ingredients. Litchi enjoys strong consumer recall, with seasonal excitement translating into willingness to purchase packaged formats that retain the characteristic aroma and taste. Organized retail, e-commerce, modern trade, and Horeca channels all provide pathways to market, while institutional buyers and corporate gifting open additional opportunities. Within Chhattisgarh and adjacent states, regional provenance can be leveraged, and there is scope for inter-state sales and selective exports where diaspora and specialty retailers value niche Indian fruit beverages.

### Value Chain
- Farm level: Member growers in and around Lundra harvest at optimal maturity, conduct basic sorting and crate packing, and deliver to the FPO collection center. Training on harvest timing, field hygiene, and rapid aggregation reduces losses and preserves quality.
- Aggregation and pre-processing: At the FPO premises, fruit is received, weighed, and batch-coded. Washing and sanitation remove surface contaminants, followed by careful deseeding and aril separation.
- Juice processing: Arils are diluted with potable water as per the product formulation, then passed through a pulper/refiner to obtain uniform juice. Filtration improves clarity and mouthfeel. Pasteurization ensures safety and extends shelf life. KMS, where permitted and within regulatory limits, may be added to maintain color and flavor.
- Packaging and storage: Hot-filled or post-pasteurized packaging in suitable food-grade containers is labeled and coded for traceability, then stored in clean, cool conditions with palletized stacking and first-expiry-first-out practices.
- Distribution: Sales occur through FPO-owned outlets, local distributors, retail chains, institutional buyers, and online channels. Cold-chain can enhance product life and quality in downstream logistics.
- By-products and waste: Seeds and peels can be valorized for extraction, animal feed, composting, or biogas. Process water is treated, and organic residues are channelled to compost pits, closing the loop and reducing disposal costs.

### Competition & Marketing
The product competes with national and regional juice, nectar, and fruit drink brands, as well as seasonal fresh fruit. Differentiation rests on provenance, farmer-ownership, traceability, and authentic taste. A compelling brand narrative—“from Lundra orchards to your glass”—can build trust. Multiple pack formats (on-the-go packs, family bottles, and premium gift packs), attractive label design, and consistent quality will support repeat purchases. Marketing levers include in-store sampling, digital campaigns, partnerships with cafés, and festive promotions. Certifications, adherence to food safety standards, and transparent ingredient lists further strengthen positioning.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material seasonality and price swings: Mitigate via pre-season contracts with members, staggered procurement, rapid processing during peak harvest, and controlled storage of semi-processed intermediates where feasible.
- Quality risks (browning, oxidation, off-flavors): Control through rapid chilling, antioxidant use where permitted, hygiene, inert handling, and validated pasteurization.
- Microbiological safety: Enforce good manufacturing practices, sanitation standard operating procedures, validated critical control points, and routine lab testing.
- Supply chain disruptions: Build diversified channel partners, maintain safety stocks of packaging materials, and plan for contingency logistics.
- Regulatory compliance: Adhere to FSSAI standards, permitted additives, and labelling norms; maintain records for traceability and recalls.
- Financial and working capital pressures: Use realistic sales projections, prudent credit policies, and access to institutional credit lines aligned with seasonal cycles.
- Climate variability impacting yields: Promote orchard best practices among members, including canopy management, mulching, and integrated pest management.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project generates local jobs in sorting, processing, packaging, logistics, quality assurance, and sales. It enables women’s participation in grading and packaging, and offers youth opportunities in retail outreach and digital marketing. Stable offtake from member orchards improves incomes, reduces distress sales, and encourages reinvestment in better farming practices. Skills training in food safety, machine operation, and entrepreneurship raises local capabilities.

### Sustainability
Resource efficiency is embedded through careful water management, cleaning-in-place systems, and effluent treatment. Energy efficiency measures—such as heat recovery, insulation, and efficient motors—reduce operating footprint. Packaging choices can prioritize recyclable materials. Organic residues are composted or valorized, minimizing landfill. With good agronomy among members and responsible processing practices, the overall value chain advances environmental stewardship while delivering high-quality juice.
