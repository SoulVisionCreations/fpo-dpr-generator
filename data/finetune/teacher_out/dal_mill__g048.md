## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents a dal milling enterprise for Beas Valley Pulses Producer Company Ltd, Raison, Naggar, Kullu, Himachal Pradesh. The enterprise focuses on gram-based dal produced by de-husking and splitting chickpea into clean, uniform cotyledons for culinary use. Dal, or split pulses, are a staple of Indian nutrition, valued for plant-based protein, dietary fibre, and micronutrients. The FPO proposes to leverage farmer aggregation, community-level storage, and professional processing to deliver consistent quality dal to nearby rural and semi-urban markets, institutional buyers, and retail consumers.

### Uses & Benefits
- Culinary versatility across home cooking, food service, and ready-to-cook segments.
- Nutritional profile supporting vegetarian diets, with beneficial fibre and micronutrients.
- Long shelf-life when processed and packed correctly, enabling reliable year-round supply.
- By-products such as husk and brokens add value through feed and flour channels.
- FPO-led processing strengthens farmer incomes through assured offtake and fair grading.

### Market Potential
Dal is a high-frequency purchase in Indian households and a core input for messes, hostels, canteens, quick-service restaurants, and public nutrition programs. Demand remains resilient across income segments due to habitual consumption patterns. Urbanisation, rising awareness of unpolished variants, and growth of organised retail and e-commerce are expanding the market for hygienically milled, consistently graded, and attractively packed dal. The proposed unit can serve nearby hill districts and access larger pulse-trading corridors through transport linkages to adjoining plains. Institutional buyers increasingly prefer traceable FPO brands with quality assurance and reliable fulfilment.

### Value Chain
- Aggregation: The FPO mobilises member farmers and allied growers to aggregate whole gram during harvest windows. Direct procurement reduces intermediation and ensures traceability.
- Primary handling: Produce is cleaned, graded, and stored under moisture-safe conditions in godowns near the mill, with pallets, aeration, and stack discipline to preserve quality.
- Processing: The flow covers soaking for husk loosening, drying, cleaning, de-husking in emery rolls, aspiration and sieving to remove husk, splitting, and optional polishing depending on market preference for unpolished or polished dal. Consistent moisture management underpins milling efficiency and recovery.
- Quality assurance: In-house testing verifies moisture, impurities, damaged grains, and final product attributes. Batch-wise records support traceability and recall readiness.
- Packaging and dispatch: Dal is packed in food-grade sacks or consumer packs and routed through wholesale, retail, and institutional channels. By-products such as husk and brokens are channelled to feed mills and flour processors, improving overall unit economics.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition stems from traditional dal mills in pulse belts and national brands offering packaged pulses. The FPO can differentiate through:
- Direct-from-farm sourcing that tells a traceability and fairness story.
- Strict process control yielding uniform cooking time, taste, and reduced impurities.
- Distinct product lines: unpolished dal for health-conscious consumers, polished dal for conventional markets, and B2B bulk packs for kitchens and processors.
- Branding, AGMARK and FSSAI compliance, and clean, tamper-evident packaging.
- Market development through local kirana stores, mandis, modern trade, horeca, government procurement when eligible, and digital channels.
- Community outreach, sampling, and recipe-led communication to build trust and repeat purchase.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price volatility: Use advance procurement, pooled buying through the FPO, and staggered stocking aligned to cash credit capacity.
- Moisture and quality risk: Deploy calibrated moisture meters, covered transport, warehouse hygiene, and scientific drying protocols to stabilise milling recovery.
- Processing losses: Maintain preventive maintenance, operator training, and yield benchmarking by lot to fine-tune de-husking pressure and splitter settings.
- Power interruptions: Provide backup power and voltage stabilisation; consider gradual adoption of solar to offset grid variability.
- Working capital pressure: Optimise inventory turns, negotiate supplier and buyer credit terms prudently, and prioritise cash-and-carry with new accounts.
- Market competition: Differentiate on provenance, quality consistency, and reliable fulfilment; nurture anchor B2B accounts to secure base volumes.
- Compliance and food safety: Maintain FSSAI licensing, periodic audits, and documented SOPs for sanitation, pest control, and traceability.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The enterprise creates local livelihoods in procurement, grading, milling, packing, logistics, quality control, and sales. It expands women’s participation in sorting, packing, and administration, and builds technical skills among rural youth. By linking farmers to value addition and branded sales, the FPO reduces distress selling, improves price realisation, shares surpluses among members, and catalyses allied micro-enterprises for transport, packaging, and retail.

### Sustainability
The mill encourages resource-efficient operations through energy-efficient motors, dust extraction and reuse, and optional rooftop solar. Water use for soaking is managed through recirculation and safe discharge practices. Husk and fines are valorised as cattle feed or fuel, minimising waste. Preference for recyclable or biodegradable packaging and route planning for dispatch reduces the environmental footprint. Continuous farmer training on post-harvest handling further limits losses across the chain.
