## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents a dal milling enterprise for Bichhiya Moong Dal Producer Company Ltd at Bichhiya, Mandla, Madhya Pradesh. The unit focuses on gram-based dal, converting whole chickpeas into clean, uniform, hygienically processed split pulses. Pulses hold a central place in the Indian diet as an affordable and reliable source of plant protein, complemented by dietary fibre, iron and B-vitamins. Establishing a dal mill within a pulse-growing belt connects primary producers directly to value addition, reduces logistics gaps, and enables the Farmer Producer Organization (FPO) to capture higher margins across the chain.

The process follows established, food-safe unit operations: scientific procurement and storage of whole gram; conditioning by soaking; drying to safe and processable moisture; pre- and post-dehusking cleaning and grading; de-husking using emery rolls; splitting in a dedicated lentil splitter; polishing to desired appearance; and packing and dispatch. This flow balances quality, yield, shelf-life and operational efficiency, and is well-suited to rural or semi-urban pulse clusters.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a daily staple across households, institutional kitchens and the food service sector. It is versatile for direct cooking, value-added ready mixes, and as an ingredient in snacks and flours. Clean, graded and well-polished dal offers better cooking performance, improved taste, and longer shelf-life. For consumers, hygienic packaging enhances safety and convenience. For the FPO and its members, local processing unlocks higher value realization, stabilizes market access, and builds a regional brand rooted in traceability and farmer ownership.

### Market Potential
The market for packaged and standardized dal remains resilient, supported by steady consumption across demographics and geographies. Rising urbanization, food safety awareness, and preference for branded, traceable staples are deepening the shift from loose to packaged pulses. Institutional demand from public programs, hostels, hospitals, canteens and food processors adds a stable bulk offtake channel. Proximity to pulse cultivation in Madhya Pradesh enables reliable raw material sourcing, seasonal aggregation advantages, and competitive delivered costs to nearby consumption hubs. Modern retail and online marketplaces offer incremental reach, while traditional wholesale and mandi networks continue to move volumes at scale.

### Value Chain
A farmer-centric value chain underpins the project. The FPO aggregates gram from member farmers using transparent grades and quality parameters. Scientific storage preserves quality and minimizes losses. Conditioning through soaking and controlled drying prepares grain for efficient de-husking. Cleaning removes foreign matter at appropriate stages to protect equipment and enhance product quality. Emery roll de-husking and splitting yield uniform cotyledons, followed by polishing to market preferences and grading for consistency. Finished dal is packed in food-grade sacks or retail pouches and dispatched through a multi-channel distribution network.

By-products such as husk, brokens and fines can be channelled into feed, flour or fuel applications, improving overall resource efficiency. Quality assurance spans moisture management, size uniformity, minimal broken content, and food safety compliance under applicable standards. The FPO can further strengthen backward linkages with agronomy advisories, seed access and harvest scheduling to enhance farmer incomes and raw material consistency.

### Competition & Marketing
The competitive set includes local mills supplying loose dal, regional brands with strong mandi linkages, and national brands with extensive retail coverage. The FPO’s differentiators lie in farmer ownership, traceability to origin, consistent quality, and credible compliance. A dual strategy of bulk supplies for institutions and wholesalers, alongside branded retail packs for urban and semi-urban outlets, widens market access. Certification and compliance with relevant food safety standards, AGMARK grading where applicable, and adoption of good manufacturing and hygiene practices build trust.

Marketing will emphasize purity, taste, and farmer-backed provenance. Seasonally aligned procurement and efficient milling protect margins. Trade promotions, partnerships with wholesalers and kirana stores, hospitality and catering tie-ups, and participation in government or development-led procurement opportunities expand demand. Thoughtful packaging design, clear labelling, and presence on digital commerce platforms support brand visibility.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: Mitigate through member-centric procurement plans, staggered buying, quality-based pricing, and prudent inventory management aligned to storage capacity and cash flow.
- Quality and storage risk: Address via scientific storage, moisture and pest management, sanitation protocols, and routine quality checks.
- Operational downtime: Reduce through preventive maintenance, trained operators, adequate spares, and service support from OEMs.
- Power reliability: Plan backup power and energy-efficient machinery to stabilize throughput and cost.
- Market price risk: Balance bulk and retail channels, pursue institutional contracts, and maintain a flexible sales mix to protect margins.
- Compliance and food safety: Implement robust SOPs, staff training, and documented systems for traceability and recall readiness.
- Finance and liquidity: Ensure adequate working capital lines, disciplined receivables management, and insurance coverage across assets and stock.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project creates steady local employment in operations, quality control, packaging, warehousing, logistics and administration, with strong scope for women’s participation. It builds technical skills among rural youth and anchors enterprise in the pulse-growing region, reducing distress migration. Member farmers benefit from assured offtake, better grading transparency, and improved price realization through value addition. Local ecosystem enterprises in transport, maintenance, packaging and services also gain.

### Sustainability
The dal mill contributes to lower post-harvest losses by enabling timely aggregation and processing. Cleaner production can be pursued through energy-efficient motors, aspiration and dust control, and consideration of renewable energy for drying or auxiliary loads. Water used in conditioning can be managed responsibly with reuse where feasible and proper effluent practices. Husk and fines can substitute fuel or serve as inputs for feed, reducing waste. Emphasis on recyclable or biodegradable packaging and adherence to good environmental practices aligns the enterprise with sustainable development goals.
