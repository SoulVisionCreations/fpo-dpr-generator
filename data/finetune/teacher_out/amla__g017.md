## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Mango juice is a ready-to-serve beverage made by extracting the wholesome pulp of ripe mangoes and converting it into a stable, refreshing drink that retains the fruit’s characteristic aroma, sweetness, and golden hue. The product can be offered as a clarified juice or as a nectar-style beverage with desirable mouthfeel, derived from carefully selected cultivars. For Malda Mango Producers Company Ltd, based in Mahadipur, English Bazar, Malda, West Bengal, the raw material base includes renowned local varieties prized for their superior flavour and texture. The FPO’s integrated approach enables traceable sourcing from member orchards, scientific grading and ripening, hygienic washing, pulping, filtration, and pasteurization, followed by dosing of a permitted preservative such as potassium metabisulphite, finished by sanitary filling and packaging. Consistent sensory quality is ensured through standardized operating procedures, trained personnel, and robust quality assurance.

### Uses & Benefits
Mango juice serves household consumption, foodservice, institutional demand, and travel retail. The beverage offers convenience, year-round availability beyond the harvest window, and a consistent taste profile. It provides natural fruit goodness, with carotenoids contributing to colour and appeal, and antioxidants supporting wellness positioning. For consumers, the product satisfies both refreshment and indulgence, while for channel partners it delivers dependable shelf stability and predictable throughput. For farmers, conversion of fresh fruit into value-added juice stabilizes returns, reduces waste, and opens markets across geographies.

### Market Potential
India’s deep cultural affinity with mango, rising demand for packaged beverages, expanding modern retail, and rapid penetration of e-commerce create a favourable backdrop for mango juice. Consumers increasingly prefer safe, branded, and traceable products with consistent quality. The HoReCa segment values bulk packs and dependable supply during off-season months. Malda’s reputation for premium mangoes strengthens origin branding, enabling a distinctive proposition that competes on authenticity and freshness. There is steady interest from export channels for ethnic and tropical fruit beverages, offering diversification potential once domestic distribution is established.

### Value Chain
The value chain begins in member orchards with improved harvesting, pre-cooling, and careful handling to preserve fruit integrity. At the FPO facility, fruit is received, graded, washed, and de-stoned. The pulp is extracted through hygienic pulping, then filtered to achieve desired clarity or texture. Thermal processing ensures microbial safety, followed by cooling, dosing of an approved preservative, and final pasteurization. The product is filled into bottles or pouches, coded, and packed for dispatch. Upstream, the FPO organizes input supply, advisory, and aggregation; downstream, it coordinates logistics, warehousing, and multi-channel distribution. By-products such as peel and stones can be valorized as feedstock for pectin extraction, animal feed, compost, or bioenergy, enhancing circularity and reducing waste.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition arises from national beverage brands, regional fruit processors, and local fresh-juice operators. The FPO’s farm-to-bottle traceability, authentic cultivar selection, and transparent sourcing provide a strong differentiation. A marketing mix anchored in origin storytelling, season-led campaigns, and quality certifications can build trust. Sales channels include general trade, modern trade, institutional buyers, e-commerce, and on-the-go outlets. Product formats can cater to retail consumers as well as foodservice needs. Co-branding with retailers, festival promotions, and sampling at high-footfall locations can accelerate trial and repeat purchase.

### Risk & Mitigation
Key risks include crop variability due to weather, seasonal gluts and price swings, microbial and food-safety challenges, packaging cost inflation, and logistics disruptions. Mitigation measures include member-based procurement planning with quality norms, staggered harvesting and ripening protocols, buffer storage of semi-processed pulp, and robust sanitation with validated critical controls. Long-term packaging contracts reduce volatility, while diversified channels distribute demand risk. Quality systems aligned with national regulations and hazard analysis frameworks sustain compliance. Business interruption and stock coverage via insurance add resilience.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project creates stable employment in processing, quality assurance, maintenance, warehousing, and sales, with additional seasonal roles in sorting, washing, and packing. It supports women’s participation in hygienic handling and packaging roles and builds youth skills in food technology and operations. Farmer members benefit from assured procurement, transparent pricing, and capacity building. Reduced post-harvest losses and better market access uplift local incomes, stimulate allied services such as transport and packaging, and strengthen the rural economy.

### Sustainability
Sustainability is embedded through efficient water use with cleaning-in-place and reuse where feasible, energy-efficient thermal systems, and adoption of renewable power where practical. Organic residues are converted into compost, feed, or bioenergy, moving the plant toward near-zero waste. Short supply chains and origin-focused sourcing reduce transport emissions. Packaging optimization with recyclable options and responsible disposal guidelines further shrink the footprint. Continuous farmer training promotes climate-smart orchard management and integrated pest and nutrient practices.
