## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is split pulses obtained by de-husking and splitting whole pulses. Gram-based dal, processed from chickpea, is a staple in Indian households and institutions. It is a vital protein source for vegetarian diets and is rich in fibre, iron, and B-vitamins. This DPR proposes a farmer-owned dal milling unit under Keshkal Tribal Pulses Farmer Producer Company Ltd, located in Keshkal, Kondagaon, Chhattisgarh. Setting up the unit in a pulse-growing belt enables direct aggregation from member farmers, lowers logistics costs, and strengthens regional value addition and farmer incomes.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is consumed daily across cuisines in cooked, tempered, and soup-like preparations, and serves as an ingredient in snacks, mixes, flours, and ready-to-cook items. Milling improves cooking quality, reduces cooking time, and standardizes taste and texture. Packaged dal ensures hygiene, sorting, and uniform quality that consumers and institutional buyers demand. By-products like husk and brokens find value in feed, flour, and fuel applications, enhancing resource efficiency. For the FPO, dal milling transforms a commodity into a branded, quality-assured product with better margins, shorter working capital cycles, and direct access to markets.

### Market Potential
India is the world’s largest producer and consumer of pulses, and dal remains central to nutrition and food security. Health-conscious consumers are shifting toward unadulterated, traceable, protein-rich foods, creating strong demand for packaged, standardized dal. Institutions such as hostels, canteens, and social welfare programs prefer reliable bulk supply. E-commerce and modern retail are expanding the reach of regional brands. Chhattisgarh’s pulse-growing clusters offer assured raw material and proximity to consuming markets across central and eastern India, positioning the FPO to serve both local and regional demand.

### Value Chain
The value chain begins with member farmers and allied growers. The FPO aggregates, tests for moisture and quality, and stores whole pulses under proper ventilation. Processing involves soaking, drying, cleaning to remove impurities, de-husking in emery roll machines, secondary cleaning to separate husk, splitting in a lentil splitter, polishing to improve appearance, grading, and packing in food-grade sacks. Quality control spans each stage: sample testing, sieve analysis, moisture checks, and sensory grading. Finished goods are warehoused for dispatch to wholesale, retail, and institutional buyers. By-products are channelled to feed and fuel markets. Digital traceability, batch coding, and compliance with food safety norms build trust with buyers.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition includes local unorganized mills, regional brands, national FMCG players, and private-label buyers. The FPO’s strategy emphasizes farm-to-fork traceability, community ownership, and transparent sourcing. Key differentiators include consistent de-stoning, moisture-controlled processing, uniform grading, and hygienic packaging. The marketing mix spans:
- Community brand for retail packs targeting neighborhood stores and e-commerce
- Bulk supply to wholesalers, kirana distributors, and modern trade private labels
- Institutional sales to canteens, hostels, and government-linked programs where eligible
- Partnerships with SHGs and cooperative retail outlets for last-mile penetration
Brand building includes origin storytelling, quality seals under relevant food safety and grading standards, attractive packaging, and point-of-sale visibility. A dedicated sales team engages channel partners with stable pricing, timely delivery, and service. 

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility and crop risk: Mitigate through diversified sourcing from member farmers, pre-harvest tie-ups, staggered procurement, and prudent stocking.
- Quality variability: Deploy strict inbound QC, calibrated grading and cleaning, and batch-wise moisture control.
- Processing downtime: Enter into annual maintenance contracts, maintain critical spares, and train operators on preventive maintenance.
- Energy and water reliability: Secure backup power, optimize motors and airflow, and reuse soaking water where feasible while complying with norms.
- Market and price risk: Balance between retail, wholesale, and institutional channels; offer private-label conversion; maintain a buffer of finished goods.
- Compliance and safety: Maintain licenses, food safety audits, sanitation SOPs, worker PPE, and insurance for assets and stocks.
- Working capital constraints: Align credit terms with buyers, use cash-credit limits prudently, and sequence procurement with order book.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project generates steady local employment in operations, logistics, quality assurance, administration, and sales, with strong opportunities for women in grading, packing, and lab functions. Members benefit from assured market access, transparent pricing, and potential patronage returns. Local value addition reduces distress sales, supports ancillary livelihoods in transport and packaging, and builds enterprise skills among tribal youth. Reliable supply of clean, quality-assured dal contributes to community nutrition and regional economic resilience.

### Sustainability
The unit will prioritize energy-efficient motors and dust-control systems, with options for solar augmentation. Soaking and cleaning water will be managed with reuse and safe discharge practices. Husk and other by-products can be valorized as fuel, compost, or feed, minimizing waste. Packaging will consider recyclable materials and extended producer responsibility where applicable. Process design promotes minimal product loss, safe working conditions, and compliance with environmental and food safety regulations. Proximity sourcing reduces transport emissions and supports climate-resilient local value chains.
