## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Bastar Adivasi Dugdh Utpadak FPC Ltd, Tokapal, Bastar, Chhattisgarh proposes to establish a dedicated ghee making unit to add value to locally sourced milk and expand market presence under an FPO-owned brand. Ghee, or clarified butter, is India’s most celebrated traditional dairy fat, prized for its rich nutty flavour, culinary versatility, and suitability for diverse dietary preferences. It is naturally lactose-free and gluten-free. The unit will follow a streamlined, food-safe process starting from raw milk procurement through pasteurization, cooling, homogenizing, cream separation, chilling, butter churning, ghee clarification, and packaging, with quality control embedded at each step.

### Uses & Benefits
Ghee is integral to everyday cooking, traditional sweets, festive and ceremonial uses, foodservice applications, and as a flavour enhancer in modern recipes. Its high smoke point supports frying and sautéing, while its aroma elevates premium preparations. As a traditional fat, ghee resonates with consumers seeking authentic taste, clean labels, and products rooted in local dairy ecosystems. The product’s stability and convenience make it suitable for both household and institutional consumption.

### Market Potential
Demand for ghee is broad-based across urban, semi-urban, and rural markets, spanning households, sweet-makers, bakeries, restaurants, canteens, temples, and wellness-focused consumers. Preference for traditional fats, interest in minimally processed foods, expanding modern retail, and e-commerce adoption support category growth. Regional flavour profiles and provenance-based branding offer further differentiation. The Bastar FPO can leverage proximity to raw milk, cooperative trust, and a farmer-owned narrative to position a reliable, traceable, and community-benefiting brand.

### Value Chain
The value chain begins with milk aggregation from member farmers and nearby suppliers, supported by hygienic collection, rapid chilling, and transparent quality checks. Pasteurized and cooled milk is homogenized to standardize quality, followed by cream separation. Chilled cream is churned to produce butter and buttermilk; butter is then clarified to ghee, with fine filtration to deliver clarity, aroma, and granulation associated with premium quality. Packaging in food-grade containers safeguards shelf stability and flavour. Supporting inputs include tested packaging materials, utilities, and lab consumables. Finished goods move through distributors, retailers, institutional buyers, and digital channels. Traceability, lab testing, and standard operating procedures ensure consistent quality throughout.

### Competition & Marketing
The category features cooperative and private brands, alongside local dairy artisans with strong regional loyalties. To stand out, the FPO will emphasize freshness of source milk, careful clarification for signature aroma and grain, rigorous food safety compliance, and equitable farmer returns. A multi-channel approach is envisaged: general trade through kirana stores, modern retail, e-commerce and quick-commerce platforms, and institutional sales to sweet shops, bakeries, restaurants, and community kitchens. Packaging will span consumer and institutional packs, with clear labelling on ingredient purity and storage guidance. Optional quality seals and certifications can reinforce trust. On-ground sampling, chef partnerships, festival tie-ins, and storytelling about Bastar’s farmer community will deepen brand connect.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw milk quality variability and adulteration risks are addressed through strict reception testing, supplier vetting, and farmer training on hygienic milking and rapid chilling.
- Seasonal fluctuations in milk availability are managed via procurement planning, buffer stock of intermediate butter, and supply diversification within the FPO network.
- Price volatility is mitigated through structured procurement arrangements, dynamic pricing with members, and ongoing margin monitoring.
- Processing risks such as contamination and equipment downtime are minimized through sanitation protocols, CIP, preventive maintenance, and trained operators.
- Packaging and logistics disruptions are reduced by dual-sourcing, adequate safety stocks, and route optimization.
- Compliance and reputational risks are handled through adherence to food safety standards, batch-wise traceability, and recall preparedness with product and public liability insurance.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit will create direct jobs in production, quality control, packaging, logistics, sales, and administration, alongside indirect opportunities in milk collection, transport, and packaging supply. As a farmer-owned enterprise, the FPO model channels value addition back to members through better price realization, stable offtake, capacity building, and patronage incentives. Inclusion of women’s collectives in collection, testing, and packing roles enhances livelihoods and community development in Bastar’s tribal regions.

### Sustainability
The facility design prioritizes efficient thermal and electrical energy use, with options for biomass-based or other cleaner fuels for steam generation and heat recovery to reduce consumption. Water stewardship includes reuse of condensate, disciplined CIP cycles, and compliant effluent treatment. By-products such as buttermilk and ghee residue can be valorized for sale to food businesses, supporting circularity and waste minimization. Recyclable or returnable packaging formats, waste segregation, and responsible sourcing policies further strengthen the sustainability profile.
