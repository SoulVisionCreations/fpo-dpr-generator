## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is the split form of pulses obtained after de-husking and splitting the whole grain. In this project, gram-based dal is produced from chickpea (chana) through a sequence of soaking, drying, cleaning, de-husking, splitting, polishing, and packing. Pulses are central to the Indian vegetarian diet and are valued for protein, fibre, iron, and B‑vitamins. The proposed dal mill will be implemented by Khangchendzonga Pulses Producer Co. Ltd, Damthang, Namchi, Namchi, Sikkim, leveraging an FPO-led aggregation and processing model close to the pulse-growing belts for assured raw-material access and lower logistics cost.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a daily staple across Indian cuisines, used in soups, curries, snacks, flours, and ready mixes. It cooks relatively quickly, stores well, and fits diverse dietary patterns. For consumers, quality dal offers desirable taste, aroma, cooking consistency, and nutrition. For farmers and the FPO, dal milling converts primary produce into a higher-value, branded product, stabilizes prices across seasons, reduces post-harvest losses, and shortens market chains. By-products such as husk and brokens can be monetized for feed or fuel, improving overall resource efficiency.

### Market Potential
The market for dal is broad-based, spanning household kitchens, hotels and restaurants, institutional nutrition programmes, wholesale mandis, and modern retail. Rising health consciousness, preference for plant-based protein, and sustained demand from regional cuisines support stable consumption. Packaging, food safety, and consistent grading are gaining importance as consumers seek cleaner labels and reliable cooking performance. For Sikkim and adjoining regions, there is an opportunity to serve local demand while tapping inter-state trade for gram-based dal. FPO-led branding can command trust through traceability from member farms.

### Value Chain
The value chain begins with member farmers cultivating chickpea and supplying whole grains to the FPO collection points. The FPO aggregates, grades for size and moisture, and stores raw pulses in ventilated godowns. Processing follows a systematic route: soaking to condition kernels, drying to optimal moisture, screening to remove foreign matter, de-husking in emery roll machines, husk separation through reel machines, splitting in lentil splitters, gentle polishing as per market preference, and final packing in sacks or consumer packs. Quality control at each stage ensures uniform grain size, low admixture, and safe moisture levels. Packed dal moves through multiple channels: wholesale distributors, retail stores, institutional buyers, e-commerce, and FPO outlets. Husk and brokens are sold to feed or allied users, enhancing value realization and minimizing waste.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition includes local dal millers, regional brands, private labels, and national players. Differentiation for the FPO will come from origin linkage, freshness, transparent grading, and credible quality certifications. A dual strategy—regional wholesale penetration and a focused branded play—can expand market reach. Key levers include attractive and sturdy packaging, clear cooking instructions, periodic consumer sampling, tie-ups with self-help groups and cooperatives, and supply agreements with hotels, caterers, and nutrition programmes. Digital storytelling around farmer ownership and sustainable practices strengthens brand equity and price realization.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material volatility: Mitigate through staggered procurement, forward purchase agreements with members, and buffer stocks aligned with storage norms.
- Quality and storage risks: Deploy calibrated moisture meters, scientific warehousing, periodic fumigation aligned with food safety norms, and first‑in‑first‑out protocols.
- Processing downtime: Maintain spares, annual maintenance contracts, operator training, and process checklists to reduce breakdowns.
- Power reliability: Provide backup power and consider energy‑efficient drives and dryers to reduce operating disruptions.
- Working capital gaps: Use inventory financing, supplier credit, and disciplined receivables management supported by digital invoicing.
- Compliance and food safety: Implement standard operating procedures, hygiene audits, batch traceability, and regular staff training.
- Market competition: Focus on quality consistency, reliable supply, and relationship-based sales to institutions and retailers.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The dal mill will generate skilled and semi‑skilled jobs in procurement, processing, quality control, packing, logistics, and sales. It opens avenues for women’s participation in grading and packing. Farmer members benefit from assured offtake, better price discovery, and shared margins through dividends or patronage bonuses. Localized value addition stimulates allied enterprises in transport, packaging, and maintenance, circulating income within the community and strengthening rural livelihoods.

### Sustainability
Sustainable elements can be embedded across the operation: water reuse in soaking, energy‑efficient dryers and motors, solar augmentation, and proper handling of soak water. Husk and brokens can be valorized for feed or as biomass fuel, reducing landfill. Recyclable or low‑impact packaging and optimized route planning can lower the footprint further. Training on safe chemical use and sanitation enhances worker welfare and product integrity.
