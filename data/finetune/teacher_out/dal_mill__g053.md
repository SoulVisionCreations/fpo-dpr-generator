## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents the establishment of a dal milling unit by Pusad Chana Utpadak Producer Company Limited, Pusad, Yavatmal, Maharashtra. The proposed unit will focus on gram-based dal, converting whole chickpea into clean, de-husked, polished splits through an integrated and food-safe process. Locating the mill in a pulse-growing belt ensures close proximity to member farmers, lower logistics costs, better control over quality, and improved price realization for the primary producers.

Dal milling comprises sequential operations of soaking, drying, cleaning, de-husking, grading, splitting, polishing, and hygienic packing. The FPO-led model strengthens aggregation, traceability, and standardization while retaining value within the local economy.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a daily staple and a core protein source for vegetarian diets. It is rich in dietary fibre, iron, and B-vitamins, and features prominently in home cooking, quick-service formats, institutional kitchens, and food processing applications such as flours, mixes, and snack bases. Standardized dal delivers consistent cooking quality and taste, improving household convenience and reducing fuel and time use.

Beyond premium-quality dal, the mill can monetize by-products. Brokens serve blended or processed applications, husk and bran support feed or fuel uses, and graded screenings are routed to appropriate end-uses, improving the overall recovery value. Hygienic packaging with proper labelling supports retail and B2B sales while assuring food safety and traceability.

### Market Potential
Demand for dal is broad-based and resilient, supported by population growth, rising health awareness, and a strong cultural preference for pulses. Urban retail and e-commerce channels continue to expand, even as traditional wholesale and mandi networks remain significant. Institutional kitchens, hostels, hospitals, canteens, and food service aggregators value assured quality and reliable supply.

Policy focus on pulses, farmer collectivization, and decentralized processing augments raw material availability and strengthens local value addition. Branded and private-label demand for standardized, contaminant-free dal is robust. Transparent sourcing from an FPO and consistent milling quality position the enterprise competitively across multiple price tiers and pack formats.

### Value Chain
- Farmer members aggregate whole chickpea under FPO-led procurement, with primary cleaning at village or collection centres.
- The mill undertakes soaking, drying, cleaning, de-husking, splitting, polishing, grading, and packing under good manufacturing practices.
- Finished goods and by-products are routed to wholesale, retail, institutional, and processing segments through FPO-managed distribution or channel partners.
- End-to-end traceability, batch-wise quality records, and feedback loops strengthen procurement planning and milling parameters.
- Efficient storage, pest management, and moisture control reduce losses and preserve quality through the chain.

### Competition & Marketing
The competitive landscape includes regional dal mills, national brands, private labels of modern retail, and import-based traders. The FPO’s advantage lies in direct farm linkages, transparent quality, food-safe processing, and fair, stable pricing to both farmers and buyers. Marketing will emphasize:
- Traceable, consistent quality with lab-backed assurance.
- Clean, uniform grains with low foreign matter and stable cooking performance.
- Multiple pack formats for retail and bulk packs for institutions.
- Tie-ups with wholesalers, kirana and modern trade, HORECA, caterers, and processors.
- Digital engagement, e-commerce listings, and community-level brand building in the FPO command area.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price and availability: Mitigated by staggered procurement, storage planning, member pre-harvest engagement, and offtake commitments with buyers.
- Quality variability and moisture: Addressed through grading, moisture control protocols, and batch-specific process adjustments.
- Processing losses and downtime: Reduced by preventive maintenance, trained operators, and equipment AMC.
- Power and utilities interruptions: Managed via backup solutions and energy-efficiency upgrades.
- Working capital stress: Eased through prudent credit policies, inventory turns, and banking limits aligned to seasonal procurement.
- Compliance and food safety: Ensured with robust SOPs, periodic audits, and staff training.
- Pest and storage risks: Managed through integrated pest management, safe fumigation, and warehouse hygiene.
- Climate-linked shocks: Addressed through diversified sourcing within the FPO network and crop advisory support.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The mill generates direct skilled and semi-skilled jobs in operations, quality, maintenance, stores, logistics, and administration, with strong inclusion potential for women and youth. It catalyzes allied services in transport, packaging, warehousing, and machine servicing. By aggregating and processing locally, the FPO raises farm-gate realization, reduces distress sales, and captures a larger share of consumer value within the community. Capacity building in quality and business management enhances long-term resilience.

### Sustainability
Sustainability is embedded through efficient milling, reduced wastage, and valorization of by-products. Options include cleaner energy sources, energy-efficient motors, and feasible solar integration. Water use in soaking is optimized with reuse safeguards and compliant discharge. Dust and noise are controlled through appropriate enclosures and filtration. Recyclable or biodegradable packaging, responsible waste segregation, and safe chemical handling round out the environmental stewardship framework.
