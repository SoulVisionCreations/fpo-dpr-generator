## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Dal is a staple split pulse produced by de-husking and splitting whole pulses such as chickpea. It forms the core protein of the Indian vegetarian diet and is valued for fibre, iron, and B‑vitamins. The proposed unit of Tokapal Bastar Arhar Producer Company Limited at Tokapal, Bastar, Chhattisgarh, aims to establish a modern dal mill focused on gram-based dal with farmer-centric sourcing and traceable quality. Locating the unit in a pulse-growing belt enables direct aggregation from member farmers, lower logistics costs, and better control over grain moisture and quality.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is consumed daily across households, community kitchens, restaurants, and institutional canteens. It is versatile in traditional and contemporary cuisines and suits a range of cooking formats. Nutritionally dense, shelf-stable, and easy to transport, dal supports food security while offering value addition over raw whole pulses. The milling process also generates by-products such as husk and brokens that can be used as cattle feed or biomass fuel, enhancing overall resource efficiency and additional income streams for the FPO.

### Market Potential
India is a leading producer and consumer of pulses with consistent, broad-based demand across regions and income segments. Demand is supported by rising health consciousness, increased urban retail penetration, government nutrition programs, and the preference for plant proteins. Gram-based dal enjoys year-round offtake, with festive and wedding seasons adding demand peaks. The market is served by a mix of branded packaged dal, private labels, and local millers. There is clear headroom for quality-assured, traceable FPO-branded dal in both retail and institutional channels, including kirana, modern trade, HoReCa, and e-commerce.

### Value Chain
- Farm-level aggregation: direct procurement from member farmers and local mandis, with moisture and purity checks, and fair, transparent pricing.
- Primary handling: scientific storage on pallets, aeration, and periodic fumigation; batch-wise identification to maintain traceability.
- Processing sequence:
  - Soaking in clean water for an extended period to condition the grain
  - Drying through air dryers or sun-curing to achieve uniform moisture
  - Cleaning and grading to remove stones, dust, and off-spec grains
  - De-husking using emery roll systems with controlled abrasion
  - Fine aspiration and separation to remove husk and fines
  - Splitting in dedicated lentil splitters with careful setting of clearances
  - Polishing as per market preference using mild media and food-safe agents
  - Final grading and metal detection where applicable
  - Packing in retail and bulk formats with batch coding and FSSAI-compliant labels
- Logistics and distribution: dispatch to nearby mandis and wholesalers, retail networks, institutions, and FPO-owned outlets; backhauls leveraged for freight efficiency.
- By-product utilization: husk and brokens sold to feed makers or used as biomass; dust collected via bag filters to improve housekeeping and safety.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition spans unorganized local mills, established regional brands, and national players. Differentiation for the FPO rests on consistent quality, farmer traceability, hygienic processing, and fair pricing. The marketing mix will focus on:
- Brand positioning around purity, nutrition, and farmer ownership
- Distinct SKUs for retail and institutional buyers with clear grade descriptors
- Tie-ups with kirana stores, bulk buyers, hostels, hospitals, and government-linked nutrition programs where eligible
- Digital engagement for awareness, e-commerce listings, and community outreach
- In-store visibility, sampling, and collaborations with local self-help groups to promote adoption

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw material price and availability: forward linkages with members, staggered procurement, and calibrated stocking policies
- Quality variability and moisture: strict inward QC, standardized conditioning, and batch-wise processing SOPs
- Storage and pest risk: aerated godowns, pallets, fumigation plans, and insurance
- Operational downtime: AMC with OEMs, preventive maintenance schedules, and critical spares inventory
- Regulatory and food safety: FSSAI licensing, adherence to good manufacturing practices, regular audits, and staff training
- Working capital strain: disciplined inventory turns, reliable receivable management, and bank working capital lines
- Market fluctuations: diversified channel mix, flexible product mix, and continuous customer feedback loops

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project creates local employment in procurement, plant operations, quality control, packaging, warehousing, logistics, and sales. Women and youth can be engaged in grading, packing, and store operations. Direct farmer procurement improves price realization, reduces distress sales, and strengthens community resilience. The FPO’s professionalization fosters better governance, skill development, and retention of value within the region.

### Sustainability
Dal milling is inherently low-waste. The unit will target energy-efficient motors, dust collection systems, and responsible water use with reuse of soak water where feasible. By-product valorization reduces disposal loads, while biomass-based thermal use can offset fossil fuels. The plant layout will promote safe material handling, noise and dust control, and compliance with environmental norms. Preference for recyclable packaging and local sourcing further lowers the footprint.
