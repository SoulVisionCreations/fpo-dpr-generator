## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
This Detailed Project Report presents a dal milling enterprise focused on gram-based dal, to be owned and operated by Ghatal Masoor Producer Company Limited at Chandrakona Road, Chandrakona-I, Paschim Medinipur, West Bengal. Dal, or split pulses, is obtained by de-husking and splitting whole pulses such as chickpea. As a staple in Indian cuisine and a primary protein source in vegetarian diets, dal holds deep cultural, nutritional, and economic significance. The proposed facility will convert locally procured whole gram into consistent-quality dal through soaking, drying, cleaning, de-husking, splitting, polishing, and packing. The location strategy leverages proximity to pulse-growing belts, reducing logistics costs, post-harvest losses, and transit risks while improving farmer realisations through direct procurement and aggregation by the FPO.

### Uses & Benefits
Dal is a daily kitchen essential across households, institutional kitchens, restaurants, and food service, consumed as soups, curries, flours, snacks, and ready mixes. It offers plant-based protein along with dietary fibre, iron, and B-group vitamins, supporting balanced nutrition. Milling adds value to farm produce, improves storability, and enables standardised cooking quality. For the FPO and its members, dal milling ensures assured procurement, transparent grading, and better share of the consumer rupee. For consumers, it delivers consistent taste, uniform cooking, food safety assurance, and convenience through hygienic packaging and reliable availability.

### Market Potential
India exhibits sustained demand for pulses across regions and income segments, supported by dietary preferences, health consciousness, and institutional consumption. Urbanisation, growth in modern retail, and the rise of e-commerce are expanding demand for branded and hygienically packed dal. Government nutrition schemes, social safety programmes, and public procurement add stability to baseline offtake. In eastern markets, including West Bengal and neighbouring states, a steady preference for gram-based dal and derived products supports year-round sales, with seasonal variations in crop arrivals managed through calibrated procurement and storage. FPO-led traceability, transparent quality grading, and fair pricing enhance trust among wholesale and retail buyers.

### Value Chain
The value chain begins with member-farmer aggregation and direct procurement of whole gram. After intake and quality check, produce is stored under aerated, pest-protected conditions. Processing includes soaking to condition kernels, followed by drying under controlled conditions. Initial cleaning removes coarse impurities, after which de-husking with emery rolls detaches the outer husk. Subsequent fine cleaning separates husk and fines. The conditioned kernels are then split in the lentil splitting unit, gently polished to desired finish, and packed in food-grade sacks for dispatch. By-products such as husk and broken fractions are channelled to feed, flour, or energy applications, improving overall recovery values. Forward linkages cover mandis, wholesalers, institutional buyers, retailers, and digital marketplaces, supported by reliable transport and inventory management.

### Competition & Marketing
Competition involves local cottage and small mills, regional brands, and national players offering a spectrum of quality and price points. The FPO’s differentiation rests on traceable sourcing, farmer-centric pricing, tight process control, food safety compliance, and fresh dispatch cycles. A dual strategy of bulk supplies to institutional buyers and branded packs for retail enhances reach. Marketing priorities include clear labelling, batch-wise traceability, visible quality cues such as uniform grain size and low breakage, and consistent polishing levels matched to regional preferences. Distribution will target kirana stores, mandis, modern trade, e-commerce platforms, and institutional canteens, supported by sampling, chef outreach, and collaborations with self-help groups and community networks. Certifications such as FSSAI and voluntary quality marks will reinforce credibility.

### Risk & Mitigation
Key risks include raw material price swings, production variability due to weather, storage losses, machine downtime, and quality deviations in moisture or polishing. These are mitigated by diversified sourcing from member clusters, season-aligned procurement, buffer stocking within safe storage norms, and robust quality assurance covering moisture testing, calibration of de-huskers, and sieve settings. Preventive maintenance and annual service contracts reduce breakdown risk, while provision for backup power ensures continuity. Sound working capital planning and credit controls protect liquidity. Comprehensive insurance, adherence to food safety standards, and transparent grievance redressal strengthen resilience. Market risks are moderated through long-term supply relationships and a balanced buyer mix across wholesale, retail, and institutional channels.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The project generates direct jobs in operations, maintenance, quality assurance, warehousing, and sales, with additional opportunities for women in grading and packing. It stimulates ancillary services in logistics, materials handling, and packaging. By anchoring processing near farms, the FPO curbs distress sales, reduces wastage, and channels more value to producers. Capacity building in post-harvest handling, quality grading, and enterprise management elevates rural skills. Stable market access and fair procurement norms strengthen household incomes and community resilience.

### Sustainability
Pulses naturally support sustainable agriculture through nitrogen fixation and lower input intensity. The mill’s design promotes resource efficiency through controlled soaking and drying, energy-efficient drives, and the option to integrate renewable power. Water stewardship involves reuse where feasible and responsible discharge. Husk and fines can be valorised as feed, compost, or fuel, minimising waste. Recyclable packaging and responsible sourcing policies reduce environmental footprint. Compliance with food safety, labour, and environmental standards anchors a long-term, climate-resilient business model.
