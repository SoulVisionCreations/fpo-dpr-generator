## 2. Product, Market & Value Chain

### Introduction
Keshkal Dugdh Utpadak FPC Ltd proposes a ghee making unit in Keshkal, Kondagaon, Chhattisgarh. Ghee, or clarified butter, is a signature Indian dairy fat cherished for its aroma, granular texture, and culinary versatility. It is traditionally prepared from milk through cream separation and butter clarification, delivering a stable, lactose-free and gluten-free product with a rich, nutty flavour. The proposed facility leverages abundant local milk availability, short farm-to-factory logistics, and the FPO’s community connect for assured raw material and market linkage.

The processing line follows a hygienic, food-safe sequence: procurement of raw milk from member producers, pasteurization, cooling, homogenization, cream separation, chilling, butter churning, controlled clarification to obtain ghee, and secure packaging. Emphasis is placed on quality control, traceability, and compliance with FSSAI standards across procurement, processing, and distribution.

### Uses & Benefits
- Daily cooking medium with high thermal stability, well-suited for Indian cuisines and deep-frying applications.
- Enhances taste and aroma in home kitchens, restaurants, sweetmeat manufacturing, and snack processing.
- Long ambient shelf-stability when packed well, supporting extended distribution in rural and urban markets.
- Lactose-free and gluten-free attributes appeal to consumers with dietary sensitivities.
- Cultural and religious significance during festivals, offerings, and traditional ceremonies.
- Potential for value-added variants and premium positioning based on purity, source, and process.

### Market Potential
Demand for packaged, standardized ghee continues to expand with rising incomes, brand consciousness, and the shift from loose to packaged formats. Traditional affinity, regional preferences, and festival consumption underpin consistent offtake through the year, with spikes around celebratory seasons. Food service establishments, sweet shops, namkeen manufacturers, and institutional buyers create steady bulk demand, while retail packs drive brand visibility and margins. The FPO’s local brand story, authenticity, and direct farmer connect are natural differentiators against generic offerings.

### Value Chain
- Milk sourcing: Aggregation from member dairy farmers through village-level collection points with basic quality checks and rapid chilling to preserve fat quality.
- Primary processing: Pasteurization and homogenization tailored to ghee production, ensuring microbial safety and consistent cream yield.
- Cream and butter preparation: Efficient separation and controlled churning to obtain clean, fresh butter ready for clarification.
- Ghee clarification: Uniform heating under monitored conditions to deliver desirable colour, aroma, and granular texture, followed by filtration to remove residues.
- Packaging and storage: Food-grade jars, tins, and pouches with tamper-evident sealing; ambient warehousing with pest-proofing and FIFO discipline.
- Distribution and sales: Hybrid network combining local kirana, wholesalers, modern trade, e-commerce, HoReCa, and institutional supply, supported by demand planning and route scheduling.
- By-product utilization: Skimmed milk routed to liquid milk sales or further processing; buttermilk and other streams leveraged for local markets to maximize value recovery.

### Competition & Marketing
The market features cooperative dairies, private dairies, and regional brands. Success hinges on assured purity, flavour consistency, and price-value balance. The FPO can build a trust-led brand narrative anchored in farmer ownership, traceable sourcing, and rigorous testing. Key strategies include:
- Distinctive packaging and labelling focused on purity, origin, and process credentials.
- A thoughtful pack-size portfolio for household, retail, and bulk users.
- In-shop visibility, tasting promotions, and digital storytelling on provenance and quality.
- Institutional tie-ups with sweet shops, caterers, and food processors for stable offtake.
- Certification, food safety mark-ups, and batch-wise lab reports to reinforce credibility.

### Risk & Mitigation
- Raw milk variability: Introduce farmer training, transparent quality-linked pricing, and prompt chilling to stabilize fat and microbiological quality.
- Price volatility: Use procurement protocols, seasonal buffering, and flexible pack pricing to protect margins.
- Food safety and compliance: Implement SOP-driven GMP and HACCP, rigorous QA/QC, and external audits.
- Power and utilities reliability: Provide backup systems and preventive maintenance to curb downtime.
- Market competition: Differentiate on authenticity, freshness, and farmer-owned branding; maintain strong service levels.
- Working capital pressure: Align credit terms, optimize inventory, and use supply chain finance where feasible.
- Fire and hot-oil handling: Enforce safety procedures, staff training, and adequate insurance coverage.

### Employment & Socio-Economic Impact
The unit enables stable market access for member farmers, fair and timely payments, and local value addition. It creates skilled and semi-skilled employment in processing, QA, logistics, and sales, with opportunities for women in packing and lab functions. Extension services, bulk milk cooling, and quality-linked incentives improve farm productivity and household incomes, reinforcing rural livelihoods and community resilience.

### Sustainability
Energy-efficient boilers and heat-recovery options reduce thermal load. Water stewardship through CIP optimization and treated effluent reuse lowers the environmental footprint. Proper handling of residues and productive use of by-products enhance resource efficiency. Preference for recyclable packaging and responsible sourcing policies further strengthens the unit’s sustainability profile.
